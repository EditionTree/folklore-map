Ghosts Slaghtaverty, County Londonderry

Abhartach

Patrick Weston Joyce recorded the legend of Abhartach, a cruel dwarf-magician who repeatedly returned after burial. A neighbouring chieftain finally subdued his power by burying him upside-down beneath a stone monument associated with Slaghtaverty.

A visual interpretation of Abhartach returning from his burial at Slaghtaverty
The legend

Abhartach is a figure from the folklore of County Londonderry whose legend is among the most striking of Ireland's undead traditions. As recorded by the antiquarian Patrick Weston Joyce, Abhartach was a cruel and tyrannical dwarf-chieftain — some say a magician — so hated that a neighbouring chief named Cathán killed and buried him. But the next day Abhartach returned, more terrible than before, demanding a bowl of blood from his subjects' veins to sustain him.

A cruel dwarf-magician who repeatedly returned after burial.

The magician who would not stay buried

Twice more he was slain and twice more he rose, until Cathán sought the counsel of a saint or druid, who revealed that Abhartach was one of the marbh bheo, the 'walking dead', and could not be truly killed — only restrained. He must be slain with a sword of yew, buried upside down, and covered with thorns and a great stone, and so he was, at a townland still called Slaghtaverty, 'the dwarf's grave', where a leaning stone and thorn tree are pointed out. Some have argued that this blood-drinking revenant, fixed in the earth by yew and stone, fed into Bram Stoker's conception of the vampire.

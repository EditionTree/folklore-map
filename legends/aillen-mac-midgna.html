Aillen mac Midgna

AL-yane mak MID-nuh

Each Samhain, Aillen mac Midgna lulled the defenders of Tara to sleep with enchanted music before burning the royal hall. Fionn mac Cumhaill resisted the spell and slew him, earning leadership of the Fianna.

A visual interpretation of Aillen enchanting the defenders of Tara
The Legend

Aillen mac Midgna, sometimes called 'the Burner', is a fire-breathing fairy musician of the Tuatha Dé Danann in Irish legend, remembered above all as the destroyer of Tara. Each year at Samhain, the tradition runs, Aillen would come up from the otherworld dwelling of the sídhe and approach the royal seat of Tara, playing on his timpán a music so sweet and enchanting that every warrior set to defend the place fell helplessly asleep. Then, while the defenders slumbered, he would breathe out fire and burn the great hall of the kings to the ground. For twenty-three years no one could prevent it, for none could stay awake against his lulling music.

Each Samhain, Aillen mac Midgna lulled the defenders of Tara to sleep with enchanted music before burning the royal hall.

The Music Before the Fire

The young Fionn mac Cumhaill broke the curse. Coming to Tara to claim his place, he kept himself awake by pressing the point of his own spear — a poisoned, magical weapon — against his forehead, so that the pain overcame the enchantment of the music. Alert while all others slept, Fionn faced Aillen, withstood his fiery breath beneath a cloak, and ran him down and killed him as he fled back toward the sídhe. By slaying the Burner of Tara, Fionn won the leadership of the Fianna, and the deed became one of the founding exploits of Ireland's greatest band of heroes.

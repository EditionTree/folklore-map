Witches Stenness, Orkney, Scotland

Allison Balfour

Tortured for two days with her husband and children, Allison Balfour 'confessed' to plotting against the Earl of Orkney by witchcraft, then retracted it before being strangled and burned in 1594.

The Legend

In 1594, Allison Balfour of Stenness was caught up in a political conspiracy. Associates of Patrick Stewart, the powerful 2nd Earl of Orkney, accused her of agreeing to use witchcraft against him on their behalf. The evidence against her amounted to little more than a misshapen lump of wax, presented as proof of contact with infernal forces. To extract a confession, the authorities subjected her to forty-eight hours of torture in the caschielawis, a leg-crushing iron frame, while also turning on her family: her 81-year-old husband was placed in the 'lang irons', her son was tortured in the boots, and her seven-year-old daughter had her fingers crushed in pilliwinks (thumbscrews) before her eyes.

On the scaffold, before her execution, she publicly retracted everything, declaring the confession false and extracted only by torture.

The Confession Retracted

Broken by the ordeal, Balfour confessed, later saying she did so 'partly to eschew a greater torment' and on a promise that her life would be spared. On the scaffold, before her execution by strangulation and burning on 16 December 1594, she publicly retracted everything, declaring the confession false and extracted only by torture.

Her case is one of the earliest and most thoroughly documented of Orkney's witch trials, cited repeatedly by historians such as Julian Goodare as a textbook example of how torture, politics and the witch-hunting frenzy combined to destroy an entire family. It was also vindicated in a small way two years later, when another of the accused conspirators was tried in Edinburgh and the evidence drawn from Balfour's confession was thrown out as unreliable, obtained as it had been under torture. The Orkney Heritage Society's witchcraft trial memorial in Kirkwall now commemorates her alongside Orkney's other victims.

Anantis Castle Revenant

William of Newburgh's most dreadful revenant: a wicked man of 'Anantis castle' (often identified with Alnwick) rose blood-bloated to spread a deadly pestilence, until two brothers dug him up and burned his heart.

The Legend

The most elaborate of William of Newburgh's revenant tales concerns a thoroughly wicked man who had settled near the 'castle of Anantis', a place long debated by scholars, but often identified with Alnwick in Northumberland. Spying jealously on his unfaithful wife, he fell from a beam, died unconfessed, and was buried; thereafter his corpse roamed abroad by night, and wherever it passed the air grew foul and a wasting sickness spread, until whole families were dying and the town was emptying.

Thereafter his corpse roamed abroad by night, and wherever it passed the air grew foul and a wasting sickness spread.

The Corpse That Carried Pestilence

On Palm Sunday two brothers whose own father had died of the plague resolved to end it. They dug up the body and found it gorged and swollen with the blood it had drained from the living; striking it open, they tore out the heart and burned the whole corpse to ashes, after which the pestilence ceased. William of Newburgh, writing around 1198, presents the Anantis case as the worst of several such walking-corpse stories he collected from his own era, alongside the Berwick Revenant and the Buckinghamshire Revenant recorded elsewhere on this site, all drawn from the same chapter of his Historia rerum Anglicarum; taken together they make his account one of the most sustained bodies of English revenant testimony to survive from the twelfth century. The blood-bloated, plague-bringing corpse destroyed by burning is the closest the medieval English record comes to the later folklore of the vampire, and among the earliest such accounts in Europe.

Andraste

A warrior goddess invoked by Boudica before her revolt against Rome. Her name survives through Roman accounts, fierce and blood-bright at the edge of history and myth.

A visual interpretation of Andraste invoked before Boudicca's revolt
The Legend

Andraste is a warrior-goddess of the ancient Britons, known from a single dramatic moment in the historical record: the great revolt of Boudica against Rome in AD 60–61. The Roman historian Cassius Dio relates that Boudica, queen of the Iceni of East Anglia, invoked Andraste before leading her people to war, releasing a hare from the folds of her dress as a means of divination and calling on the goddess for victory. The name is usually understood to mean something like 'the invincible' or 'she who is unconquered'.

A warrior goddess invoked by Boudica before her revolt against Rome.

The Goddess at the Edge of History

Dio's account makes Andraste a goddess of war, slaughter and victory, and links her cult to a sacred grove in which the Britons made terrible offerings of their Roman captives. Whether Andraste was a major deity of the Iceni or a more localised or epithet-form of a goddess is impossible to know from so slender a source; she may be connected with the Gaulish Andarta, a similarly named goddess honoured on the Continent.

Because she survives only through the pen of a hostile Roman writing more than a century after the events, Andraste hovers between history and myth — a fierce British goddess glimpsed at the very edge of the documentary record, forever bound to the doomed but devastating rising of Boudica. She remains a potent emblem of native British religion and resistance, a divine patroness of a people fighting for their freedom against the Empire.

Anne Bonny

Anne Bonny became one of the best-known pirates of the Golden Age. Her birthplace is uncertain, but the enduring traditional account places her near Cork. Sparse historical records left room for a much larger legend, retold through books, songs, theatre and screen.

The Legend

Anne Bonny was one of the most famous women of the Golden Age of Piracy, and tradition holds that she was born near Kinsale in County Cork, the illegitimate daughter of a lawyer who took her to the Carolinas. Restless and fierce, she abandoned a poor husband in the pirate haven of New Providence and ran off with the flamboyant captain 'Calico Jack' Rackham, sailing openly as a member of his crew.

Sparse historical records left room for a much larger legend.

The Pirate Beyond the Record

Aboard Rackham's sloop she fought alongside Mary Read, the two women winning a reputation for courage that outshone the men. When the King's forces took the ship off Jamaica in 1720, it was said that Bonny and Read were the only two who stood and fought while the rest of the crew lay drunk below. Both escaped hanging by pleading pregnancy; Read died in prison, but Bonny's ultimate fate is unknown. She simply vanishes from the record. Of the captured Rackham she is said to have remarked that had he fought like a man, he need not have hanged like a dog.

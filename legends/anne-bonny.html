Anne Bonny

Anne Bonny fled a poor marriage for the pirate Calico Jack Rackham and fought alongside Mary Read when their ship was taken off Jamaica in 1720. Both escaped hanging by pleading pregnancy; Read died in prison, but Bonny's fate afterward is unknown.

The Legend

Anne Bonny was one of the most famous women of the Golden Age of piracy. Tradition holds she was born around 1698 near Cork, the illegitimate daughter of a lawyer and his maid, and that when the affair became public the family emigrated in disgrace to the Carolinas. She married a poor sailor, James Bonny, was disowned by her father for it, and fled with him to the pirate haven of New Providence in the Bahamas, only to abandon that marriage too for the flamboyant captain 'Calico Jack' Rackham, sailing openly as a member of his crew.

Sparse historical records left room for a much larger legend.

The Pirate Beyond the Record

Aboard Rackham's stolen sloop William she fought alongside Mary Read, an Englishwoman raised as a boy from childhood who had already served on a warship and in the army in Flanders before turning to piracy herself. In August 1720, the pirate hunter Captain Jonathan Barnet caught the ship off Negril, Jamaica, on the governor's orders. By the account later published by Captain Charles Johnson, Bonny, Read and one other were the only crew members who stayed on deck to fight, calling down to the men drinking below to come up and fight like men and firing pistols into the hold among them regardless.

Both women escaped hanging by pleading pregnancy at their trial in Spanish Town that November. Mary Read died of fever in prison the following April; Anne Bonny's fate afterward is far less certain. One later account has her returning to South Carolina and living out her life there, dying in 1782, but the documentary trail simply goes cold. Of the captured Rackham, condemned to hang at Port Royal, she is said to have remarked that had he fought like a man, he need not have hanged like a dog.

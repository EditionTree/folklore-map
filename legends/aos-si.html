Aos Sí

EES SHEE

The people of the mounds — an ancient supernatural race who retreated into the hollow hills when the Gaels conquered Ireland. They inhabit the fairy forts and ring barrows of the landscape. To disturb their homes brings ruin. To be taken by them is to never fully return.

The Legend

The Aos Sí — the 'people of the mounds', older spelling aes sídhe — are the supernatural race at the heart of Irish and Scottish Gaelic belief, understood as the diminished successors of the Tuatha Dé Danann. When the Gaels took Ireland, tradition holds, the defeated Tuatha Dé withdrew not overseas but into the otherworld beneath the land, taking up their dwelling in the ancient burial mounds, the sídhe, and in the hollow hills, lakes and raths.

To disturb their homes brings ruin. To be taken by them is to never fully return.

The People Beneath the Hills

From there they remained a constant unseen presence, the 'Good People' or 'fair folk' whom it was wiser to praise than to name. They could bless a household with luck or blight it with misfortune, lead travellers astray, steal away brides, infants and the newly dead, and demanded that their forts, thorn-trees and paths be left undisturbed. Offerings of milk and bread were set out, and great festivals such as Samhain and Bealtaine marked the times when the veil between their world and the mortal one grew thin. From the Aos Sí descend the banshee, the leprechaun and the host of individual fairy beings of Gaelic tradition.

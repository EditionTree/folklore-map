Arawn

King of Annwn, the Welsh Otherworld, who exchanges places with Pwyll in the First Branch of the Mabinogi. He is not a devil, but a ruler of another order of being.

A visual interpretation of Arawn as the regal king of Annwn
The legend

Arawn is the king of Annwn, the Otherworld of Welsh myth, and one of the central figures of the First Branch of the Mabinogi. Annwn is no Christian hell but a realm of its own order — a land of feasting, beauty and plenty, ruled by its own kings and subject to its own rivalries. Arawn first appears as a grey-clad huntsman in the forest, where Pwyll, prince of Dyfed, gives offence by driving Arawn's hounds from a stag they have brought down and setting his own pack on it instead. To make amends, Arawn proposes an extraordinary bargain.

He is not a devil, but a ruler of another order of being.

The king beyond the mortal world

The two men exchange shapes and kingdoms for a year. Pwyll, in Arawn's form, goes to rule Annwn and to face Arawn's great enemy, the rival king Hafgan, whom Arawn can never finally defeat; Arawn, in Pwyll's shape, rules Dyfed. At the year's end Pwyll meets Hafgan in single combat and kills him with a single stroke — having been warned that a second blow would only revive his foe — and so wins Annwn for Arawn. Throughout the year Pwyll sleeps each night chastely beside Arawn's beautiful queen, never touching her, an honourable restraint that wins him Arawn's lasting friendship and gratitude.

From this bargain Pwyll earns his title Pen Annwn, 'Head of Annwn', and a deep alliance is forged between the two realms, sealed with gifts of horses, hounds and treasure. Arawn is significant precisely because he is not a devil or a monster but a courteous, honourable ruler of another order of being — an image of the Welsh Otherworld as a mirror-kingdom alongside the mortal world, with which mortals might deal as equals.

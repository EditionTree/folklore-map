Balor of the Evil Eye

The terrible king of the Fomorians, whose single great eye — kept shut by a ring — would kill all it looked upon when opened. It took four men to lift his eyelid in battle. His own grandson Lugh slew him with a slingshot through the eye, fulfilling a prophecy Balor had tried all his life to prevent.

A visual interpretation of Balor's deadly eye being opened for battle
The Legend

Balor of the Evil Eye is the dread king of the Fomorians, the monstrous race of Irish myth, and dwelt by tradition on Tory Island off the Donegal coast. His single great eye, set in the middle or back of his head, had grown venomous from the fumes of a druid's brew; kept shut beneath a vast lid that four men hauled up with a ring and a hook, it withered and slew armies with a single baleful glance when opened on the battlefield.

It took four men to lift his eyelid in battle.

The Eye That Killed All It Saw

A prophecy warned that Balor would be killed by his own grandson, so he imprisoned his daughter Eithne in a tower to keep her childless. The hero Cian reached her nonetheless, and she bore Lugh of the Long Arm. At the second Battle of Mag Tuired, as Balor's deadly eye was being levered open, Lugh struck it through with a sling-stone — in some tellings driving the eye out the back of his head so that its death-gaze fell upon the Fomorian host itself. With Balor's fall the Tuatha Dé Danann broke the Fomorian power over Ireland.

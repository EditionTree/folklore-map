Balor of the Evil Eye

The terrible king of the Fomorians, whose single great eye, kept shut by a ring, would kill all it looked upon when opened. It took four men to lift his eyelid in battle. His own grandson Lugh slew him with a slingshot through the eye, fulfilling a prophecy Balor had tried all his life to prevent.

The Legend

Balor of the Evil Eye ruled the Fomorians, the monstrous race of Irish myth, dwelling by tradition on Tory Island off the Donegal coast. His single great eye, set in the middle or back of his head, turned venomous when, as a young man, he looked in on his father's druids brewing a magic concoction and the fumes settled into it. Ever after it was kept shut beneath a vast lid that four men hauled open with a polished ring, and once raised it withered and slew whole armies with a single glance, so that a host many thousands strong could offer no resistance against it.

It took four men to lift his eyelid in battle.

The Eye That Killed All It Saw

A prophecy warned that Balor would be killed by his own grandson, so he imprisoned his daughter Eithne in a tower to keep her childless. The hero Cian reached her nonetheless, and she bore Lugh of the Long Arm. At the Second Battle of Mag Tuired, Balor asked that his eyelid be raised so he could look upon his opponent, and as it opened Lugh struck it through with a sling stone, driving the eye out through the back of his head so that its own destructive gaze fell on the Fomorian ranks behind him. Balor toppled onto his own army, crushing twenty-seven of his men beneath him, and the shock of the impact was said to burst blood from the lips of the Fomorian champion Indech mac De Domnann. With Balor's fall the Tuatha De Danann broke the Fomorian power over Ireland for good.

Bampfylde Moore Carew

Bampfylde Moore Carew was a Devon-born rogue whose embellished life story made him the self-styled King of the Beggars and Gypsies. Chapbook accounts turned his disguises, escapes, and confidence tricks into a durable popular legend, blending historical fact with folk invention across 18th-century England.

The Legend

Bampfylde Moore Carew (1693–1759) was a real Devon man, son of a Bickleigh rector, whose embroidered life became one of the most popular chapbooks of the eighteenth century. Running away from school to join a band of Gypsies, he made a career of vagrancy and ingenious imposture, and was eventually hailed, in his own and his publishers' telling, as the 'King of the Beggars'.

the self-styled King of the Beggars and Gypsies

The Rogue King on the Road

The legend delights in his disguises: he passed himself off as a shipwrecked sailor, a ruined Quaker, a madman, a rat-catcher, even as several different drowned men to wring charity from the same gentry. Transported to the American colonies, he is said to have escaped, lived among Native Americans, and made his way home. How much is fact and how much chapbook invention is impossible to untangle. Carew survives as a folk-hero of the open road, the trickster who lived by his wits and turned his own life into a tall tale.

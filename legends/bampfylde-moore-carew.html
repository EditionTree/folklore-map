Bampfylde Moore Carew

Bampfylde Moore Carew was a Devon-born rogue whose embellished life of disguises and confidence tricks turned him into the self-styled King of the Beggars.

The Legend

Bampfylde Moore Carew (1693–1759) was a real Devon man, son of a Bickleigh rector, whose embroidered life became one of the most popular chapbooks of the eighteenth century. Running away from school to join a band of Gypsies, he made a career of vagrancy and ingenious imposture, and was eventually hailed, in his own and his publishers' telling, as the 'King of the Beggars'.

the self-styled King of the Beggars and Gypsies

The Rogue King on the Road

The legend delights in his disguises: he passed himself off as a shipwrecked sailor, a ruined Quaker, a madman, a rat-catcher, and more than once as a destitute widow in mourning weeds with a borrowed baby on her hip to work on the sympathy of the gentry, even as several different drowned men to wring charity from the same households twice over. Transported to the American colonies, he is said to have escaped, lived among Native Americans, and made his way home. How much is fact and how much chapbook invention is impossible to untangle.

The Life and Adventures of Bampfylde Moore Carew was first published in 1745 and stayed in print for the best part of a century, reissued again and again as both a full biography and a cheap chapbook, and resurfacing in the Victorian era as a thirty-eight part penny dreadful. Carew survives as a folk-hero of the open road, the trickster who lived by his wits and turned his own life into a tall tale that outlasted him by two centuries.

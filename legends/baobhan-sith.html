Baobhan Sith

BAA-van SHEE

Highland vampiric spirits — beautiful women in green gowns who appear to hunters in the wilderness, dance with them through the night, and drain their blood. Iron is the only protection; one man survived by sheltering among his horses.

The Legend

The Baobhan Sith is among the most dangerous spirits of the Scottish Highlands, a beautiful and deadly female fairy whose name translates roughly as "woman of the sidhe" and whose habits have long earned her comparison to a vampire. In the version recorded by the folklorist Donald A. Mackenzie, a party of hunters shelters for the night in a lonely shieling and wishes aloud for women to dance with. Four beautiful women in long green gowns duly appear and join the dance, but as the night wears on the dancers turn predatory, fastening on the men to drain their blood, and by morning the hunters are found dead and bloodless.

The Dancers in Green

The one survivor, in the usual telling, is the man who slipped outside to tend the horses, for the Baobhan Sith cannot abide cold iron. It was the iron of the horseshoes and harness, or his own dirk, that kept his pursuer at bay until cock-crow drove her off. Her long green dress hides the deer's hooves she has in place of feet, betraying her otherworldly nature.

Folklorists disagree on exactly how to classify her: some group her with vampires, others closer to the seductive fairy women found across Gaelic tradition, and more than one telling gives her the same fondness for green worn by other otherworldly women of Highland legend. The shielings and lonely bothies where hunters once bedded down for the night, the exact kind of shelter at the centre of her stories, still dot the Highland glens where the tale is told.

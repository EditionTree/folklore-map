Baobhan Sith

BAA-van SHEE

Highland vampiric spirits — beautiful women in green gowns who appear to hunters in the wilderness, dance with them through the night, and drain their blood. Iron is the only protection; one man survived by sheltering among his horses.

A visual interpretation of the Baobhan Sith dancing in the Highland wilderness
The Legend

The Baobhan Sith is among the most dangerous spirits of the Scottish Highlands, a beautiful and deadly female fairy often likened to a vampire. The classic tale tells of a party of hunters who shelter for the night in a lonely bothy, and wish aloud for women to dance with. Soon four beautiful women in long green gowns appear and join them in the dance — but as the night wears on the dancers turn predatory, fastening on the men to drain their blood, and by morning the hunters are found dead and bloodless.

The Dancers in Green

The one survivor, in the usual telling, is the man who slipped outside to tend the horses, for the Baobhan Sith cannot abide cold iron — it was the iron of the horseshoes and harness, or his own dirk, that kept his pursuer at bay until cock-crow drove her off. Her long green dress hides the deer's hooves she has in place of feet, betraying her otherworldly nature. A creature of the wild glens and the hunters' camp, the Baobhan Sith embodies the peril of the beautiful stranger met in lonely places after dark.

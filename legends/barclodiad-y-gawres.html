Barclodiad y Gawres

bark-LOD-yad uh GOW-ress

Barclodiad y Gawres, the Giantess's Apronful, is a Neolithic passage tomb on the coast of Anglesey. Legend says a giantess dropped the stones from her apron after learning how far she still had to travel. The decorated chamber stones and the site itself are protected by Cadw.

A visual interpretation of the giantess whose apronful became Barclodiad y Gawres
The Legend

Barclodiad y Gawres — 'the Giantess's Apronful' — is a fine Neolithic passage tomb on the cliffs of Anglesey, famous for the spiral and zigzag carvings on its stones, and its name preserves a folk explanation for how such a great mound of stones came to be. A giantess, the story runs, was carrying an apronful of stones across the island when the strings of her apron broke, spilling the load — and the scattered heap became the cairn.

The Giantess's Apronful

The motif is widespread across Britain and Ireland, where many a chambered cairn, standing stone or boulder field is credited to a giant or giantess who dropped, threw or built it. Such tales reflect the way later generations, finding monuments far older than memory, reached for the only builders vast enough to have raised them. At Barclodiad y Gawres the apron-spill legend gave a homely, human-scaled story to one of Wales's most important prehistoric tombs.

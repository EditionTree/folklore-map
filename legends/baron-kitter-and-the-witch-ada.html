Baron Kitter and the Witch Ada

A Norwegian tyrant hunted the Isle of Man's wildlife to extinction until the witch Ada burned his castle behind his back. His drowned crew gave their name to the islet of Kitterland forever after.

The Legend

Manx tradition holds that, generations before recorded history, a Norwegian baron named Kitter ruled the Isle of Man from a castle on the mountain of Barrule. Kitter was an obsessive hunter, and in time he wiped out the island's native bison and elk entirely. The Manx people, fearing he would turn next to their livestock and to the wild creatures of the hills, went to the wisest of the island's witches for help.

The Story

When Kitter crossed to the Calf of Man to hunt red deer, he left his castle in the care of his cook, Eaoch. While Eaoch slept, the witch Ada worked a spell on the cooking pot left over the fire, so that the fat boiled over and set the castle alight. Eaoch's cries carried across the water to Kitter on the Calf, and he and his companions launched a small currach to race back and save it. Partway across the sound, the currach struck a rock in the treacherous currents and everyone aboard was drowned. The rock has been called Kitterland ever since.

The story was collected by Sophia Morrison in her 1911 Manx Fairy Tales, itself drawing on folklore already circulating in earlier nineteenth-century collections of Manx tradition. It remains attached to a real, named place. Kitterland is a small islet in the Calf Sound between the Isle of Man and the Calf of Man, now in the care of the Manx National Trust and known today chiefly as a haven for seabirds rather than the site of a drowned baron's ambition.

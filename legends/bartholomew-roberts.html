Bartholomew Roberts

The Welsh pirate Barti Ddu, later called Black Bart, captured more than four hundred vessels during the Golden Age of Piracy. His birthplace at Little Newcastle still bears a memorial stone.

A visual interpretation of Bartholomew Roberts and the maritime legend
The Legend

Bartholomew Roberts — 'Black Bart' — was born John Roberts around 1682 at Casnewydd-Bach (Little Newcastle) in Pembrokeshire, and rose to become the most successful pirate of the entire Golden Age of Piracy, taking over 400 ships in a career of barely three years. He came to piracy late and by accident: serving as second mate on a slave ship, he was captured off the West African coast in 1719 by the Welsh pirate Howell Davis. When Davis was killed in an ambush on the island of Príncipe only six weeks later, the crew, impressed by the newcomer's skill and boldness, elected Roberts their captain — and he reportedly accepted with the dark remark that if he was to be a pirate, it was better to be a commander than a common man.

The Figure Behind the Legend

He proved a natural. Tall, dark and famously well-dressed — going into battle in crimson damask, a feathered hat and a gold chain — Roberts ranged from the coast of Brazil to Newfoundland and back to West Africa, capturing prizes by the hundred and flying his own black flags, including one showing himself standing upon two skulls labelled for the governors of Barbados and Martinique he so hated. Unusually for a pirate he was said to be a teetotaller who drank only tea, and he drew up a celebrated pirate code governing his crew.

His end came in February 1722 off Cape Lopez in West Africa, when the Royal Navy warship HMS Swallow caught his fleet. Roberts was killed early in the fight by a blast of grapeshot to the throat, and his men, honouring his wish, weighted his body and cast it into the sea before surrendering — so that the greatest pirate of the age was never taken. His death is often reckoned to mark the end of the Golden Age of Piracy itself. Little Newcastle remembers its son with a memorial in the village.

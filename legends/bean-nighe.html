Bean Nighe

pen NYEE-uh

The washerwoman at the ford — a small, webbed-footed spirit found scrubbing blood-stained shrouds in running water. To see her is to know that death is coming. If you can sneak behind her and take her breast, she must grant you a wish before she will let you go.

The Legend

The Bean Nighe — the 'washerwoman' — is one of the most chilling death-omens of Scottish Highland and Hebridean tradition, a fairy woman encountered at a lonely ford or stream, washing the blood-stained grave-clothes of those about to die. Small and often described with webbed feet, a single nostril or pendulous breasts, she is a form of the banshee, and to come upon her was to receive a warning of mortality — sometimes one's own.

To see her is to know that death is coming.

The Washer at the Ford

Highland lore held that the Bean Nighe could be compelled to grant wishes or to name the doomed: a bold person who crept up and seized her, or who suckled at her breast and claimed kinship, could force her to answer three questions and to spare those whose shrouds she washed. But to be seen by her first was perilous. Believed by some to be the ghost of a woman who died in childbirth, doomed to wash until the day she would naturally have died, the Bean Nighe haunts the threshold between this world and the next at the running water that so often marks that boundary in Celtic belief.

Bedd Dorti, the Witch's Grave

A 17th-century woman feared as a witch was hurled in a cask from the cliffs above Llyn Tecwyn Uchaf, and the cairn of stones marking where she landed, Bedd Dorti, is said to bring death within a year to anyone who passes without adding a stone to the pile.

The Legend

On the high ground above Llyn Tecwyn Uchaf, on the old road between Llandecwyn and Maentwrog in Meirionnydd, stands a stone-covered mound known as Bedd Dorti, Dorti's Grave, or in fuller Welsh tradition Dorti'r Wrach, Dorti the Witch. Local tradition holds that Dorti was a seventeenth-century woman widely feared as a witch, always seen with a black cat on her shoulder, who was seized by her neighbours, sealed inside a cask and hurled from the rocks above the lake rather than tried under the law. The mound was said to mark the spot where the cask came to rest.

Local tradition holds that anyone who passes Bedd Dorti without adding a stone risks death within a year.

The Cairn Above the Lake

The site carries its own surviving superstition: travellers passing the mound are expected to add a white stone to the heap, on pain of dying within the year if they fail to do so. The antiquarian W. J. Hemp, recording the tradition for the Royal Commission on the Ancient and Historical Monuments of Wales, was told in 1943 that the mound had already been disturbed once, moved around 1920 during the construction of a dam at the foot of the lake.

The story took one more turn decades later. When Welsh Water cut into the mound during works around the turn of the twenty-first century, it proved to be not an artificial cairn at all but a natural ridge of rock, no burial and no witch beneath the stones, only the story the white stones themselves had kept alive. Dorti's grave survives today as one of Meirionnydd's best-known pieces of witch-lore regardless of what the ground turned out to hold.

Sacred Sites Coachford, County Cork

Beenalaght

Six tall stones on a Cork hillside known in Irish as An Seisear — ‘the Six’ — commemorating, by local legend, six chieftains buried here in ancient times.

The Legend

Beenalaght stands on a flat pasture in the townland of Reanthesure, half a kilometre west of the village of Bweeng in County Cork. The alignment of six standing stones — five still upright, ranging from 1.8 to 3 metres in height, with a sixth now prostrate — is known locally as An Seisear, the Irish word for a group of six people. The place-name itself, Binn na Leacht, translates as ‘monument hill,’ marking the site as a place of memorial since the language first took root here.

The Story

Local legend holds that the stones commemorate six chieftains who were buried at Beenalaght in ancient times. The use of ‘seisear’ — which refers specifically to six people, not six objects — suggests the folklore is not merely an explanation for why six stones happen to stand in a row, but carries a genuine tradition of the site as a burial ground for persons of rank. Whether or not actual chieftains lie beneath, the tradition speaks to how communities across centuries understood and explained the monuments they inherited.

The alignment sits within a rich archaeological landscape: megalithic sites lie within a few kilometres in several directions, and the broader Mallow-Coachford corridor is scattered with standing stones and circles dating to the Bronze Age. Beenalaght’s stones, tall and closely spaced, are among the most visually striking in the region.

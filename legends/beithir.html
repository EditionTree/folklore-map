Beithir

BAY-hir

The Beithir is a venomous serpent-dragon of Scottish Gaelic folklore. An Islay tradition tells of a beast travelling from Scanlastle toward Loch Indaal, where sailors killed it using flesh-covered barrels armed with iron spikes.

The Legend

The Beithir is the great serpent-dragon of Scottish Gaelic folklore, a vast and venomous wingless wyrm of the Highlands and Islands, dwelling in mountain corries, caves and lochs. Lacking the wings and fire of the heraldic dragon, the Beithir was imagined rather as a monstrous serpent of enormous size, its sting deadly; tradition held that if a snake were killed, its head must be destroyed and carried well away from the body, for otherwise the head might grow into a Beithir.

The Serpent on the Shore

An Islay tradition tells of a Beithir that travelled overland from Scanlastle toward Loch Indaal, tearing up the ground and leaving a trail of destruction as it went. Sailors on the loch are said to have destroyed it by lashing a line of empty barrels into a bridge, driving iron spikes through the wood, and baiting them with roasted meat; when the starving beast came down for the smell of the flesh, the spiked barrels tore it apart as it tried to cross. The Islay tale is specifically recorded in John Gregorson Campbell's 'Superstitions of the Highlands and Islands of Scotland' (1900) and 'Witchcraft and Second Sight in the Highlands and Islands of Scotland' (1902). Like the lake-monsters and water-horses of the same country, the Beithir belongs to the Highland sense that the wild places, the high corrie, the deep loch, the lonely glen, harboured creatures of great and dangerous power, the native dragons of the Gaelic world.

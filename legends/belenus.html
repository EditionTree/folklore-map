Belenus

A Celtic healing and solar god whose cult spread widely across the ancient Celtic world. Later antiquarians often connected him with bright fire, renewal, and sacred springs.

The Legend

Belenus — also written Belenos or Bel — was one of the most widely worshipped of the ancient Celtic gods, a deity of light, healing and the life-giving power of the sun whose cult is attested by inscriptions across a broad sweep of the Celtic world, from northern Italy and Gaul to Britain. His name is generally taken to mean 'the shining one' or 'bright', marking him as a solar or radiant god, and he was sometimes identified by the Romans with Apollo in his aspect as a healer and bringer of light.

The Divine Presence

Belenus was particularly associated with healing springs and with the restoration of health, and his sanctuaries, like those of Apollo, seem to have drawn the sick seeking cure. Later antiquarian and folk tradition connected his name with the great Celtic fire-festival of Beltane, kept at the start of May, when bonfires were lit to welcome the summer half of the year and cattle were driven between the flames for protection — the element 'Bel-' in Beltane being popularly linked to the bright god, though scholars debate the true connection.

Whether or not Beltane truly bears his name, Belenus was one of the major deities of the pre-Roman Celtic pantheon: a god of the sun's renewing warmth, of healing waters, and of the turning of the year toward light and growth. In Britain, native Celtic deities like Belenus were honoured at springs and high places, worship later overlaid first by Roman religion, then by Christian devotion.

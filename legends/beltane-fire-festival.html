Beltane Fire Festival

A modern fire festival on Calton Hill inspired by ancient Beltane seasonal rites. It turns the coming of summer into theatre, flame, drums, and red-painted myth.

The Legend

The Beltane Fire Festival is a spectacular modern celebration held each year on the night of the 30th of April on Calton Hill in the heart of Edinburgh, a torch-lit pageant of drumming, dance, fire and elaborate body-painted performers that draws thousands of spectators to watch the coming of summer enacted in flame against the night sky. Revived in 1988, it is a deliberate modern re-imagining of one of the four great festivals of the ancient Celtic year.

Though a contemporary creation rather than an unbroken survival, it consciously revives the imagery and meaning of the old Beltane.

Fire on Calton Hill

Beltane itself, kept at the start of May, marked the beginning of the bright summer half of the year in the old Gaelic calendar, the counterpart to Samhain at summer's end. Its central rite was fire: great bonfires were kindled on the hills, and cattle were driven between two fires to purify and protect them as they went out to the summer pastures, while people leapt the flames and doused their hearths to relight them from the sacred fire. It was a threshold time, when the boundary with the Otherworld grew thin and the fairies were abroad.

The modern Edinburgh festival weaves these ancient themes into a great piece of street theatre and ritual drama, structured around the procession of the May Queen and the Green Man and his death and rebirth, the triumph of summer over winter, played out by fire-performers and drummers across the hill. Though a contemporary creation rather than an unbroken survival, it consciously revives the imagery and meaning of the old Beltane, turning the ancient fire-festival of the Celtic May into living, blazing spectacle in the modern city.

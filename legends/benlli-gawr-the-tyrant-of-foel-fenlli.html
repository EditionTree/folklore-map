Benlli Gawr, the Tyrant of Foel Fenlli

A tyrant giant-king of Powys who turned away a wandering saint was consumed, with his entire hillfort, by fire that fell from heaven that same night.

The Legend

Benlli Gawr ('Benlli the Giant') appears in the earliest layer of Welsh legendary history as a cruel and tyrannical king ruling part of Powys in the early fifth century, based at the hillfort now known as Foel Fenlli in the Clwydian Range of Denbighshire, which still bears his name. His story is preserved in the ninth-century Historia Brittonum, traditionally attributed to the Welsh monk Nennius, making it one of the oldest recorded Welsh giant-legends rather than a later folk invention.

The Story

According to the Historia, Saint Germanus of Auxerre travelled to Benlli's court seeking hospitality and was refused entry, left to wait outside as night fell. Only one of Benlli's household, a servant named Cadell, took pity on the saint and secretly brought him food and shelter; Germanus, recognising Cadell's piety, warned him to leave the tyrant's fortress before it was too late. That same night, fire fell from heaven and consumed Benlli's fortress along with the king and his entire household — 'so that not one escaped', in the words of the Historia — while Cadell, spared for his kindness to the saint, was said to go on to found a new royal line in Powys.

The tale functions as an origin myth explaining both the fiery destruction implied by the hillfort's dramatic hilltop position and the legitimacy of later Powys kings claiming descent from the pious Cadell rather than the destroyed tyrant. Foel Fenlli's Iron Age hillfort remains, later reoccupied in the early medieval period, still crown the hill today, giving the legend an unusually direct and visitable physical setting.

Bisterne Dragon

A New Forest dragon said to have terrorised Bisterne from its lair near Burley. Sir Maurice de Berkeley slew it after a brutal fight; the body became Bolton's Bench, and the knight returned there to die beneath a yew.

A visual interpretation of the Bisterne Dragon on the edge of the New Forest
The legend

The Bisterne Dragon haunted the New Forest in Hampshire, lairing — tradition says — on Burley Beacon and descending each day upon the hamlet of Bisterne, where the terrified people set out a daily pail of milk to appease it and keep it from carrying off their cattle and themselves. At length a knight, named in the legend as Sir Maurice de Berkeley, undertook to rid the country of the beast.

the terrified people set out a daily pail of milk to appease it

The dragon on the forest edge

He had himself coated in birdlime and powdered glass, or in some tellings simply armoured and accompanied by two great mastiff dogs, and waited for the dragon at its feeding-place. In the savage fight that followed his dogs were killed but the dragon was slain — yet Sir Maurice did not long enjoy his victory: worn down by the struggle and, the story says, by the venom or the horror of it, he fell into a decline and died soon after. The Berkeley family long kept a dragon in their arms and crest in memory of the deed, and a hill near Bisterne is still called Dragon Field, fixing the New Forest's dragon to its place.

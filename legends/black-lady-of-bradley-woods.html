Black Lady of Bradley Woods

A pale, tear-stained woman in a flowing black cloak wanders Bradley Woods near Grimsby, searching for the baby soldiers tore from her in the Wars of the Roses. Call for her lost child on Christmas Eve, tradition says, and she appears.

The Legend

The Black Lady of Bradley Woods is a sorrowful ghost said to haunt the woods near the village of Bradley, on the edge of Grimsby in Lincolnshire. Witnesses over the years have described her in consistent terms: a young and pretty woman, dressed in a flowing black cloak and hood that hides her hair but reveals a pale, mournful, tear-stained face. She is held to be entirely harmless, a pitiful rather than a frightening apparition, appearing among the trees and then fading away, forever grieving.

The Black Lady of Bradley Woods is a ghost which reportedly haunts the woods near the village of Bradley, Lincolnshire, England

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

The legend that explains her dates the tragedy to the Wars of the Roses in the 15th century. A young woodsman, it is said, was pressed into military service and went off to war, leaving his wife and infant child behind in their cottage in the woods; he never returned. In the cruellest version of the tale, the lonely young woman was set upon by enemy soldiers who assaulted her and carried off her baby, and ever afterward she wandered the woods searching for her lost child, a search her spirit has continued down the centuries. An alternative tradition, prompted by her dark, habit-like clothing, makes her the ghost of a nun from the vanished convent at nearby Nunsthorpe. No written record of the tale has been traced earlier than 20th-century compilations of Lincolnshire hauntings, so while the story is set during the Wars of the Roses, its oral currency before that point cannot be independently confirmed.

Like many such hauntings, the Black Lady long served as a bogey to frighten children into good behaviour, with parents warning that the Black Lady would come for those not safely in bed. Local tradition even holds that she can be summoned: that walking around a particular spot in the woods, or calling for her child, on Christmas Eve will make her appear. She remains one of Lincolnshire's best-loved ghost legends, a figure of grief and lost motherhood haunting the Bradley woods.

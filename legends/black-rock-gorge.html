Black Rock Gorge

A deep Highland gorge where the Lady of Balconie was lured to her death by a mysterious man in green, believed to be the Devil, and her cries still echo from the depths.

The Legend

Black Rock Gorge cuts through ancient Old Red Sandstone near Evanton in Easter Ross, its sheer walls plunging into shadow where the Allt Graad tumbles below. The gorge's most enduring legend tells of the Lady of Balconie, a noblewoman from nearby Balconie Castle who encountered a dark-looking man dressed in green while walking in the surrounding woods. Charmed by his manner, she followed him deeper into the forest until he led her to the gorge's edge and over it to her death. Local tradition holds that the man was the Devil in disguise, and that the Lady's anguished cries can still be heard rising from the depths.

The Lady of Balconie is said to have been led to the gorge by a dark man in green.

The Cry from the Gorge

The tale does not end with her fall. When she plunged into the gorge, the Lady is said to have dropped her house keys, leaving a permanent impression in the rock face that visitors claim to see to this day. A later strand of the legend tells of a fisherman who followed mysterious tracks into a cave within the gorge, where he discovered the Grey Lady baking cakes for the Devil himself. When the Devil's hounds caught the fisherman's scent, the Grey Lady threw her cakes to distract the beasts, giving the terrified man just enough time to flee.

The earliest substantial printed account of the Lady of Balconie is found in the geologist and writer Hugh Miller's 'Scenes and Legends of the North of Scotland' (1835), which set the story down as it was already being told among people of the district, suggesting the tale had circulated by word of mouth for some time before Miller committed it to paper.

The gorge's eerie atmosphere has continued to inspire. In 2004, its dramatic setting was used as a filming location for Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire, yet for centuries before the cameras arrived, locals knew Black Rock Gorge as a place where the boundary between the natural and the supernatural felt dangerously thin.

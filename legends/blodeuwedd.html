Deities Gwynedd, Wales

Blodeuwedd

The wizard Gwydion conjured a woman from nine wild flowers of Wales — meadowsweet, broom, oak, and six others — to be a wife for the cursed hero Lleu; she fell in love with a passing hunter, conspired to kill her husband, and was turned into an owl condemned to haunt the night forever shunned by every other bird.

A visual interpretation of Blodeuwedd's transformation in the Mabinogi
The Legend

Blodeuwedd ('Flower Face') is the created woman of the Fourth Branch of the Welsh Mabinogi, one of the most morally ambiguous figures in Welsh mythology. Lleu Llaw Gyffes had been cursed by his mother Arianrhod never to have a wife of the human race. His uncle, the great sorcerer Gwydion fab Dôn, and the mathematician-wizard Math fab Mathonwy circumvented the curse by creating a woman from the flowers of the oak, the broom, and the meadowsweet — and from six other blossoms the tale does not name. The flowers were worked into human form with an act of Gwydion's magic, and Blodeuwedd — 'flower face' — came into being.

As punishment the wizard Gwydion turned her into an owl.

The Woman Made from Flowers

She was given to Lleu as his wife and they lived together at the court of Ardudwy in Gwynedd. But when a lord named Gronw Pebr came hunting in the area, Blodeuwedd fell immediately and deeply in love with him. She and Gronw plotted Lleu's death, but this was complicated by the conditions of another curse: Lleu could only be killed at a precise moment — neither by day nor night, neither indoors nor outdoors, neither on horseback nor on foot — and only with a spear that had been a year in the making on Sundays. Blodeuwedd coaxed the conditions from Lleu himself, staged the precise scenario, and Gronw's spear struck him. Lleu transformed into a wounded eagle and flew to an oak tree in the mountains of Gwynedd.

Gwydion tracked the eagle by following a sow that fed on the flesh falling from the tree, sang three englyns to coax Lleu down, and restored him to human form. Gronw Pebr was killed and Gwydion turned Blodeuwedd into an owl — to wander ashamed through the night, hated by all other birds. The Welsh name for owl, tylluan, is said to derive from this story. The tale is a sophisticated study in agency, creation, and moral responsibility: Blodeuwedd acts on authentic feeling, but she was made without choice and without a past. Whether she is villain or victim is a question the Mabinogi deliberately refuses to answer.

Bluecap

A mine spirit seen as a small blue flame in the pit. Unlike crueler goblins, Bluecaps were often helpful workers, but expected fair wages left for them like any miner.

The Legend

The Bluecap (or Blue-cap) is a mine-spirit of the folklore of the northern English coalfields, especially of Northumberland and Durham, where the pitmen told of a helpful goblin that worked alongside them in the dark of the workings. It appeared, when it appeared at all, as a small dancing blue flame — a pale light flickering in the gallery — which would settle on a full coal-tub and guide it along the underground ways, doing the work of a strong man and moving the heavy corves of coal as if by itself.

The Story

What sets the Bluecap apart from the crueller goblins of folklore is its honesty and its expectation of fair dealing. The Bluecap would labour for the miners, but it demanded to be paid a proper working wage, no more and no less. The pitmen would leave its earnings in a quiet corner of the mine; if the sum left was too little, the offended Bluecap would refuse to touch it, and if too much, it would take only its rightful due and leave the surplus behind — a spirit of scrupulous fairness in an industry of hard and dangerous labour.

The Bluecap belongs to the wider family of mine-spirits — kin to the Cornish Knockers and the Welsh coblynau — beings born of the underground world's real perils, who might warn of danger, lead to good seams, or punish the disrespectful. As a fair-dealing fellow-worker rather than a tormentor, the Bluecap reflects the pitmen's own values, a small blue flame of comradeship and honest wages in the deep dark of the northern pits.

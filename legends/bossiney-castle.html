Bossiney Castle

Beneath Bossiney Mound near Tintagel, King Arthur's golden Round Table is said to lie buried, rising each Midsummer Eve at midnight to glitter in the moonlight before sinking back into the earth to await his return.

The Legend

Bossiney, near Tintagel on the north coast of Cornwall, is a small place with a great Arthurian legend, centred on Bossiney Mound, a grassy, man-made motte that was once the heart of a Norman castle, set in the very heart of Cornwall's Arthurian country. The manor was recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086 as Botcinnii, held by Robert, Count of Mortain, the Conqueror's half-brother, who built the neighbouring castles of Launceston and Trematon and may have raised Bossiney's motte himself, or left the work to his successors in the century that followed. The mound was first surveyed by the antiquarian Henry MacLauchlan in 1852, the earliest scholarly record of the site beyond the bare Domesday entry. By the mid-13th century the earls of Cornwall had shifted their seat to neighbouring Tintagel, and Bossiney's own castle faded from use, leaving only the motte and the legend it carries.

In Tintagel country, low earthworks are enough for legend to imagine hidden courts.

The Vanished Court

Legend holds that King Arthur's golden Round Table lies buried beneath Bossiney Mound, and that once each year, at midnight on Midsummer Eve, the great table rises up out of the ground to stand glittering in the moonlight, throwing a flash of light into the sky before it sinks again into the earth. The yearly rising is said to herald, or to await, the prophesied return of Arthur and his knights from their long sleep, ready to come again in Britain's hour of need.

The tradition belongs to the wider Cornish landscape of Arthurian belief around Tintagel, the headland of his conception, with Merlin's Cave below it, and to the great British myth of the sleeping king who is not dead but only waiting. In a Christianised version of the tale, the golden Round Table will rise at the end of the world to be carried up to heaven, the saints seated round it. With its buried golden table and its midsummer rising, Bossiney Mound keeps the promise of Arthur's return fixed to a quiet green hillock on the Cornish coast.

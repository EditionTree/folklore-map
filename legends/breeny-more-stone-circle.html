Sacred Sites Breeny More, County Cork

Breeny More Stone Circle

An axial stone circle near Kealkill whose Irish name — brúine móra, ‘great dwellings of the fairies’ — marks it as a place the Good People claimed long before archaeologists mapped its solstice alignments.

A visual interpretation of Breeny More Stone Circle near Kealkill
The Legend

Breeny More stone circle stands a kilometre southeast of Kealkill village in West Cork, overlooking Bantry Bay. An axial circle with two entrance stones aligned northeast to southwest, it captures both winter and summer solstice — a deliberate astronomical orientation that links it to the wider tradition of Cork-Kerry stone circles built as observatories and ceremonial sites.

The Irish name of Breeny More carries the memory of fairy dwellings in the hills.

Great Dwellings of the Fairies

The site’s Irish name, brúine móra — ‘great dwellings of the fairies’ — places it firmly within Ireland’s fairy-fort tradition. Across the country, ancient stone structures were understood as doorways to the Otherworld, homes of the sí who would punish anyone rash enough to disturb their dwelling. That Breeny More earned such a name suggests it retained an atmosphere of supernatural danger long after its Bronze Age builders had passed from memory.

Within the circle lie four boulder burials dating to the mid to late Bronze Age, between 1500 and 1000 BC. This rare burial type, found only in Cork and Kerry with eighty-four examples in total, represents a regional mortuary tradition found nowhere else in Ireland. No human skeletal material has survived in the acidic soil, leaving the identity of those interred here to the imagination of the fairies and the archaeologists alike.

Brollachan

BROL-akh-an

A formless, voiceless spirit of the Scottish Highlands — the Brollachan has no fixed shape and takes the form of whatever vessel or hollow it inhabits. It speaks only two words — 'myself' and 'thee' — and when threatened becomes whatever thing causes most fear.

The Legend

The Brollachan is a formless, half-made spirit of the Scottish Highlands, the offspring or attendant of the dreaded water-demon called the Fuath. It has no fixed shape of its own and no power of speech beyond two phrases — 'Myself' and 'Thyself' — and it lurks by night, able to fill or inhabit any vessel or form it finds, growing monstrous in the dark.

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

The classic tale, recorded by J. F. Campbell, tells of a crippled beggar sleeping by a mill fire when the Brollachan comes in and lies down in the embers beside him. When the man stirs the fire and burns it, the shapeless thing shrieks, and its mother the Fuath rushes in demanding who has hurt her child. The Brollachan can only cry 'Myself!' — and so, when the Fuath would avenge it, there is no one to blame, and the beggar escapes. The story turns on the same trick by which Odysseus blinded the Cyclops, showing how widely that ancient motif travelled into the folklore of the glens.

Brown Lady of Raynham Hall

Britain's most famous ghost photograph: a brown-robed figure descending a staircase at Raynham Hall, said to be Dorothy Walpole, locked away by her husband until her death in 1726.

A visual interpretation of the Brown Lady descending the staircase at Raynham Hall
The Legend

The Brown Lady is traditionally identified as Dorothy Walpole (1686–1726), sister of Britain's first Prime Minister Robert Walpole. According to the legend, her marriage to the volatile Charles Townshend, 2nd Viscount Townshend, ended after he discovered she had been unfaithful; he is said to have confined her to Raynham Hall, where she lived out her remaining years isolated from her children before dying, officially of smallpox, in 1726.

Britain's most famous ghost photograph shows a brown-robed figure descending a staircase at Raynham Hall.

The Figure on the Staircase

Sightings of a woman in a brown brocade dress with empty eye sockets date back to at least Christmas 1835, when Colonel Loftus and a house guest both reported seeing the figure on the staircase. The following year the novelist Captain Frederick Marryat — a friend of Charles Dickens — stayed at the Hall specifically to investigate, and according to his daughter's account encountered the apparition in a corridor and fired his revolver at it; the bullet passed straight through and lodged in a door. Sightings reportedly grew rarer after that.

The Brown Lady's fame rests largely on a single photograph taken on 19 September 1936 by Country Life photographers Captain Hubert Provand and Indre Shira, who said they watched a 'vapoury form' descend the staircase as they prepared to shoot the hall's interior. Published that December, the image was examined by paranormal investigator Harry Price, who could find no evidence of fakery. Later skeptics, including Joe Nickell, have argued it shows signs of double exposure, and magician John Booth recreated similar effects using mundane techniques — but no explanation has fully displaced the photograph's reputation as the most iconic ghost image ever taken.

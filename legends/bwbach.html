Bwbach

BOO-bakh

A Welsh household spirit similar to a brownie, rewarding good housekeeping and punishing idleness or disrespect. In later tales it gained a sharp dislike for teetotallers and dissenting ministers.

The Legend

The Bwbach (also written Bwca) is the Welsh household spirit, the close cousin of the English brownie and the Scottish brownie or bwca — a small, hairy, helpful goblin that attaches itself to a farm or homestead and labours about it unseen, especially by night. In return for its work threshing, churning and tidying, the Bwbach asks only to be treated with respect and to be left its due: a bowl of cream or milk, a share of the food, and a warm place by the fire. A well-kept house with a good-natured, generous family would prosper under its care.

The Otherworld at the Heart of the Tale

But the Bwbach was easily offended, and woe to the household that slighted it. Stinginess, mockery, or (fatally) any attempt to pay it with clothes or to drive it away would turn the helpful spirit into a malicious poltergeist, breaking crockery, souring milk, pinching the idle and making the house a misery. Like the brownie it had its pride: a famous trait of the Bwbach in later folklore was a sharp dislike of teetotallers and of dour, dissenting ministers, one celebrated tale tells of a Bwbach that plagued a Methodist preacher who would take no beer, tipping him from his chair and bedevilling him until he fled.

The Bwbach embodies the old moral economy of the folk household: prosperity and ease for the diligent, the respectful and the generous; chaos and ill-luck for the lazy, the mean and the proud. It is among the most homely and human of Welsh fairy beings, a spirit of the hearth keeping watch over the conduct of the family it served.

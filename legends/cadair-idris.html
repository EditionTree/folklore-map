Cadair Idris

KAD-air ID-riss

Cadair Idris, the Chair of Idris, is associated with the giant Idris Gawr. A local tradition warns that anyone who spends the night on the mountain will awaken either mad or a poet.

The Legend

Cadair Idris, 'the Chair of Idris', is a great mountain rising above the southern edge of Snowdonia in Gwynedd. Tradition holds it to be the seat of Idris, a giant (Idris Gawr) or, in some tellings, a mighty prince, warrior and astronomer-poet who would sit upon the mountain to study the stars, the broad hollow of the summit forming his chair. The mountain's dramatic cliffs, dark lake-filled cwms and frequent crown of cloud have long made it a place of awe.

Those who slept on the summit were said to wake mad, dead, or a poet.

The Night on the Mountain

Its most famous legend concerns the danger and the gift of sleeping there. It is said that anyone who spends a night alone on the summit of Cadair Idris will come down in the morning either a madman or a poet — the mountain bestowing either inspiration or ruin on whoever dares the vigil, with no middle path. The earliest known record of the belief appears in Edward Jones's The Bardic Museum of Primitive British Literature (1802), and it was repeated and embroidered throughout the 19th-century Welsh Romantic revival, casting the mountain as a threshold of the Otherworld, a place where the boundary between mortal mind and the powers beyond it grows thin.

The dark lake of Llyn Cau, cradled in the mountain's flank, has its own legends of a bottomless depth and a lurking monster, and the Cŵn Annwn, the spectral hounds of the Welsh Otherworld, were said to course the heights. Between the giant's chair, the poet's perilous sleep and the haunted lake, Cadair Idris is one of the most legend-laden mountains in Wales, a peak where landscape and myth are inseparable.

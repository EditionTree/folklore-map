Deities Highland, Scotland

Cailleach

KAL-yakh

The divine hag of winter, one of the oldest supernatural beings in Celtic mythology, shaped the mountains of Scotland by striding across the land and dropping boulders from her apron. Ben Nevis is said to be her throne.

The Legend

A one-eyed giantess of immense age, the Cailleach, the Veiled One or Hag, personifies winter itself across Gaelic Scotland and Ireland. She washes her plaid in the whirlpool of the Corryvreckan until it turns white as snow, and with her hammer and creel she shapes the land, letting boulders fall to raise mountains and carve out glens. Many a Highland peak and cairn is held to be her work or her resting seat, and Ben Nevis itself is counted among her thrones.

The Hag of Winter

In Ireland she is remembered above all as the Cailleach Bheara, the Hag of Beara, whose lament survives as one of the oldest poems in Old Irish, composed around the ninth or tenth century: an old woman looking back on a life that once knew kings, mead and wine, and now knows only whey-water and the company of shrivelled crones. Tradition gave her seven periods of youth, in each of which she outlived a husband and bore children who became the ancestors of whole regional dynasties, among them the Corcu Duibne and Corca Loighdhe. She is tied to real ground across the south-west of Ireland too, said to have been born at Teach Mor near Dingle in County Kerry, remembered in the rock formation called Hag's Head on the Cliffs of Moher in County Clare, and represented by a natural boulder at Kilcatherine on the Beara peninsula itself.

She rules the dark half of the year, from Samhain to Beltane, when she either turns to stone or gives way to the summer goddess Bride; in some tellings the two are one being, eternally renewed. As guardian of the wild places and herder of the red deer, the Cailleach embodies the harsh, sovereign power of the northern land, surviving in place-names from the Highlands to Kerry and remaining among the oldest and most formidable deities still remembered in these islands.

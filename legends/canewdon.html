Witches Essex

Canewdon

The Essex village of Canewdon was held to harbour six witches at all times — 'as many witches as there are trees in the churchyard' — and a master witch while the church tower stood, lore tied to the cunning-man George Pickingill.

A visual interpretation of Canewdon's churchyard witch-lore
The Legend

Canewdon, on the high ground above the River Crouch, was for generations reckoned the most bewitched village in Essex. The saying ran that so long as the old church tower stood there would always be six witches in the parish, or in another telling 'as many witches as there are trees in the churchyard' — and that whenever a witch died, a stone would fall from the tower and another woman would take up the power.

So long as the old church tower stood, Canewdon was said to harbour witches in every generation.

The Village of Six Witches

The tradition gathered around the figure of George Pickingill, a nineteenth-century farm labourer and reputed cunning-man of Canewdon, said to command the local witches and to halt farm machinery until he was paid his due. Other tales told of running round the church at midnight to raise the spirits, or of witches who could stop a wagon dead in the road. Whatever the truth of Pickingill himself, the village's reputation made it a fixed point in the witch-lore of the Essex countryside.

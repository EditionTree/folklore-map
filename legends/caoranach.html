Caoranach

Caoranach is a monstrous creature of Irish folklore associated with Lough Derg in County Donegal. One local tradition tells that a small worm cast into the lake grew into a beast that devoured cattle until it was slain, its blood staining the rocks red.

The Legend

Caoranach is a monstrous water-beast of Irish legend whose home is Lough Derg in County Donegal, one of Ireland's great pilgrimage sites. In the tradition she is sometimes named as the mother of demons, or as the last of a brood of serpents loosed upon Ireland. One widely told version begins small and sinister: a tiny worm or creature cast into the lake that fed and grew until it became a ravening beast, devouring cattle and any traveller who came too near the water's edge.

The Water Legend at the Heart of the Tale

Her death is bound up with Ireland's patron saint. In the local legend it is Saint Patrick who does battle with Caoranach, wrestling or hunting the monster through the dark water until he slays her — and her blood, pouring out, is said to have stained the lake and its stones red, giving Lough Derg ('the red lake') its very name. The combat is the Irish echo of the wider Christian motif of the saint who conquers the serpent of the old pagan order, and it helped sanctify the lake as holy ground.

It is no accident that the island in Lough Derg became known as Saint Patrick's Purgatory, one of the most important medieval pilgrimage destinations in Europe. The slaying of Caoranach gave the place its founding miracle: where once a demon-serpent had ruled the waters, penitents now came to fast, pray and keep vigil. The story binds landscape, etymology and faith together in a single vivid image of a monster overthrown.

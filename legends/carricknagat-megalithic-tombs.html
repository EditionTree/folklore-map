Carricknagat Megalithic Tombs

Two Neolithic tombs near Ballygawley — the Giant’s Grave and Dermot & Gráinne’s Bed — whose name, ‘the cats’ stone’, hints at wild spirits haunting the ancient dead.

The Legend

Carricknagat sits halfway between Ballygawley and Ballintogher in County Sligo, near a source of the Ballisodare River. Built between 4000 and 2500 BC, the site holds two distinct megalithic structures, each carrying its own folk name. The western tomb, a wedge-shaped gallery grave, is known locally as the Giant’s Grave — its trapezoidal chamber, four metres long with jamb-divided inner sections, evokes the resting place of some colossal ancient being.

The Story

The second structure, a dolmen, bears the name Leaba Dhiarmada agus Ghráinne — Dermot and Gráinne’s Bed. Across Ireland, dozens of megalithic tombs carry this title, each claimed as a stopping place where the legendary lovers rested during their flight from the ageing Fionn mac Cumhaill. That Carricknagat earned its own place in this cycle suggests the site held particular significance in local storytelling traditions.

The place-name itself carries a darker resonance. Carricknagat translates as ‘the cats’ stone’ or ‘the pine martens’ stone,’ reflecting a widespread folk belief that ancient monuments are haunted by wild animals representing the spirits of the dead who were interred there.

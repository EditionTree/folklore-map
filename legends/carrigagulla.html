Sacred Sites Carrigagulla, County Cork

Carrigagulla

None of Carrigagulla's sixteen stones stands higher than three feet, so short that local tradition credits the fairies, not human hands, with raising them.

The Legend

On a low hill north-east of Ballinagree, County Cork, sits a stone circle so modest in height that local tradition refused to credit it to human builders at all. Carrigagulla's sixteen stones, arranged around a central slab in a ring about eight metres across, come in at less than three feet at their tallest, and generations of storytelling put that down to the fairies, said to have raised the circle themselves rather than any ordinary hand.

The megalithic complex keeps its old silence in the Cork uplands.

The Stones Near Ballinagree

The circle is only part of a wider complex on the hillside. Close by stood a row of five stones; today four of them do duty as farm gates, moved from their original alignment at some point in the last century, with only one left standing where it was set. An ogham stone once stood nearby too, until it was uncovered by workers cutting turf and taken into the care of Cork Public Museum in 1940, where it has stayed ever since.

Archaeologists group the main ring as "Carrigagulla A" within the wider complex, and comparison with other excavated circles in the region suggests it was raised sometime between about 2400 and 1400 BC, well before the fairy story grew up to explain its oddly low stones.

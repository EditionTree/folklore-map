Carrigaphooca Stone Circle

Carrigaphooca Stone Circle is a stone circle and National Monument located in County Cork, Ireland. It is situated 4.4 km (2.7 mi) west of Macroom, immediately east of Carrigaphooca Castle, north of the N22, and near the confluence of the River Sullane and River Foherish. The name means "stone of the púca".

The Legend

Carrigaphooca Stone Circle is a small prehistoric stone circle in County Cork, set on high ground west of Macroom near the ruined tower of Carrigaphooca Castle, above the meeting of two rivers — and its very name binds it to one of the most feared spirits of Irish folklore. 'Carraig a' Phúca' means 'the Rock of the Púca', the Pooka, the shape-shifting goblin or phantom of Irish tradition whose haunts and crossings are marked across the country in such place-names.

The Story

The Púca is a creature of darkness and the wild, able to take many forms — most often a black horse, but also a goat, a hound, a bull or a monstrous dark man — and is by turns mischievous and malevolent. In its horse-shape it was said to seize unwary travellers, especially those abroad at night, and carry them on a terrifying headlong gallop across the country before throwing them off, shaken and lost; it haunted lonely roads, ruins and rocky places exactly like the crag at Carrigaphooca. November (Samhain) was its season, when its power was strongest.

Both the circle and the nearby castle, perched on its dark rock, gathered the Púca's reputation, the whole place held to be haunted by the spirit and avoided after dark. As a prehistoric monument whose name and lore are given over to the Pooka, Carrigaphooca is a fine example of how an ancient site becomes the dwelling of a folklore being, the old stones and the rocky crag above the river marked forever as the rock of the goblin-horse.

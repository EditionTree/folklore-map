Castle Downs, Cornwall

Crowning the hilltop of Castle an Dinas, this triple-ringed Iron Age fort served as King Arthur’s legendary hunting seat — a place where ghost soldiers still drill by moonlight.

The Legend

Castle an Dinas, the great hillfort at the summit of Castle Downs near St Columb Major, is one of Cornwall’s most storied ancient sites. Arthurian tradition names it as King Arthur’s hunting lodge, from which he rode out to hunt across Tregoss Moor. A lost stone near St Columb was once said to bear the four hoofprints of his horse. William of Worcester recorded the site’s legends during his 1478 visit to Cornwall, noting that Gorlois, Duke of Cornwall and husband of Ygraine — Arthur’s mother — was slain here. The 1504 Cornish-language play Beunans Meriasek further cemented the fort as a seat of the Dukes of Cornwall in local tradition.

Ghost soldiers are said to drill by moonlight within the hillfort's ramparts.

Arthur's Hunting Seat

The hillfort’s supernatural reputation extends beyond Arthurian romance. Cornish historian Samuel Drew recorded an extraordinary sighting from around the late eighteenth century: a spectral army appeared in the sky above the fort, possibly an unusual atmospheric phenomenon that locals interpreted through the lens of legend. In 1867, the Cornish scholar Henry Jenner collected a story from an elderly man at nearby Quoit who claimed to have witnessed the ghosts of King Arthur’s soldiers drilling within the ramparts, their muskets glinting in the moonlight. The Old Cornwall Society maintains ancient tradition by holding midsummer bonfires at the summit each year, honouring the Summer Solstice with ceremonies rooted in pre-Christian observance.

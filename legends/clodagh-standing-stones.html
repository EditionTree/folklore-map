Clodagh Standing Stones

Clodagh's two unequal stones are known locally as Adam and Eve, and belong to a Munster tradition that credits such pairs to a giant's stone-throwing contest.

The Legend

Two standing stones rise unevenly from a field three miles north-east of Drimoleague, County Cork: the taller reaching 1.5 metres, its partner to the south-west barely 0.85 metres, an unequal pair probably raised in the Bronze Age for a purpose no longer recorded. Local tradition gave the pair a homelier reading than archaeology can offer, naming such uneven couples "Adam and Eve" stones, one taken for a man and the other for a woman.

The two uprights form a quiet stone row in the Cork countryside.

The Paired Stones

Across Munster, similar stone pairs and rows are often folded into the same story: a stone-throwing contest between Fionn mac Cumhaill and a rival giant, the surviving stones marking where the missiles came down. No specific tale of that kind survives for Clodagh itself, but the pattern was common enough that it likely coloured how local people understood the stones once their original purpose had been forgotten.

The Clodagh pair is not alone on its hillside. Around 200 metres to the south stands a second pair of standing stones, set just three metres from a complete five-stone circle, part of a wider cluster of Bronze Age monuments that still stands in the fields above Drimoleague.

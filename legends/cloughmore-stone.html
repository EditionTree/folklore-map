Cloughmore Stone

A 50-tonne boulder Finn McCool supposedly hurled clean across Carlingford Lough at a rival giant, gouging a 'wishing chair' where it landed above Rostrevor.

The Legend

High on the slopes of Slieve Martin, nearly a thousand feet above the County Down village of Rostrevor, sits Cloughmore — from the Irish An Chloch Mhór, 'the big stone'. Geologists identify it as a glacial erratic, a fifty-tonne block of granite carried from an island off the Scottish coast and dropped here some ten thousand years ago as the last ice age retreated. Local tradition tells a different story entirely: that the giant warrior Fionn mac Cumhaill, locked in a three-day battle with a rival giant who styled himself the Giant of Snow and Ice, tore the boulder from the Cooley Mountains across the water in County Louth and flung it bodily over Carlingford Lough, crushing his opponent beneath it on impact.

The Story

The stone's landing is said to have carved out a hollow at its base known as Finn's Wishing Chair, and a persistent local custom holds that circling the boulder seven times will bring good luck to the walker who does it. The site now sits within Kilbroney Park and forms part of a National Nature Reserve, drawing hikers up the Cloughmore Stone trail for the view over Carlingford Lough and the Cooley Peninsula as much as for the folklore — a rare case of a scientifically explained landscape feature that has kept its giant's-throw legend fully alive alongside the geology.

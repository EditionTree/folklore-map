Birth of Albion

Black Dogs of Britain & IrelandArthurian BritainHaunted Churches & ChurchyardsLegends of the Sea & ShoreCursed Places & Ill-Fated StonesStanding Stones & Stone CirclesDragons & SerpentsHoly Wells & Healing SpringsBirth of Albion

Before the histories, Britain had a birth-story. Exiled Trojans led by Brutus, great-grandson of Aeneas, are said to have landed at Totnes to find an island held by giants — Gogmagog and his kind — whom they cast down to claim the land, renaming Albion as Britain. These are the legends of that founding: the Trojan founders, the last giants of Albion, and the mythic figures woven into the origin of the island itself.

The 'Matter of Britain' gave the island a legendary prehistory reaching back to the fall of Troy. Geoffrey of Monmouth's Historia Regum Britanniae (c.1136) fixed the story in its familiar form — Brutus and his followers settling an Albion inhabited only by giants, Corineus wrestling Gogmagog into the sea off the Cornish coast, and a line of legendary kings descending toward Lear, Lud and Cymbeline. Though wholly unhistorical, the tale was widely believed for centuries and left its mark on the landscape, from the Brutus Stone at Totnes to the Gog and Magog effigies still carried in the Lord Mayor's Show.

Some places carry a warning. A stone that must not be moved, a treasure that cannot be lifted, a family doom or a blight laid on a hall — the curse explains misfortune and enforces a boundary the living are not meant to cross. England's screaming skulls are among the best documented of these traditions: skulls of disputed origin kept in houses such as Bettiscombe Manor in Dorset, said to bring storms and poltergeist activity on anyone who tries to remove or bury them, a belief already old enough to draw curiosity-seekers by the 1880s. Many such traditions attach to tombs, boundary stones and disturbed ground.

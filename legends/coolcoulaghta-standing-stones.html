Coolcoulaghta Standing Stones

A Bronze Age stone pair in West Cork, aligned toward nearby Dunbeacon circle — torn from the ground in 1980, then reinstated three years later after local outcry proved ancient stones still command reverence.

The Legend

The Coolcoulaghta Standing Stones are a pair of Bronze Age monoliths standing about 1.8 metres tall in a field three kilometres southwest of Durrus in West Cork. Their alignment points directly toward the Dunbeacon stone circle, approximately four hundred metres to the west, suggesting the two sites formed a deliberate ceremonial pairing in the prehistoric landscape.

The Story

In Irish folklore, standing stone pairs like these are often attributed to Fionn mac Cumhaill, said to have hurled them from nearby mountains. The tradition reflects a deeper truth about how communities have understood these monuments: as objects of superhuman power, placed by beings greater than ordinary mortals. Other stones in the region carry petrification legends — tales of saints turning dairy thieves or transgressors to stone in moments of holy wrath.

The most remarkable chapter in Coolcoulaghta’s story is modern. In 1980, the stones were removed from their ancient positions. The act provoked such local outcry that the Office of Public Works reinstated them three years later, using a plan and elevation drawing made by Ordnance Survey Ireland archaeologists in 1977. Six thousand years after they were first raised, people fought to put them back — a striking testament to the hold these stones retain on the communities who live among them.

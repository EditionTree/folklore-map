Cú Roí mac Dáire

koo ROH-ee mak DAH-reh

The Legend

Cú Roí mac Dáire is a supernatural warrior-king of Munster in the Irish Ulster Cycle, a figure of immense and uncanny power whose hilltop fortress is identified with Caherconree on the Slieve Mish mountains of the Dingle peninsula in County Kerry. So great was his magic that his stronghold was said to revolve each night, its entrance hidden, so that no enemy could find the door after dark.

His stronghold is associated with Caherconree in the Slieve Mish Mountains, where legend says his fortress revolved at night so that enemies could not find its entrance.

The Fortress on the Mountain

He is most famous as the mysterious challenger in the tale of Bricriu's Feast: disguised as a hideous churl, he proposes a beheading game to the heroes of Ulster — he will let a man behead him tonight if that man will bear the return blow tomorrow. Only Cú Chulainn keeps the bargain and bares his neck, and for his courage is named the greatest champion of Ireland; the test is the clear ancestor of the medieval Gawain and the Green Knight. Cú Roí's own end comes through betrayal: his wife Bláthnat, in love with Cú Chulainn, reveals the secret of his life and signals the attack by which he is slain.

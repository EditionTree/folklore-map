Cu Sith

KOO-shee

A vast fairy hound of the Highlands, the size of a young bull and green as the mounds it haunts, gave voice three times while hunting. After the third bay, anyone who had not reached shelter was overcome with fatal terror.

The Legend

The Cu Sith, the 'fairy dog' of the Scottish Highlands, is a spectral hound the size of a young bull, with a shaggy dark-green coat and a long braided or coiled tail. Unlike the black dogs of England it is green as the fairy mounds it haunts, and it moves in eerie silence save for the three great bays it gives when on the hunt. Those bays were a death warning: anyone who heard them and had not reached safety by the third was said to be overcome with terror, sometimes fatally.

Those bays were a death-warning: anyone who heard them and failed to reach safety before the third was said to be overcome, or carried off.

The Three Bays

In Highland belief the Cu Sith served the fairies as hunting dog, watchdog and expedition companion, and it was especially feared as a stealer of nursing mothers, carried off to suckle the children of the Otherworld while their own infants went hungry. It was said to hunt always in a straight line, cutting across the land by hidden tracks rather than following any path a mortal would choose, so that its appearance ahead gave almost no warning at all. Folklorists have long ranked it more dangerous even than the Cat Sith, the fairy cat blamed for stealing the souls of the newly dead. The Cu Sith, tradition held, could take a soul from someone still living.

The creature belongs to the rich Gaelic tradition of supernatural hounds collected by John Gregorson Campbell in his Superstitions of the Highlands and Islands of Scotland (1900), and survives as one of the most distinctive of all the fairy beasts of Scotland.

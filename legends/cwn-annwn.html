Cŵn Annwn

koon AN-oon

The spectral hounds of Annwn, the Welsh Otherworld. Their baying grows quieter as they draw nearer — silence means they are upon you.

The Legend

The Cŵn Annwn, the 'Hounds of Annwn', are the spectral hunting dogs of the Welsh Otherworld, the pack of Arawn, king of Annwn, and later in folk tradition of Gwyn ap Nudd. In the medieval texts they are creatures of fearful beauty: shining white with blood-red ears, the traditional colouring of Otherworld animals in Celtic myth, glimpsed coursing a stag through the forest in the opening of the First Branch of the Mabinogi, where their master and Pwyll first meet.

Their baying grows quieter as they draw nearer - silence means they are upon you.

The Hounds of the Otherworld

In later Welsh folklore they became darker and more dreadful, the hounds of the Wild Hunt, sweeping across the night sky and the wild uplands of Wales, especially around the Brecon Beacons and Cadair Idris, as a portent of death. To hear them was to know that a soul was soon to be gathered. Their most uncanny trait is the way their cry behaves: the baying of the Cŵn Annwn sounds loudest when the pack is far off and grows fainter as they draw near, so that a distant howl is the true warning and a near-silent whisper means they are almost upon you.

They belong to the wide British and European family of phantom hunting hounds (kin to the Gabriel Hounds, the Wisht Hounds of Dartmoor and Dando's Dogs) but keep their distinctly Welsh character as the hounds of Annwn, escorting the dead to the Otherworld. With the coming of Christianity they were sometimes recast as hell-hounds, but their oldest role is gentler and stranger: the psychopomp pack of a king of the dead.

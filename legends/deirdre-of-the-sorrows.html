Deirdre of the Sorrows

Prophesied at birth to bring ruin to Ulster, Deirdre chose the warrior Naoise over the king who claimed her, fled with him to Scotland, and was lured back under a false promise of safe conduct. Naoise was killed. Deirdre, refusing every comfort, dashed her head against a stone rather than live without him.

The Legend

Deirdre is the tragic heroine of the Ulster Cycle, whose story is counted among the Three Sorrowful Tales of Ireland. At her birth it was prophesied that her beauty would bring death and ruin to Ulster, and the king Conchobar mac Nessa had her raised in seclusion to become his own wife. But Deirdre fell in love instead with the young warrior Naoise, and with him and his brothers, the sons of Uisneach, she fled across the sea to Scotland, living free in the glens of Argyll around Glen Etive.

Deirdre chose the warrior Naoise over the king who claimed her

The Exile Before Return

Conchobar lured them home with a false promise of safety, guaranteed by the hero Fergus. The pledge was betrayed: Naoise and his brothers were treacherously killed, and Deirdre, forced into the king's keeping, lived a year in unbroken grief before taking her own life rather than be passed to the man who had murdered her love, in some tellings dashing her head against a rock from his chariot. From her and Naoise's graves, it is said, two yew trees grew and intertwined.

The tale is preserved as Longes mac nUislenn ('The Exile of the Sons of Uisliu') in the Book of Leinster, a manuscript compiled around 1160, though scholars date the story's language to the Old Irish period, likely the ninth century, several centuries before that copy was made.

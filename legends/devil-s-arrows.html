Devil's Arrows

Three great standing stones near Boroughbridge, said to have been hurled by the Devil at the town of Aldborough. He missed, which is exactly the kind of thing folklore remembers forever.

The Legend

The Devil's Arrows are a row of three huge prehistoric standing stones near Boroughbridge in North Yorkshire, gritstone monoliths up to some seven metres tall and deeply weathered into long vertical grooves at their tops, among the most impressive Bronze Age monuments in the north of England. The stones were quarried from Plumpton Rocks, some fifteen kilometres away, and may originally have formed part of a larger alignment of as many as five stones, of which two are now lost. Their great size and the strange fluting of their summits invited explanation, and folklore supplied it in the figure of the Devil.

The Devil hurled them at Aldborough, the story says, and missed.

The Stones That Missed

The legend tells that the Devil, in a fury against the nearby town (usually the old Roman settlement of Aldborough), hurled these great stones at it to destroy it, launching them like arrows or bolts from a hill some miles off (in some versions How Hill or Howe Hill). But his aim was poor: the mighty missiles fell short and stuck fast in the ground where they now stand, missing the town entirely. The deep grooves down the stones are pointed to as the very marks of the Devil's fingers, scored into the rock as he gripped and flung them.

Such 'the Devil threw it' tales cluster around great prehistoric stones across Britain, where the medieval and later imagination, unable to credit human hands with raising such monuments, attributed them to giants or to the Devil, and explained any oddity of form as the mark of his touch. The Devil's Arrows, with their fingered grooves and their story of a misjudged throw at Aldborough, are among the finest examples of the type, folklore growing up to account for stones whose true makers had been forgotten.

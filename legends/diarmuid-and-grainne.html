Diarmuid and Grainne

Grainne placed a geis on the young warrior Diarmuid to flee with her on their wedding night rather than marry the ageing Fionn. For sixteen years they were hunted across Ireland and Scotland, leaving their names on hollow hills and stone beds. He died on Ben Bulben, killed by the boar Fionn refused to help him fight.

A visual interpretation of Diarmuid and Grainne in flight
The Legend

One of the great love-tales of the Irish Fenian Cycle, the pursuit of Diarmuid and Grainne begins when Grainne, betrothed to the ageing Fionn mac Cumhaill, instead lays a binding geis on the young and handsome warrior Diarmuid Ua Duibhne, compelling him to carry her away on the eve of the wedding. For years the lovers are hunted the length of Ireland by Fionn and the Fianna, sheltering in woods and cromlechs — many a dolmen is still called 'the bed of Diarmuid and Grainne'.

For sixteen years they were hunted across Ireland and Scotland

The Lovers on the Run

At last a grudging peace is made, but Fionn's jealousy is not spent. Diarmuid is fated to die by a great boar, and when he is gored hunting the wild pig of Ben Bulben in Sligo, Fionn — who alone could heal him with a drink of water carried in his hands — twice lets the water slip away until it is too late. The tale, with its old king, young lovers and doomed hero, is a clear forerunner of the later romance of Tristan and Iseult.

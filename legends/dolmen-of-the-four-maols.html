Dolmen of the Four Maols

Bronze Age burial cist on Primrose Hill where four brothers, the Maols, were interred after their execution for murdering Bishop Ceallach of Kilmoremoy in a 7th-century tale of kingship, treachery, and revenge.

The Legend

The Dolmen of the Four Maols overlooks the River Moy just outside Ballina, sitting atop Primrose Hill. Though its stones date to around 2000 BCE as the remains of a Bronze Age cist grave, its name and fame come from a much later tale rooted in the early Christian period. Ceallach, the story goes, was the rightful heir to the throne of Connacht; when his father fell in battle in 547 CE, his cousin Colman seized power instead. Rather than contest the crown, Ceallach returned to his religious studies and eventually became Bishop of Kilmoremoy, the ancient diocese of Ballina.

Four brothers were said to rest beneath the stones after Ceallach's murder was avenged.

The Tomb on Primrose Hill

When Colman died and his son Guaire Aidne inherited the kingship, Guaire grew fearful that the bishop might one day reclaim his birthright. He hired four brothers, Mael Mac Deoraidh, Maelcroin, Maeldalua and Maelseanaigh, together the Four Maols, to murder Ceallach, promising them land and wealth in return. The brothers killed the bishop and hid his body inside the trunk of a tree.

Ceallach's brother Cu-Coingelt hunted down the assassins and found them drunk at a celebratory feast. They were dragged across the river to Ardnaree, the 'Hill of Execution', and killed by quartering. Because the four brothers were of noble birth, tradition still demanded a proper burial, so their remains were carried back across the Moy and laid to rest at the ancient cist that has borne their name ever since, giving Ballina one of its most enduring landmarks.

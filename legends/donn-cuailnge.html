Donn Cúailnge

DON KOOL-nyuh

Two divine swineherds reborn through a dozen lifetimes became rival bulls. Donn Cuailnge was the prize behind the Tain Bo Cuailnge, and after a battle that shook every province, he bellowed three times and died at Cooley.

The Legend

Donn Cuailnge, the Brown One of Cooley, was no ordinary bull but a supernatural creature with a long and violent history behind it. The earliest tellings in the Ulster Cycle trace him and his Connacht rival Finnbhennach, the White-Horned, back to two divine swineherds named Friuch and Rucht, who fell into a feud after Friuch cast a spell that made Rucht's pigs grow thin out of spite. Enemies for life, the two passed through a chain of reincarnations, ravens, stags, warriors, phantoms, and finally maggots swallowed whole by two drinking cows, before being reborn as bulls: Donn Cuailnge to Daire mac Fiachna on the Cooley Peninsula in what is now County Louth, Finnbhennach to the herds of Ailill, king of Connacht.

The great Brown Bull of Cooley was the prize that sparked the Táin Bó Cúailnge.

The Bull That Began a War

The bull's powers were extraordinary. He could serve fifty heifers in a single day, his bellow filled the valley, a hundred warriors could shelter in his shadow, and spectral beings kept their distance from him. It was Queen Medb's jealousy at Ailill owning a bull to match Donn Cuailnge's equal that led her to launch the great cattle-raid described in the Tain Bo Cuailnge, the central epic of the Ulster Cycle, invading Ulster to seize him for herself.

Even after the war the two bulls could not be kept apart. Brought together again at Rath na dTairbh, 'the ringfort of the bulls', near Cruachan in Connacht, they fought for a full day and night, goring and trampling across the landscape with such force that the noise carried through every province, and place-names were said to mark each spot where a piece of the loser's dismembered body fell after Donn Cuailnge finally gored Finnbhennach to death and dragged the remains across the country on his horns. Mortally wounded himself, the Brown Bull made his way alone back to the Cooley Peninsula, bellowed three times, and died there, ending the cycle of enmity that had carried two rival swineherds through a dozen lifetimes.

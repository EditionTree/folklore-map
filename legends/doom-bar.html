Doom Bar

A dangerous sandbar at the mouth of the River Camel, linked to the Mermaid of Padstow. In legend, a dying mermaid cursed the harbour after being shot, raising the bar to wreck ships forever.

The legend

The Doom Bar is a treacherous bank of sand stretching across the mouth of the Camel estuary near Padstow in north Cornwall, a real and deadly hazard that has wrecked countless ships over the centuries — and Cornish legend explains its existence as a mermaid's dying curse. In the story a local man, out shooting near Hawker's Cove, saw a beautiful young woman in the water and either fell to talking with her or, in the harsher versions, shot her — only to discover too late that she was a mermaid of Padstow, the guardian spirit of the harbour, whose presence had kept its waters deep and safe.

The story

Mortally wounded and enraged at the betrayal, the dying mermaid pronounced a curse upon the harbour she had once protected: she swore that Padstow should have a bar of doom, a barren bank of sand that would choke the river-mouth and bring death and ruin to its shipping. As she sank beneath the waves a great storm rose, and when it cleared the Doom Bar had appeared across the estuary, where it has lain ever since, shifting and lethal, ending the days when great ships could ride safely into the once-prosperous port.

The legend is a classic example of folklore grown up to account for a genuine and dangerous feature of the landscape — the sandbar's grim toll of shipwrecks given a tragic supernatural cause. The Doom Bar remains a byword on the Cornish coast, its name now also carried far and wide by a popular ale, though its origin lies in a drowned mermaid's last, vengeful breath.

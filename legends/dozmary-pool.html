Dozmary Pool

A lonely pool on Bodmin Moor, often named as one of the places where Arthur's sword Excalibur was returned to the Lady of the Lake. It is also tied to ghostly and infernal stories.

The Legend

Dozmary Pool is a lonely, reed-fringed lake high on Bodmin Moor in Cornwall, a still sheet of water in a bare and windswept landscape that has drawn legend to it for centuries. Its most famous association is Arthurian: Dozmary is one of the waters claimed as the home of the Lady of the Lake, and the place where, after the last battle, the dying Arthur commanded Sir Bedivere to cast away the sword Excalibur, where a hand rose from the pool to catch the blade, brandish it, and draw it under. The pool was long reputed to be bottomless, which suited its role as a doorway to the Otherworld.

The Water Legend at the Heart of the Tale

Dozmary's other great legend is darker. It is bound to the ghost of Jan Tregeagle, a wicked 17th-century steward and magistrate of fearsome local memory, whose soul was conjured back from the dead to give evidence in a lawsuit and then could not be got rid of. To keep him from Hell and out of mischief, Tregeagle was set an impossible, eternal penance at Dozmary Pool: to empty the bottomless lake using only a leaking limpet shell. There he labours still, in the telling, until the howling of the moorland storms, when the demons come for him and he flees across the moor with the hounds of Hell at his heels, his shrieks heard on the wind.

In reality the pool is not bottomless (it has been known to dry out in times of drought), but its bleak isolation and its double burden of legend, Arthurian and infernal, have made it one of the most atmospheric and storied spots on Bodmin Moor.

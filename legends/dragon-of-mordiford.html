Dragon of Mordiford

A girl named Maud is said to have found a tiny serpent and raised it in secret until it grew into a dragon. It took to the hills above Mordiford, feeding on livestock and men while sparing only her.

The Legend

The Dragon of Mordiford is one of the best-known of the English dragon legends, attached to the Herefordshire village of Mordiford where the River Lugg meets the Wye. The story begins tenderly: a young girl named Maud found a beautiful little creature in the woods, no bigger than a cucumber, brilliantly coloured, and took it home as a pet and hid it. Despite her family's alarm she fed and cared for it, and the creature thrived, and grew, and kept growing, until it was a full-sized dragon that could no longer be hidden and took itself off to live in the wooded hills above the village, on Mordiford's 'Dragon's Walk'.

Maud's Secret Serpent

As it grew the dragon turned to preying on the countryside, devouring cattle and sheep and at last human victims, terrorising the whole district, yet it never harmed Maud, the girl who had raised it, sparing her alone out of its old affection. The villagers lived in dread until a champion came forward to destroy the beast. In the most popular version the dragon-slayer (sometimes named as a condemned criminal promised his freedom, sometimes as a member of the local Garston family) hid inside a cider barrel set by the river where the dragon came to drink, and shot or speared it through the bung-hole as it approached, killing the monster, though in some tellings he too died from its venomous dying breath.

For centuries a great painted image of a green dragon adorned the west end of Mordiford church, said to commemorate the legend, until it was removed in the early 19th century. The tale, with its poignant detail of the girl whose pet outgrew all bounds and the dragon that spared only her, remains among the most vivid of Britain's dragon stories.

Dragon of Strathmartine

Nine daughters of a farmer near Strathmartine were taken one by one by a great serpent dwelling in the moorland outside Dundee. A young man named Martin lured the beast to its death with cunning and a spiked ball of dough. The Dragon Stone standing near the site of the slaying once marked the field where the creature fell.

A visual interpretation of Martin outwitting the Dragon of Strathmartine
The Legend

The legend is fixed to the country north of Dundee, around Strathmartine and Baldragon, whose very names were said to recall the tale. A farmer's nine daughters went one by one to a well in the moor, and each was seized and devoured by a great serpent or dragon that lay coiled there. When the last did not return, the father raised the alarm, and a young man named Martin — betrothed to one of the daughters — took up a club and went out against the beast.

Martin lured the beast to its death with cunning and a spiked ball of dough.

The Spiked Ball of Dough

Driving it back blow by blow, the people cried 'Strike, Martin!' as he beat it down, and the place where it died was said to be remembered in the name Strathmartine, while a nearby spot was called Baldragon, the dragon's dwelling. The Martin Stone, a Pictish symbol stone still standing near Bridgefoot, bears a carved serpent that local tradition links to the slain dragon, tying the story to a real monument in the fields.

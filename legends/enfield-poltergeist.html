Enfield Poltergeist

Britain's most famous modern haunting: flying furniture, ghostly knockings, and a gruff old man's voice issuing from eleven-year-old Janet Hodgson at an Enfield council house, 1977-79.

The Legend

The Enfield poltergeist is the most famous British haunting of the twentieth century, reported between 1977 and 1979 at a council house at 284 Green Street in Enfield, north London. The Hodgson family (a single mother and her four children) described knockings, furniture that slid and overturned, objects that flew across rooms, and above all phenomena centred on eleven-year-old Janet: levitations, and a gruff old man's voice said to issue from her.

The family described knockings, furniture that slid and overturned, and objects that flew across rooms.

The House on Green Street

The case drew investigators from the Society for Psychical Research, notably Maurice Grosse and Guy Lyon Playfair, whose long vigils produced photographs and tape recordings that believers held to be compelling and sceptics dismissed as a mixture of trickery by the children and the suggestibility of the watchers. Playfair set down the fullest account in his 1980 book 'This House Is Haunted', drawing on Grosse's contemporaneous case notes and roughly 600 pages of tape transcripts made during the investigation itself, still the primary published record of what the family, Grosse and Playfair say took place at Green Street.

Never resolved either way, Enfield became a touchstone in the British argument over the reality of poltergeists, and its fame has only grown through books, documentaries and films that have made the Green Street house a modern legend.

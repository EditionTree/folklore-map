Fad Felen

VAHD VEH-len

The Yellow Plague — a monstrous creature of Welsh legend whose breath carried pestilence across the land. Said to take the form of a great serpent or a yellow mist rolling off the mountains, it features in tales of St Teilo who fled its devastation across the sea to Brittany.

A visual interpretation of Fad Felen, the Yellow Plague
The Legend

The Fad Felen — the 'Yellow Plague' or Yellow Pestilence — is a personification in Welsh tradition of the devastating sickness that swept Britain in the sixth century. Folklore turned the epidemic into a monstrous apparition: a spectral yellow creature, sometimes imagined as a pale column of mist or a ghastly beast, whose very breath or gaze carried death across the land.

The Yellow Plague was said to take the form of a great serpent or yellow mist.

The Breath of the Plague

It is bound above all to the death of Maelgwn Gwynedd, the powerful sixth-century king. Tradition holds that Maelgwn, sheltering in the church of Rhos, could not resist looking out through the keyhole or window and so beheld the Fad Felen — and to look upon it was to die. Through this story the historical plague that killed the king was given a face and a name, a yellow horror that even a great ruler could not escape once he had set eyes upon it.

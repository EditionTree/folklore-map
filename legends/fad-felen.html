Fad Felen

VAHD VEH-len

The Yellow Plague of sixth-century Wales, personified as a monstrous yellow apparition whose gaze alone was fatal. The poet Taliesin foretold it would rise from the marsh of Rhianedd to punish King Maelgwn Gwynedd for his crimes.

The Legend

The Fad Felen, the Yellow Plague or Yellow Pestilence, personifies the epidemic that swept Wales in the 540s. Tradition gave the sickness a shape: a column of watery cloud, one end resting on the ground and the other reaching high into the air, its hair, teeth and eyes all a livid yellow, the colour folk memory gave to the bloodless pallor of the dying. The poet Taliesin is credited with prophesying its coming, warning that a strange creature would rise from the marsh of Rhianedd to punish the crimes of Maelgwn Gwynedd, the powerful king of North Wales.

The Yellow Plague was said to take the form of a great serpent or yellow mist.

The Breath of the Plague

The prophecy fixed itself to Maelgwn's actual death. Sheltering from the plague in the church at Rhos, he could not resist looking out, by keyhole or window, and so beheld the Fad Felen; to look upon it was to die, and die he did, an event the Annales Cambriae date to 547. Through this story the historical plague that killed a king was given a face and a name, a yellow horror that even a great ruler could not escape once he had set eyes upon it.

Origins & Dating

Approximate Origin547 (historical death of Maelgwn Gwynedd, per Annales Cambriae)
Historical PeriodEarly Medieval Wales (historical death, 547); literary personification first confirmed in writing in the early 16th century
Earliest Written RecordMaelgwn's death in a 'great mortality' is recorded in the Annales Cambriae under 547; the specific personification as a yellow plague-creature and the Taliesin prophecy are first attested in the Hanes Taliesin, a text dating from the early 16th century, though drawing on earlier oral material, and in a later Welsh Triad

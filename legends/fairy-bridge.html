Fairy Bridge

A small Isle of Man bridge where travellers traditionally greet the fairies as they pass. Failing to acknowledge the Little People is considered unlucky, especially on journeys across the island.

A visual interpretation of Fairy Bridge
The Legend

The Fairy Bridge is a small, unremarkable-looking road bridge on the Isle of Man, crossing the Santon Burn on the main road between Douglas and Castletown, and yet it is one of the most observed sites of living folk custom in the British Isles. Manx tradition holds that the bridge is a haunt of the Themselves — the fairies, or 'Little People', whom the islanders have always been careful never to name directly or offend — and that anyone crossing it should greet them politely to ensure good luck and a safe journey.

The Otherworld at the Heart of the Tale

The custom is remarkably persistent. To this day many Manx people, and visitors who have learned the tradition, will offer a greeting as they pass over the bridge — 'Laa mie' (good day) in Manx Gaelic, or simply 'Hello, fairies' — and it is widely held that to cross without acknowledging the Little People risks bad luck, a spoilt journey, or worse. Drivers slow down to wish the fairies good day, and the parapets and surrounding hedges are often hung with ribbons, notes and tokens left for them.

The custom reflects the deep and characteristic Manx attitude to the fairies: a careful, respectful wariness toward an unseen people who must be placated rather than provoked, neither openly named nor lightly mocked. (Folklorists note that the modern roadside bridge may not be the 'true' original fairy bridge, an older crossing lying elsewhere, but it is here that the tradition has fixed itself.) The Fairy Bridge stands as a charming and genuinely surviving piece of folk belief, where a courtesy to the Little People is still exchanged daily on a busy island road.

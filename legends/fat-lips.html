Fat Lips

Fat Lips is the guardian spirit of Dryburgh Abbey's ruins in the Scottish Borders, said to keep the vaults dry by treading the floors in heavy iron shoes.

The Legend

Fat Lips was the guardian spirit, or brownie, of the romantic ruins of Dryburgh Abbey in Berwickshire, in the Scottish Borders — a curious and rather melancholy figure recorded by Sir Walter Scott, who knew the Borders and its lore intimately and lies buried at Dryburgh himself. The spirit took its odd name from its appearance, and was bound to the abbey ruins as their resident sprite, a lingering supernatural presence among the broken arches and old graves.

Fat Lips is the name given to a legendary spirit dwelling in Dryburgh Abbey in Berwickshire, Scotland.

The Otherworld at the Heart of the Tale

The tale Scott preserved gives Fat Lips a poignant human dimension. The 'spirit' was, in the telling, associated with a real recluse — a woman who, having lost her lover, withdrew from the world and lived for many years hidden among the vaults and ruins of Dryburgh Abbey, emerging only by night and shunning all human company. To the local people, her shadowy night-time figure flitting among the ruins became the abbey's resident brownie or bogle, and the name Fat Lips attached to the spirit said to haunt the place. It was said that Fat Lips kept the vaults dry by treading the damp floors with heavy iron shoes.

Fat Lips is a small but characteristic piece of Border folklore, blending the widespread belief in a guardian brownie attached to an old or holy site with the local memory of a real, tragic hermit — the kind of story in which a flesh-and-blood recluse fades, over the retellings, into the resident ghost or fairy of a ruin. Set among the beautiful, ivied ruins of Dryburgh, it gives the abbey a wistful spirit of its own.

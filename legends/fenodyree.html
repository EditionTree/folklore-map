Fenodyree

fuh-NAH-thuh-ree

The Fenodyree is a hairy Manx fairy known for performing heavy farm work with uncanny speed. Like related household spirits, he is offended by a gift of clothing and disappears, with one tradition associating his departure with Glen Rushen.

A visual interpretation of Fenodyree
The Legend

The Fenodyree (or phynnodderee) is the brownie of the Isle of Man, a large, hairy, ungainly spirit of great strength who attaches himself to a farm and does prodigious work by night — mowing whole meadows, threshing corn, and herding sheep with uncanny speed — asking nothing but a little food left out and, above all, never to be offered clothes. In one well-loved tale the Manx folk, grateful or pitying, left out a suit of clothes for the naked Fenodyree; offended, he recited each garment and the harm it would bring, and departed for good, in the manner of brownies everywhere who cannot abide being paid.

The Otherworld at the Heart of the Tale

Manx tradition gave him a melancholy origin: he was once one of the *ferrishyn*, the fair fairy folk, banished from their company and condemned to his shaggy, solitary shape for the sin of courting a mortal girl and missing a fairy festival to dance with her in the glen. So he labours alone among the farms of Man, half-helpful drudge and half-exile, the strongest worker on the island and the loneliest.

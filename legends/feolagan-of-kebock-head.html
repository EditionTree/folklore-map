Feolagan of Kebock Head

A mouse-sized fairy at Kebock Head paralysed grazing sheep by walking across their backs — curable only by making it retrace its steps, or trapping it in a jar of salt.

The Legend

Hebridean sheep-herding tradition, especially around Kebock Head on the Isle of Lewis, told of a small, mouse-sized fairy creature called the feolagan that would cross a sheep's back and leave it paralysed, unable to eat or drink, so that it would die unless the spell was broken. The only remedies were to catch the feolagan and force it to retrace its steps across the same sheep in the opposite direction, or — if many animals were afflicted at once — to trap the creature in a jar of salt and use the grains from the jar to treat the flock.

The Story

The creature is recorded in more than one online folklore reference with matching detail, but its provenance and antiquity could not be traced back to an older printed or academic folklore collection within the research session's search budget.

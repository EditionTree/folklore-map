Feolagan of Kebock Head

A mouse-sized fairy at Kebock Head paralysed grazing sheep by walking across their backs — curable only by making it retrace its steps, or trapping it in a jar of salt.

The Legend

Hebridean sheep-herding tradition, particularly the crofting townships around Kebock Head on the east coast of the Isle of Lewis, told of a small, mouse-sized fairy creature called the feolagan. To cross a sheep's back was enough for the feolagan to leave the animal paralysed, unable to eat or drink, so that without intervention it would die where it lay.

The Story

Two remedies were said to work. The feolagan itself could be caught and forced to retrace its steps back across the same sheep in the opposite direction, undoing whatever harm its first crossing had done. Where many animals fell ill at once and catching the creature was impractical, shepherds instead trapped it inside a jar of salt, then used grains from that jar to treat the whole flock.

The tale is recorded consistently across more than one folklore reference dealing with the Outer Hebrides, but its trail runs cold before reaching an older printed or academic collection, leaving its age and original source unclear.

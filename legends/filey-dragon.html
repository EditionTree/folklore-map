Filey Dragon

A fearsome dragon that made its lair in the tidal gully of Filey Brigg. The townsfolk defeated it by luring it to eat so much sticky parkin cake that its jaws seized shut and it plunged into the sea to drown. The jagged rocks of the Brigg are said to be its bones, jutting into the North Sea still.

A visual interpretation of the Filey Dragon on the Brigg
The Legend

The North Yorkshire coast town of Filey tells how its great rocky reef, Filey Brigg, is not natural rock at all but the bones of a dragon. A fearsome serpent once lairing in the tidal gully terrorised the townsfolk, until they hit upon a scheme as homely as the monster was terrible: they baited it with the sticky local gingerbread, parkin.

its jaws seized shut and it plunged into the sea to drown.

Parkin on the Rocks

The dragon gorged itself on so much of the clinging cake that its jaws were glued fast and its belly heavy, and when it lumbered down to the sea to wash the parkin from its teeth it floundered in the waves and drowned. Its long body, petrified, became the Brigg that still runs out from the headland, and the boulders of the reef are pointed out as its washed-out teeth. The tale is a fine example of the comic English dragon-slaying, where cunning and a local delicacy do what sword and spear could not.

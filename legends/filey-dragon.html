Filey Dragon

A fearsome dragon that made its lair in the tidal gully of Filey Brigg. The townsfolk defeated it by luring it to eat so much sticky parkin cake that its jaws seized shut and it plunged into the sea to drown. The jagged rocks of the Brigg are said to be its bones, jutting into the North Sea still.

The Legend

The North Yorkshire coast town of Filey tells how its great rocky reef, Filey Brigg, is not natural rock at all but the bones of a dragon that once laired in the tidal gully and terrorised the town. The tale is first set down in print in the Reverend Parkinson's 'Yorkshire Legends and Traditions' (1888), though the story it tells is presented as already well established local lore by that date. The townsfolk defeated it with a scheme as homely as the monster was terrible: they baited it with parkin, the sticky, treacle-dark oatmeal cake still baked in Yorkshire kitchens. In one telling it was a tailor named Ralph Parkin, known locally as Billy Biter, who came up with the plan, and local tradition holds that the cake itself took the family's name in memory of the dragon-slaying.

Parkin on the Rocks

The dragon gorged itself on so much of the clinging cake that its jaws were glued fast and its belly heavy, and when it lumbered down to the sea to wash the parkin from its teeth it floundered in the waves and drowned. Its long body, petrified, became the Brigg that still runs out from the headland, and the boulders of the reef are pointed out as its washed-out teeth. The tale is a fine example of the comic English dragon-slaying, where cunning and a local delicacy do what sword and spear could not.

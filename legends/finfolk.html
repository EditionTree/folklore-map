Finfolk

Powerful sea-sorcerers of Orcadian folklore who move between the underwater realm of Finfolkaheem and the vanishing island of Hildaland. They steal mortals for spouses and servants.

The legend

The Finfolk are the dark sorcerers of the sea in Orcadian folklore — a mysterious, amphibious race of powerful and sinister beings who dwell beneath the waves and come ashore to work their will on the islanders. Gloomy, grasping and skilled in magic, they were among the most feared of all the supernatural peoples of Orkney, for their great vice was the abduction of mortals: a Finman or Finwife would carry off a human husband, wife or servant to live with them beneath the sea, and many an Orkney tale tells of a young person snatched from the shore or the boat by the Finfolk and lost to their kin forever.

The story

The Finfolk lived between two homes. In winter they dwelt in Finfolkaheem, their splendid city on the sea-bed, a place of crystal halls and gardens of waving sea-plants lit by the glimmer of the deep. In summer they came up to Hildaland (or Hether Blether), a magical vanishing island — one of the enchanted 'disappearing isles' of Orkney lore — which could be seen on the horizon but never reached, fading away as a boat approached. A famous tale tells how the island was at last won from the Finfolk and made fast by a determined farmer, becoming the island of Eynhallow, the 'holy isle', from which the Finfolk were driven by salt and faith.

The Finwife, in particular, was dreaded: a Finman's wife was said to lose her beauty as she aged and was set to drudgery, and was always seeking mortal gold, for a Finwife who could win a human husband would keep her beauty. Distinct from the gentler selkies and the mermaids, the Finfolk are the malign magicians of the Orkney sea — proud, covetous and dangerous, a whole hidden civilisation beneath the northern waters.

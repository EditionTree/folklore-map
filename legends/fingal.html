Fingal

The great Gaelic giant-hero Fionn mac Cumhaill, known in Scotland as Fingal. He built the causeway between Scotland and Ireland so he could fight his Ulster rival — the hexagonal columns of Fingal's Cave and the Giant's Causeway are said to be his road.

The Legend

Fingal is the name under which the great Gaelic hero Fionn mac Cumhaill passes into Scottish tradition, especially in the western Highlands and Islands — popularised across Europe by James Macpherson's eighteenth-century Ossian poems, which made 'Fingal' a king of Morven. In the older folk legend he is a giant, and to him is credited the building of the Giant's Causeway, the great colonnade of basalt that runs out into the sea off Antrim.

He built the causeway between Scotland and Ireland so he could fight his Ulster rival.

The Giant Who Built the Causeway

The famous tale tells how Fingal built the causeway to cross to Scotland and fight his rival, the giant Benandonner; but seeing how huge his enemy was, he let his wife disguise him as a baby in a cradle. When Benandonner saw the size of the 'infant', he fled in terror at the thought of its father, tearing up the causeway behind him so the two could never meet — which is why only its ends remain, at the Antrim shore and at the island of Staffa, where the matching basalt columns frame Fingal's Cave. The cave's strange natural acoustics and grandeur later inspired Mendelssohn's celebrated overture.

Fintan's Grave

The Arra Mountains near Lough Derg hold Fintan's Grave, resting place of Ireland's oldest witness, who outlived the Flood as a salmon, an eagle, and a hawk before living on for millennia.

The Legend

Fintan's Grave is a mythological cave or site on the mountain of Tul Tuinde ('the hill of the wave') in the Arra Mountains near Lough Derg, associated with Fintan mac Bóchra, one of the most extraordinary figures of Irish legendary history. Fintan is the great survivor of Irish myth: he is said to have come to Ireland before the Biblical Flood, with the company of Cessair, the very first settlers, and to have lived on, alone or transformed, through all the ages that followed.

Fintan was said to remember the oldest ages of Ireland from his mountain refuge.

The Ancient Witness

When the Flood came and drowned all the rest, Fintan survived. In the tradition he was changed into a salmon, and later an eagle and a hawk, before regaining human form, and so lived for thousands of years through every successive invasion and settlement of Ireland, from the days before the Deluge down to the coming of Christianity. Because he had seen it all, Fintan became the supreme witness and rememberer, the living repository of the whole history and lore of Ireland, summoned in legend to recount the events of the ages no other living being could recall.

The place named as his grave, where the ancient witness at last lay down, fixes this vast mythological figure to the landscape near Lough Derg. Fintan belongs to the same family of Irish 'oldest beings' as the ancient animals questioned in other tales, and embodies a profound theme of Irish myth: the unbroken memory of the land, carried through flood and ages by a single deathless witness, so that nothing of the old knowledge should ever be wholly lost.

Legendary Figures Beddgelert, Gwynedd

Gelert

Gelert is the faithful hound of a famous Welsh tale. Prince Llywelyn kills the dog after mistaking blood on its muzzle for evidence that it harmed his child, then discovers that Gelert had saved the child from a wolf.

The legend

The tale of Gelert is the most famous and most heartbreaking of all Welsh dog-legends, told of the village of Beddgelert ('Gelert's grave') in Snowdonia. Prince Llywelyn the Great, it runs, owned a faithful hound named Gelert, whom he left one day to guard his infant son while he went hunting. Returning, he found the cradle overturned and empty, the child gone, and Gelert bounding to meet him with his muzzle dripping blood. Believing the hound had killed his son, Llywelyn ran it through with his sword in a fit of grief and rage.

The story

At the dog's dying yelp, the prince heard a child's cry; he found his son safe beneath the upturned cradle, and beside it the body of a great wolf that Gelert had killed in defending the baby. Too late Llywelyn understood, and he is said never to have smiled again, burying the loyal hound with honour beneath a cairn that gave the village its name. The story is a fine example of the ancient and widespread 'faithful hound wrongly slain' motif — found from Wales to India — and though the Beddgelert grave was likely promoted by an eighteenth-century innkeeper to draw visitors, the tale has long since become the village's own.

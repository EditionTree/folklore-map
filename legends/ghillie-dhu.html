Ghillie Dhu

The Ghillie Dhu (Gaelic for dark haired lad) is a solitary fairy said to have lived among the birch woods near Gairloch, clothed in leaves and moss and shy of adults but gentle toward children. He is best known through a story recorded by the Highland landowner Osgood Mackenzie, in which he sheltered a lost local girl, Jessie Macrae, in the woods overnight and led her safely home.

The Legend

Unlike many fairy figures who are anonymous or composite, the Ghillie Dhu of Gairloch was, in local memory, a specific and individual spirit, small, dark haired and dressed in leaves, moss and lichen, who was said to haunt the birch woods around Loch a Druing on the Gairloch estate through much of the nineteenth century. He avoided adults and was reportedly once the target of an attempted ambush by curious lairds, who kept watch through the night but never saw him.

A young girl named Jessie Macrae became lost in the woods after dark and was found comforted and sheltered until morning by the Ghillie Dhu.

The Watcher in the Birches

The fullest surviving account comes from Osgood Mackenzie's memoir A Hundred Years in the Highlands, published in 1921 but describing events and family lore reaching back into the earlier nineteenth century. In it, a young girl named Jessie Macrae became lost in the woods after dark and was found comforted and sheltered until morning by the Ghillie Dhu, who led her home unharmed. Mackenzie's account, along with other local testimony he collected, is the primary source for the tradition, and no earlier printed record of the Gairloch Ghillie Dhu specifically has been identified, though solitary tree and woodland spirits of this general type are attested more widely in Highland oral tradition.

Glastonbury Holy Thorn

Somerset

Joseph of Arimathea is said to have thrust his pilgrim's staff into the earth on Wearyall Hill above Glastonbury, where it took root and became a hawthorn that flowers twice yearly — at Christmas and at Easter. Cuttings from the original were preserved through the centuries; the trees on the hill today descend from those medieval grafts. A sprig is still sent to the monarch each Christmas, as it has been since the time of James I.

Gogmagog

The last of the giants of Albion, said to have roamed Cornwall before being cast into the sea by the Trojan hero Corineus. Effigies of Gog and Magog still march in the Lord Mayor's Show — ancient guardians of the City of London.

The Legend

Gogmagog is the last and greatest of the giants of Albion in the legendary history of Britain set down by Geoffrey of Monmouth. Before the coming of Brutus and his Trojans, the island was said to be held by a race of giants, of whom Gogmagog — twelve cubits tall and able to uproot oaks as though they were hazel wands — was the champion. When the Trojans landed in the west and the giants fell upon them, it was the Trojan Corineus, who delighted in wrestling giants, who took up the challenge of their leader.

Corineus heaved the giant onto his shoulders and hurled him from the heights to the rocks below.

The Last Giant of Albion

The two grappled on the cliffs above the sea near Plymouth in a terrible bout; Gogmagog crushed three of Corineus's ribs, but the enraged Trojan heaved the giant onto his shoulders and hurled him from the heights to be dashed to pieces on the rocks below, at a place long remembered as 'Gogmagog's Leap'. The giant's name lived on across the country — in the Gogmagog Hills near Cambridge, and in the pair of giant effigies, Gog and Magog, that have been carried in the Lord Mayor's Show and stood in London's Guildhall for centuries as the city's mythic guardians.

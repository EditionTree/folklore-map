Gooseberry Wife

A great caterpillar-like spirit said to guard gooseberry bushes on the Isle of Wight, keeping children from stealing fruit before it was ripe. Her name alone was enough to keep small hands away from the patch — whether she was real or merely a parents' invention, the gooseberries remained unmolested.

The Legend

The Gooseberry Wife is a nursery bogey of the Isle of Wight, conjured to keep children out of the fruit bushes before the gooseberries were ripe. She was said to take the form of a great hairy caterpillar, swollen and slow, who guarded the bushes and would seize any child who came creeping among them — 'if ye goos out in the gearden, the Gooseberry Wife'll be sure to ketch ye'.

The Otherworld at the Heart of the Tale

Like Peg Powler at the riverbank or the Awd Goggie among the orchards, she belongs to the class of 'nursery sprites' or 'cautionary spirits' that English country parents invoked to fence off danger or temptation. There is no grand legend attached to her, only the vivid threat of the watching caterpillar — but that small, strange figure preserves a whole vanished world of garden-magic and the careful guarding of a summer crop.

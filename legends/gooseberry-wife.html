Gooseberry Wife

A great hairy caterpillar-spirit said to guard the gooseberry bushes of the Isle of Wight, seizing any child who came creeping among the fruit before it ripened. Some folklorists suspect a real caterpillar swarm lies behind the tale.

The Legend

The Gooseberry Wife is a nursery bogey of the Isle of Wight, conjured to keep children out of the fruit bushes before the gooseberries were ripe. She was said to take the form of a great hairy caterpillar, swollen and slow, who guarded the bushes and would seize any child who came creeping among them: 'if ye goos out in the gearden, the Gooseberry Wife'll be sure to ketch ye'.

The Otherworld at the Heart of the Tale

Like Peg Powler at the riverbank or the Awd Goggie among the orchards, she belongs to the class of 'nursery sprites' or 'cautionary spirits' that English country parents invoked to fence off danger or temptation. There is no grand legend attached to her, only the vivid threat of the watching caterpillar.

The cryptozoologist Karl Shuker has wondered whether a real sighting lies behind the character. He records a 1970s Essex report of a child confronted at close range by a caterpillar estimated at a foot long, and speculates that reports like it, or a procession of caterpillars mistaken for one huge creature, might be the seed such nursery bogeys grew from. Real or invented, the small strange figure preserves a whole vanished world of garden-magic and the careful guarding of a summer crop.

Grace O'Malley

The sea-captain Gráinne Ní Mháille ruled the shores of Clew Bay and negotiated with Elizabeth I as an equal. Rockfleet Castle, her stronghold on the tidewater, remains the place most closely bound to her legend.

The Legend

Grace O'Malley, Gráinne Ní Mháille, 'Granuaile' in Irish, was the sea-queen of sixteenth-century Connacht, chieftain of the seafaring O'Malleys of Clew Bay in County Mayo. From her castles along the Mayo coast, chief among them Rockfleet on the bay, she commanded a fleet of galleys, levied tolls on those who fished or sailed her waters, traded and raided the length of the western seaboard, and led her men in person, ruling by sea-power in a world that expected women to do neither.

The Sea-Captain of Clew Bay

The most famous episode of her legend is her meeting in 1593 with Queen Elizabeth I at Greenwich, where the two formidable women, of an age, are said to have negotiated as equals, Grace refusing to bow as a subject, since she did not recognise Elizabeth as Queen of Ireland, and conversing in Latin, their only shared tongue. Grace's own petition to Elizabeth, dated July 1593, survives today in the State Papers at the UK National Archives, alongside the eighteen 'Articles of Interrogatory' put to her on the Queen's behalf, giving this meeting an unusually direct documentary record for a figure so wrapped in legend. Around her gathered a wealth of folklore: that she gave birth at sea and fought off Barbary pirates the next hour; that she cursed the Earl of Howth and exacted a custom from his family still honoured for centuries. Half history and half legend, Granuaile is remembered simply as Ireland's Pirate Queen, and Rockfleet, the tower house from which she ruled Clew Bay, still stands on the shore today.

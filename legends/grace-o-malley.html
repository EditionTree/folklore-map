Grace O'Malley

The sea-captain Gráinne Ní Mháille ruled the shores of Clew Bay and negotiated with Elizabeth I as an equal. Rockfleet Castle, her stronghold on the tidewater, remains the place most closely bound to her legend.

A visual interpretation of Grace O'Malley leading her crew past Rockfleet Castle
The legend

Grace O'Malley — Gráinne Ní Mháille, 'Granuaile' — was the sea-queen of sixteenth-century Connacht, chieftain of the seafaring O'Malleys of Clew Bay in County Mayo and one of the most remarkable figures in Irish history and legend. From her castles along the Mayo coast, chief among them Rockfleet on the bay, she commanded a fleet of galleys, levied tolls on those who fished or sailed her waters, traded and raided the length of the western seaboard, and led her men in person — a woman ruling by sea-power in a man's world.

The sea-captain Gráinne Ní Mháille ruled the shores of Clew Bay and negotiated with Elizabeth I as an equal.

The sea-captain of Clew Bay

The most famous episode of her legend is her meeting in 1593 with Queen Elizabeth I at Greenwich, where the two formidable women, of an age, are said to have negotiated as equals — Grace refusing to bow as a subject, since she did not recognise Elizabeth as Queen of Ireland, and conversing in Latin, their only shared tongue. Around her gathered a wealth of folklore: that she gave birth at sea and fought off Barbary pirates the next hour; that she cursed the Earl of Howth and exacted a custom from his family still honoured for centuries. Half history and half legend, Granuaile endures as the great 'Pirate Queen' of Ireland and a lasting symbol of its defiance.

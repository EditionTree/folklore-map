Grey Man of Ben MacDhui

The Grey Man of Ben MacDhui is a presence reported by walkers on the Cairngorm summit: an unseen follower, an unnerving sound of footsteps or a towering grey figure in the mist. The legend is also known by the Gaelic name Am Fear Liath Mor.

The Legend

The Grey Man of Ben MacDhui — Am Fear Liath Mòr — is the brooding presence said to haunt the summit and high corries of Ben Macdui, the second-highest mountain in Britain, deep in the Cairngorms. He is rarely clearly seen; rather he is *felt*, and heard. Climbers on the lonely plateau have described the unmistakable sound of slow, heavy footsteps crunching behind them in the mist, always a stride or two for every one of their own, and an overwhelming, irrational terror that drives them to flee headlong down the mountain.

Footsteps in the Mist

The phenomenon was given lasting fame in 1925, when the respected climber and scientist Professor Norman Collie publicly described, for the first time, an experience from 1891: seized by panic near the cairn, he had run for miles before he could master himself, and had kept the story to himself for over thirty years before finally speaking of it at a Cairngorm Club dinner. His account prompted other climbers to come forward with similar experiences: a glimpse of a vast grey figure, or a tall shape in the cloud. Sceptics point to the 'Brocken spectre' (a climber's own shadow magnified on the mist) and to infrasound or simple mountain dread, but on Ben Macdui the Grey Man endures as Britain's most famous mountain haunting.

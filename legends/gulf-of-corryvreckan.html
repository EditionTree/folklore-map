Sacred Sites Between Jura and Scarba, Argyll

Gulf of Corryvreckan

One of the world's great whirlpools, feared in Gaelic tradition and tied to tales of sea trials, drowned princes, and the Cailleach washing her plaid in the roaring waters.

A visual interpretation of the Corryvreckan whirlpool off Jura and Scarba
The Legend

The Gulf of Corryvreckan, the narrow sea-strait between the islands of Jura and Scarba off the west coast of Scotland, is the home of one of the largest and most dangerous whirlpools in the world — a roaring maelstrom whose noise, it is said, can be heard for miles, and which Gaelic tradition wreathed in legend. Its very name is bound up with the tale of a prince, Breacan, and the whirlpool is often called 'Breacan's Cauldron'.

On the third night the rope of maidens' hair broke too, and Breacan was dragged down to his death.

Breacan's Cauldron

In the legend Prince Breacan, a Norse or Irish king's son, sought the hand of a local chief's daughter, and was set the deadly test of anchoring his vessel in the whirlpool for three days and three nights. On the advice of wise men he made three great ropes — one of hemp, one of wool, and one woven from the hair of pure maidens, believed strongest of all. On the first night the hemp rope parted, on the second the wool; on the third night the rope of maidens' hair broke too, and Breacan was dragged down to his death — for one of the maidens whose hair had gone into it had not been as chaste as she claimed, and the flaw undid him.

Older still is the legend of the Cailleach — the Cailleach Bheur, the hag-goddess of winter — who uses the gulf as her washtub. As autumn turns to winter she washes her great plaid in the roaring waters of the Corryvreckan, the building tempest heard for twenty miles around and lasting three days; and when she is done, the cloth is washed pure white and laid over the land as the first blanket of snow. Between the drowned prince and the winter-hag's washing, the great whirlpool gathers some of the most vivid sea-lore in the Gaelic world.

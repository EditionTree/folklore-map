Dragons Somerset

Gurt Worm

A great serpent said to have terrorised the villages of the Quantock Hills, coiling itself around fields and devouring cattle. A local hero eventually slew it, but not before the creature's death throes carved the valleys of the hills. Its memory persists in the landscape itself.

The legend

The Gurt Worm — 'great worm', in the Somerset dialect — is the dragon of the Quantock Hills, a vast serpent said to have coiled itself about the high ground above the villages of Shervage Wood and Bicknoller, devouring sheep, cattle and the occasional unlucky traveller. So large was it that folk mistook its body for a fallen tree-trunk lying across the path.

The story

In the best-loved telling a woodman from Stogumber, sent up to cut wood in Shervage Wood, sat down to eat his bread and cheese on what he took for a log — until it began to writhe beneath him. Rather than flee, the unflappable countryman set about the worm with his axe, cutting it in two; the halves wriggled off in opposite directions, one toward Bilbrook and one toward Kingston, and being unable to rejoin, the Gurt Worm died. The tale is a fine example of the homely West-Country dragon, dispatched not by a knight but by an ordinary working man with a sharp tool and a steady nerve.

Legendary Figures Warwick and Guy's Cliffe, Warwickshire, England

Guy of Warwick

Guy of Warwick is the great hero of Warwickshire romance, credited with slaying the Dun Cow and defeating the Danish champion Colbrand. Tradition says he ended his life as a hermit at Guy's Cliffe beside the River Avon.

A visual interpretation of Guy of Warwick at Guy's Cliffe
The Legend

Guy of Warwick is the great hero of English medieval romance attached to Warwickshire, a knight whose legend swelled through the Middle Ages into one of the most popular tales in the land. Of humble birth — a steward's son — Guy won renown and the hand of the noble Felice through a string of marvellous feats: he slew the monstrous Dun Cow, a giant beast that ravaged the country; he killed a fearsome dragon in Northumberland; and, most celebrated of all, he met the gigantic Danish champion Colbrand in single combat at Winchester and felled him, delivering England from Danish tribute.

he ended his life as a hermit at Guy's Cliffe

The Hero Becomes a Hermit

At the height of his fame Guy renounced it all. Stricken with remorse, he made a pilgrimage to the Holy Land, returned to England unrecognised, and lived out his last days as a hermit in a cave at Guy's Cliffe near Warwick, his identity revealed to his wife Felice only as he lay dying. The cave and chapel at Guy's Cliffe, and a great rib long displayed as the Dun Cow's, kept his legend rooted in the Warwickshire landscape for centuries, a hero who won every glory chivalry could offer and then gave it up for God.

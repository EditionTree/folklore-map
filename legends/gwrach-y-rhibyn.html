Ghosts Ceredigion, Wales

Gwrach-y-Rhibyn

GOO-rakh uh RHEE-bin

Wales's own banshee — a hideous hag with leathery wings who shrieks at crossroads to foretell the death of those with Welsh blood. Her wail carries across the hills on still nights.

The Legend

A monstrously ugly hag with tangled hair, long black teeth, withered arms and great leathery, bat-like wings, the Gwrach-y-Rhibyn comes by night to those of true Welsh blood to warn that one of the family is about to die. Her name means roughly 'the Hag of the Mist' or 'Witch of the Rhibyn', and she is the Welsh counterpart of the Irish banshee, a hideous female death-spirit whose appearance or cry foretells a coming death.

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

Her approach is heralded above all by her terrible voice. The Gwrach-y-Rhibyn is heard wailing and shrieking in the darkness — flapping unseen against the window of the doomed person's house, or crying along the streams and crossroads — uttering a blood-chilling lament, often said to call out the name of the one who is to die, or to cry in Welsh for her husband or her child as a token of the bereavement to come. To hear her keening on the night wind, especially before a death in the family, was a sign that could not be mistaken.

In some traditions she is encountered at a misty crossroads or by water, and to glimpse her hideous face is itself an omen. The Gwrach-y-Rhibyn belongs to the wider Celtic family of foretelling death-spirits, alongside the Irish banshee and the Scottish bean nighe, and like them she embodies the old belief that death announces itself, that certain ancient families had their own attendant spirit of doom, and that the wild, lonely places — the mist, the stream, the crossroads — were where the living might meet the heralds of the dead.

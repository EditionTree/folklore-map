Gwyllgi

GWITH-lgee

The Gwyllgi, the Dog of Darkness, is a great phantom black hound of Welsh folklore with blazing eyes and a shaggy pelt. Unlike the spectral pack of the Cŵn Annwn, it is a solitary creature of roads and hedgerows. A local tradition places it in the Nant y Garth Pass in the Clwydian Hills.

A visual interpretation of the Gwyllgi, the Dog of Darkness
The Legend

The Gwyllgi — the 'Dog of Darkness' — is the great phantom black hound of Welsh folklore, a mastiff-like apparition with a shaggy coat and burning red eyes whose appearance on a lonely road froze travellers with dread. It was particularly associated with the Nant y Garth pass in Denbighshire and with other dark lanes and crossroads, where it would pad out of the night to pace beside the lone walker.

The Gwyllgi is a solitary creature of roads and hedgerows.

The Dog on the Dark Road

Unlike some spectral dogs that merely escort the traveller home, the Gwyllgi was a creature of pure terror, its breath foul and its presence an evil omen; horses shied and bolted at it, and to meet its gaze was to feel the marrow chill. It belongs to the great British and Irish family of black-dog hauntings — kin to the Norfolk Black Shuck and the Manx Moddey Dhoo — in which the night-roaming hound stands as guardian of the boundary between the living and the dead.

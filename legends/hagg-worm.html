Hagg Worm

A venomous serpent of the North Yorkshire moors, lurking in the deep wooded gullies called haggs. One of several worms said to have plagued the region before the age of saints and knights, giving the landscape both its name and its dread.

A visual interpretation of the Hagg Worm in its moorland lair
The Legend

The Hagg Worm is a venomous serpent-dragon of the North York Moors, its name taken from the 'haggs' — the deep, wooded, boggy gullies that score the high moorland, where such a creature was said to lair. Like the other 'worms' of northern England, it was imagined as a great wingless wyrm, swollen and poisonous, that crept out from its hag to prey on livestock and to poison the ground and water around it.

Something old and dangerous might still be coiled in the wild, sucking bogs of the haggs.

The Wyrm in the Hagg

The worms of Yorkshire and the north-east form a distinct family of dragon-lore — the Lambton Worm, the Sockburn Worm, the Loschy Hill dragon among them — venomous, self-healing serpents rather than the fire-breathing, winged dragons of heraldry, and almost always tied to a specific wood, hill or stretch of river. The Hagg Worm belongs to this native northern tradition, a reminder that to the moorland communities the wild, sucking bogs of the haggs were places where something old and dangerous might still be coiled.

Hartlepool Monkey

During the Napoleonic Wars, Hartlepool fishermen found a monkey from a shipwreck dressed in a French uniform, tried it on the beach for espionage, and hanged it. The town has never been allowed to forget — its football mascot is H’Angus the Monkey.

A visual interpretation of the Hartlepool monkey legend on the County Durham coast
The legend

The legend of the Hartlepool monkey is one of the most enduring and self-aware pieces of folklore in northern England. The story, set during the Napoleonic Wars of the early nineteenth century, tells of a French vessel that was wrecked off the coast of Hartlepool in County Durham. The only survivor to wash ashore was the ship’s pet monkey, dressed in a miniature military uniform — a common practice aboard warships of the period, where monkeys and other animals served as mascots and were sometimes costumed for the amusement of the crew.

The monkey, unable to answer their questions in English, was convicted of espionage and hanged from the mast of the wrecked ship.

The trial on the beach

The fishermen of Hartlepool, who had never seen a Frenchman and whose understanding of the enemy was shaped by satirical cartoons depicting the French as small, gibbering, monkey-like creatures, are said to have convened an impromptu court on the beach. The monkey, unable to answer their questions in English, was convicted of espionage and hanged from the mast of the wrecked ship. The tale may carry a darker undertone: some historians have suggested that the ‘monkey’ was in fact a powder-monkey — the term for the young boys employed on warships to carry gunpowder to the cannons — and that the hanging was of a child rather than an animal.

No contemporary record of the event has been found. The first known version of the story appears in a comic song by Ned Corvan, a Tyneside music-hall performer, in the 1850s, and Historic UK found no evidence of French shipwrecks off Hartlepool during the Napoleonic period. What makes the legend remarkable is not its historical accuracy but its afterlife: rather than burying the story, Hartlepool embraced it. The town’s football club adopted H’Angus the Monkey as its mascot, and in 2002 the man inside the suit, Stuart Drummond, stood for mayor of Hartlepool on a joke ticket and won — serving three terms. A bronze statue of the monkey now stands in the town’s marina. The legend has become an act of civic self-identification, a joke the town tells on itself with enough warmth to disarm the mockery.

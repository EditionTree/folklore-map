Henderson Stone

A granite boulder in Glencoe steeped in the legend of a Campbell soldier who warned of the 1692 massacre by speaking his warning to the stone rather than break orders directly.

A visual interpretation of the Henderson Stone legend in Glencoe
The Legend

The Henderson Stone — Clach Eanruig in Gaelic — sits in a field south of Carnoch in the Glencoe valley, an unassuming granite boulder carrying one of the Highlands’ most poignant legends. In February 1692, soldiers of the Earl of Argyll’s Regiment of Foot, mostly Campbells, were billeted with the MacDonalds of Glencoe under the pretence of hospitality. After ten days of sharing food and shelter, orders came to put every MacDonald under seventy to the sword before dawn on 13 February.

Great stone of the glen! Great is your right to be here. But if you knew what will happen this night you would be up and away.

The Warning by the Stone

According to oral tradition preserved for over 150 years, at least one Campbell soldier found the order morally unbearable. Unable to warn his host directly without disobeying orders, the soldier walked with a local man past the stone and spoke his warning to it instead: ‘Great stone of the glen! Great is your right to be here. But if you knew what will happen this night you would be up and away.’ The words, addressed to a boulder rather than a person, gave the MacDonald just enough warning to grasp the danger and flee into the winter darkness.

Clan Henderson tradition holds that the stone marks the memory of the MacEanruig or Clann Eanruig, a Gaelic-speaking branch who held authority in Glencoe from the early 11th century before displacement by the MacDonalds. Historians debate whether the folklore linking the stone to Clan Henderson’s pipers is ancient tradition or 19th-century elaboration. Either way, the boulder endures as a powerful symbol of conscience tested against obedience.

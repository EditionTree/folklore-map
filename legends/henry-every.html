Henry Every

Born near Plymouth, the elusive Henry Every became the Arch Pirate after taking an immense Mughal treasure ship in 1695. A worldwide hunt followed, but he vanished without a proven end.

The Legend

Henry Every, also called Avery, and nicknamed 'Long Ben', was born around 1659 at Newton Ferrers in Devon, and for a brief moment was the most wanted man in the world, the pirate who pulled off what was called the richest single heist in history before vanishing without trace. After a career in the Royal Navy and the slave trade, in 1694 he led a mutiny aboard the warship Charles II at A Coruña, seized the ship, renamed her the Fancy, and was elected captain by the mutineers, turning pirate in the Indian Ocean.

The Figure Behind the Legend

In 1695 he won his place in legend. Learning of a great Mughal treasure fleet returning from the pilgrimage to Mecca, Every gathered a flotilla of pirate ships and ran down its mightiest vessel, the Ganj-i-Sawai, belonging to the Emperor Aurangzeb himself. After a fierce battle the pirates took the ship and an almost unimaginable fortune in gold, silver and jewels, but also committed atrocities against the passengers so terrible that they sparked a diplomatic crisis, threatening the East India Company's whole position in India and prompting one of the first global manhunts, with a huge bounty placed on Every's head. Contemporary East India Company correspondence and depositions document the capture and the resulting crisis within months of the event; Captain Charles Johnson's 'A General History of the Pyrates' (1724) gave Every his earliest substantial narrative biography a generation later.

Then he disappeared. Every and his men split the plunder and scattered; some were caught and hanged, but Every himself slipped away and was never found. Legends multiplied, that he had set himself up as a king on a pirate island, that he lived in luxury under a false name, or, in the most ironic tale, that he was cheated of his fortune by Bristol merchants and died penniless and unknown in his native Devon. His clean escape with a king's ransom made him the great romantic pirate of the popular imagination, celebrated in ballad and story for generations.

Henry Morgan

The Welsh privateer Henry Morgan became one of the most famous raiders of the Caribbean. His early life is uncertain, but tradition places his birth around Llanrumney or nearby Pencarn. Later retellings turned the historical governor and plantation owner into a larger-than-life pirate figure.

A visual interpretation of Henry Morgan planning a Caribbean voyage
The Legend

Sir Henry Morgan (c.1635–1688) was the Welsh privateer who rose to be the most feared and successful raider of the Spanish Caribbean, and around whom a great deal of buccaneering legend gathered. Tradition places his birth at Llanrumney near Cardiff, and his early passage to the West Indies is shrouded in tales of kidnap and indenture; what is certain is that under commission from Jamaica he led devastating raids on Spanish strongholds.

Later retellings turned the historical governor and plantation owner into a larger-than-life pirate figure.

The Privateer Who Became a Pirate Legend

His storming and sack of Panama in 1671 — marching his men across the isthmus to burn the richest city of the Spanish Main — made his name ring through Europe. Far from being hanged, Morgan was knighted and made Lieutenant-Governor of Jamaica, dying respectable and wealthy. Yet popular legend, and a century of pirate fiction, recast the privateer as the archetypal buccaneer captain, blurring the careful line he himself drew between licensed privateer and outright pirate.

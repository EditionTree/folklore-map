Sacred Sites County Meath, Ireland

Hill of Tara

At the Hill of Tara in County Meath — spiritual heart of Ireland — the Lia Fáil, the Stone of Destiny, cried out beneath any man rightfully born to be High King; beneath the hill the Tuatha Dé Danann withdrew into their great mound, and the Banquet Hall was built large enough to seat the heroes of the entire island.

The Legend

The Hill of Tara (Irish: Teamhair na Rí, 'Tara of the Kings') in County Meath is a low but commanding ridge rising above the central plain of Ireland, visible for many miles in all directions. In the mythological tradition it served as the sacred seat of the High Kings of Ireland, and its landscape is layered with monuments spanning Neolithic, Bronze Age, Iron Age, and early medieval occupation — many of them identified with specific mythological events and figures.

Tara was remembered as the seat of Ireland's high kings and the speaking stone of sovereignty.

The Royal Hill

The most famous object on Tara is the Lia Fáil, the Stone of Destiny, a standing stone near the mound of the inauguration. According to tradition — recorded in the eleventh-century Lebor Gabála Érenn — the Lia Fáil was one of the four treasures brought by the Tuatha Dé Danann from their northern cities of learning. Its property was simple and spectacular: it screamed aloud when the rightful High King stepped on or touched it. The inauguration of kings at Tara involved the Feis Temro, a great feast at which the stone's silence or cry confirmed or denied legitimacy. The stone still stands on the hill — though some traditions hold that the real Lia Fáil was taken to Scotland as the Stone of Scone, and the standing stone at Tara is a later substitute.

The Mound of the Hostages (Dumha na nGiall) is the oldest surviving structure on Tara, a Neolithic passage tomb dated to around 3200 BC, contemporary with Newgrange. The Banquet Hall (Tech Midchuarta) was an enormous building recorded in the Dindshenchas as seating the entire island's population during the Feis Temro, with specific sections assigned to warriors, poets, physicians, clergy, and craftsmen by rank. Tara's cultural authority persists in Daniel O'Connell's choice of it as the site of the 1843 'monster meeting' attended by over a million people — the largest political gathering in Irish history — where the ancient seat of the High Kings was summoned in the cause of Catholic Emancipation.

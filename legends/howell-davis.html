Howell Davis

The Milford Haven pirate Howell Davis relied on disguise and deception as readily as cannon fire. His short career lasted less than a year, yet he captured fifteen known ships before an ambush ended it.

The Legend

Howell Davis was a Welsh pirate of the early 18th century, born around 1690 at Milford Haven in Pembrokeshire, whose short but spectacular career was built on cunning and deception rather than brute force. He turned pirate after the ship he served on was captured by Edward England, and quickly made a name as one of the boldest and most ingenious captains of his day. Commanding the Royal Rover, he preyed on shipping in the Cape Verde Islands and along the West African coast, and for a time joined forces with the French pirate Olivier Levasseur.

The Figure Behind the Legend

Davis's genius was the confidence trick. Rather than fight, he loved to talk and bluff his way to a prize, most famously by sailing into port flying false colours and passing himself and his men off as legitimate traders, or even as Royal Navy pirate-hunters, to be welcomed by the very authorities he meant to rob. By such ruses he took the fort and slave-castle at Anomabu and a string of rich prizes. It was among his captures, off the Gold Coast, that he took a fellow Welshman named Bartholomew Roberts, whom he kept aboard — the two conversing privately in Welsh — and who would soon eclipse him utterly.

His luck ran out on the Portuguese island of Príncipe in 1719. Attempting his favourite trick once too often, Davis schemed to lure the island's governor aboard to be held for ransom; but the governor had been tipped off, and when Davis came ashore under a flag of friendship to take wine at the fort, the Portuguese sprang an ambush and shot him down. His crew then chose Roberts to succeed him, and the apprentice went on to become the most feared pirate of the age — Davis's true legacy.

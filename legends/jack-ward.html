Pirates Faversham, Kent

Jack Ward

The Faversham-born corsair Jack Ward became England's notorious Arch-Pirate. Plays, pamphlets and ballads followed him in his own lifetime. The traditional song Captain Ward and the Rainbow casts him as a king of the sea who defeats the royal ship sent against him.

The legend

John Ward of Faversham in Kent — 'Jack Ward', later known by his Muslim name Yusuf Reis — rose from a poor fisherman to become the most notorious English pirate of the early seventeenth century, the 'Arch-Pirate' of his day. Deserting the navy, he gathered a crew, seized a ship, and made his way to the Barbary Coast, basing himself at Tunis from where he preyed on the rich shipping of the Mediterranean.

The story

Converting to Islam and amassing a fortune, Ward became a figure of fascination and scandal in England, his exploits pursued through pamphlets, ballads and a popular stage play while he himself lived beyond the reach of English law. He died, probably of plague, around 1622 in his Tunis palace. As one of the models for the romantic image of the renegade corsair, Jack Ward helped shape the very idea of the pirate that later centuries would inherit.

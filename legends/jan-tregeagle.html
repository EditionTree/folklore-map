Ghosts Cornwall

Jan Tregeagle

A corrupt magistrate in life, Jan Tregeagle was condemned after death to perform impossible tasks for eternity lest demons drag him away. He haunts Dozmary Pool on Bodmin Moor, endlessly bailing its waters with a leaking limpet shell. When storms rage across the moor, the howling is said to be Tregeagle fleeing the hounds of hell.

The Legend

Jan Tregeagle is the great damned soul of Cornish legend, a corrupt seventeenth-century steward to Baron Robartes remembered for fraud, cruelty and worse against the tenants under his charge. Long after his burial at St Breoke church, his spirit was summoned back from the grave to give evidence in a Bodmin courtroom over an unpaid rent, and once raised he could not be returned to rest. To keep his howling ghost from the world, the clergy bound him to a chain of impossible, eternal tasks meant to occupy him until Judgement Day.

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

He was condemned first to empty bottomless Dozmary Pool on Bodmin Moor with a limpet shell pierced full of holes. Fleeing the demons sent to guard him, he found brief sanctuary in St Gonand's chapel on Roche Rock, but was driven out again and set to weave ropes of sand at the mouth of the River Camel near Padstow, labours forever undone by tide and wind. Sent next to the Cober estuary below Helston, he was made to carry sacks of sand across the river, and during one great storm he is said to have dropped a sack so heavy it built the sandbar now called Loe Bar, sealing off Loe Pool, Cornwall's largest natural lake, in the process. His final and still-unfinished task set him sweeping sand from Porthcurno round the headland to Nanjizal Cove, near Land's End. On wild nights his roaring is still said to be heard across the moors and cliffs as the Devil's hounds pursue him from one task to the next. Robert Hunt collected the tale in his 'Popular Romances of the West of England' (1865), the earliest full account of Cornwall's most punished ghost.

Kelpie of Loch Garve

The kelpie of Loch Garve fell in love with his captive and built her an underwater fireplace, and to this day one stretch of the loch never freezes, said to be warmed by the chimney below.

The Legend

A young woman walking the shores of Loch Garve in Ross-shire was drawn to an extraordinarily beautiful stallion standing at the water's edge. She approached, mounted it, and the horse plunged beneath the surface, transforming as it descended into its true each-uisge form. Unlike most water-horse legends, which end in drowning and consumption, this one takes a stranger turn. The creature had genuinely fallen in love, and using his power he let her breathe underwater and carried her to his realm at the loch's bottom.

The Water Legend at the Heart of the Tale

As time passed, the woman grew miserable with the cold and the dark. The kelpie, determined to warm her home, sought out the finest stonemason in the Highlands, transformed into a horse, and confronted him. Rather than terrorising the man, he proposed a bargain: build a fireplace and chimney beneath the loch, and his life would be spared. The mason obliged, carving sculpted arches, ornate pillars, and a chimney shaft that rose to just below the surface. Both parties honoured the agreement.

The legend offers a kind of proof that can still be verified. There is a section of Loch Garve, well-known locally, that does not freeze over even in the sharpest Highland winters, while the surrounding water ices normally, attributed to the heat rising from the kelpie's chimney far below. The tale stands apart in the Scottish water-horse tradition. Most such stories warn against approaching strange horses at the water's edge; this one ends in desire, bargain, and a love that left a permanent mark on the physical landscape.

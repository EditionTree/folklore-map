Kilkenny Witch Trials

The Kilkenny witch trials of 1324 centred on accusations against the wealthy Alice Kyteler and eleven associates. Kyteler escaped, but Petronilla de Meath was tortured and burned at the stake for alleged witchcraft and heresy. The case remains a dark landmark in Irish history.

A visual interpretation of the 1324 Kilkenny witch trials
The Legend

The Kilkenny proceedings of 1324 are among the earliest and most notorious witch trials in the British Isles, centred on the wealthy Hiberno-Norman noblewoman Alice Kyteler. After the deaths of several husbands left her rich, her stepchildren accused her before Richard de Ledrede, the zealous Bishop of Ossory, of heresy and sorcery — of holding nightly meetings, sacrificing to a demon familiar named Robin Artisson, and concocting potions from gruesome ingredients.

Alice Kyteler escaped the trial, but Petronilla de Meath was left to face its terrible force.

The Charge in Kilkenny

Kyteler, well connected and protected by powerful kin, evaded the bishop and fled, probably to England, never to be punished. Her maidservant Petronilla de Meath was not so fortunate: tortured into confession, she was flogged and burned at the stake in Kilkenny on 3 November 1324 — the first person known to have been burned for heresy in Ireland. The case marked the importation into Ireland of the new continental idea of diabolical witchcraft, decades ahead of the great witch-hunts elsewhere.

Kirk Yetholm, Seat of the Gypsy Kings

Kirk Yetholm, on the Scottish Borders, was the traditional seat of the Romani 'Gypsy Kings' — the Faa dynasty — for centuries, culminating in the crowning of the last Gypsy Queen, Esther Faa Blyth, in 1898.

The Legend

Kirk Yetholm is a small village in the Scottish Borders, tucked beneath the Cheviot Hills at the end of the Pennine Way, and for some three centuries it held a unique and romantic status as the traditional capital of the Scottish Gypsies — the seat of the 'Gypsy Kings and Queens' of the Faa (or Faa-Blythe) dynasty. From at least the 17th century the Romani people of the Borders made their winter home here, and the village became bound up with their royalty.

For centuries Kirk Yetholm was remembered as the seat of the Romani rulers of the Borders.

The Border Seat

The chiefs of the Border Gypsies, the Faas, were styled kings and queens, and their accession was marked with real ceremony — coronations held in the village that drew great crowds of onlookers. A particular cottage in Kirk Yetholm became known as the 'Gypsy Palace', the residence of this dynasty. The line and its customs were celebrated in the 19th century, romanticised in literature, and woven into the lore of the Borders, where the Faas were figures of fascination, half-feared, half-admired.

The tradition reached its famous close in 1898 with the coronation of the last of the line, Esther Faa Blythe, crowned Queen of the Gypsies at Kirk Yetholm in a much-reported ceremony, after which the old royalty faded into history. The 'Gypsy Palace' cottage still stands in the village today, keeping alive the memory of Kirk Yetholm as the crowning-place of the Border Gypsy kings.

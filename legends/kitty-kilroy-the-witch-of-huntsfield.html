Kitty Kilroy, the Witch of Huntsfield

A priest's horse was said to collapse paralysed on the Huntsfield road, and the villagers who watched dared suggest fetching the local witch to cure it.

The Legend

In the townland of Huntsfield near Kilkelly, County Mayo, local memory preserved the story of a woman named Kitty Kilroy who was reputed to practise witchcraft, roughly a generation before the account was written down. What marked her out as much as any spell was her absence from the parish's religious life: she was said never to attend Mass or take the Sacraments, a defiance that drew open hostility from the local priests.

The Story

The one incident remembered in detail concerns a priest travelling the Huntsfield road on horseback, whose horse suddenly "got paralized" and collapsed beneath him. As villagers gathered to help, someone in the crowd suggested sending for the witch, since her reputed power might be the only thing able to undo whatever had struck the animal down. The suggestion itself was recorded as a scandal, "a great insult to the priest," caught between the parish's official religious authority and an informal, grudging belief in Kitty Kilroy's own power that the priest's own flock could not quite shake off.

The account comes from the National Folklore Collection's Schools' Collection, gathered in the 1930s when Irish schoolchildren were sent to record local tradition from older relatives and neighbours. This version was collected by Annie M. Dunleavy from Mrs Patrick Boyle of nearby Urlaur. Like most Schools' Collection witch stories, it preserves a community's reputation and rumour about a named local figure rather than any court record or formal accusation, and should be read as a piece of living oral folklore rather than documented history.

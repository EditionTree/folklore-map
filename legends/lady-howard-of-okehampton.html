Ghosts Okehampton, Devon, England

Lady Howard of Okehampton

Devon tradition condemns Lady Mary Howard to ride each night from Tavistock to Okehampton Castle in a carriage made from the bones of her husbands. A black dog runs before the carriage and plucks one blade of grass from the castle mound on arrival. Only when every blade is gone will the haunting end.

A visual interpretation of Lady Howard of Okehampton
The Legend

Devon tradition turned Lady Mary Howard (1596–1671), a much-married heiress of Fitzford near Tavistock, into a damned and restless ghost. Rumour blamed her for the deaths of husbands and even a child, and the legend condemns her to do nightly penance: each night she must ride from the gates of Fitzford to Okehampton Castle and back in a coach made of the bones of her dead husbands, drawn by headless horses and driven by a headless coachman.

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

Before the coach runs a great black hound with one glowing eye, and at Okehampton the dog must pluck a single blade of grass and carry it back to Fitzford. The penance will last, it is said, until every blade of grass on Okehampton Castle green has been taken — that is, until the end of the world. The grim image of the bone-coach was fixed in the popular mind by a Victorian ballad, and remains one of the best-known of all Dartmoor's spectral legends.

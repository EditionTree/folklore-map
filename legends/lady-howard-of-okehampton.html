Ghosts Okehampton, Devon, England

Lady Howard of Okehampton

Devon tradition condemns Lady Mary Howard to ride each night from Tavistock to Okehampton Castle in a carriage made from the bones of her husbands. A black dog runs before the carriage and plucks one blade of grass from the castle mound on arrival. Only when every blade is gone will the haunting end.

The Legend

Devon tradition turned Lady Mary Howard (1596-1671), a much-married heiress of Fitzford near Tavistock, into a damned and restless ghost. Rumour blamed her for the deaths of husbands and even a child, and the legend condemns her to nightly penance: each night she must ride from the gates of Fitzford to Okehampton Castle and back in a coach made of the bones of her dead husbands, drawn by headless horses and driven by a headless coachman.

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

Before the coach runs a great black hound with one glowing eye, and at Okehampton the dog must pluck a single blade of grass and carry it back to Fitzford. The penance will last, it is said, until every blade of grass on Okehampton Castle green has been taken, that is, until the end of the world.

The woman behind the legend was real, and her actual life gives the story an odder shape than the ghost tale suggests. Born Mary Fitz, daughter of the volatile John Fitz, she outlived four husbands, the last of them Sir Richard Grenville, who married her in 1629, treated her violently, and was jailed after the couple separated in 1631. She died at Fitzford on 17 October 1671, aged seventy-five. Later tellers seem to have folded her own father's crimes, and those of an unrelated Jacobean noblewoman also called Lady Frances Howard, into Mary's reputation, turning a woman who had simply survived four marriages into a supposed murderer of husbands.

The grim image of the bone coach was fixed in the popular mind by a ballad Sabine Baring-Gould printed in Songs of the West in 1891, built from fragments of an older song few by then could recall in full, and the tale is still told on Dartmoor of the coach that runs between Fitzford and the castle green.

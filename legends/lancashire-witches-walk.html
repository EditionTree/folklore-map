Lancashire Witches Walk

A 51-mile footpath retraces the route the Pendle witches were marched to their 1612 trial, from Barrowford beneath Pendle Hill to the gallows at Lancaster Castle — ten cast-iron waymarkers along the way carry lines from a poem by Carol Ann Duffy.

The Legend

The Lancashire Witches Walk is a 51-mile (82 km) long-distance footpath through Lancashire, opened in 2012 to mark the 400th anniversary of the most notorious witch trials in English history — the trial and hanging of the Pendle witches in 1612. The route runs from Barrowford, in the shadow of Pendle Hill where the accused lived, to Lancaster Castle, where ten of them were tried and condemned, and it deliberately traces the most likely path along which the prisoners were marched the long miles across the county to their trial and execution.

The modern route turns a landscape of accusation into a long act of remembrance.

The Path from Pendle

The trials it commemorates were a grim drama of poverty, family feud and accusation among the cottagers of the Pendle district. Two rival families, headed by the old women remembered as Demdike and Chattox, were caught up in a spiral of charges of bewitchment and witchcraft; under questioning a nine-year-old girl, Jennet Device, gave evidence that helped to hang her own mother, grandmother and neighbours. In all, ten people were executed on Gallows Hill at Lancaster in August 1612, making the Pendle case one of the largest and best-recorded witch trials in English history.

The modern walk turns that dark history into an act of memory and landscape. Along the route stand ten cast-iron waymarkers, each set with a tercet from a specially commissioned poem by the then Poet Laureate Carol Ann Duffy, so that walkers retracing the condemned women's last journey pass, marker by marker, through the verses of their remembrance. The trail binds the folklore of the Lancashire witches to the very ground they walked, linking Pendle Hill, the bleak moorland miles, and the castle where their story ended.

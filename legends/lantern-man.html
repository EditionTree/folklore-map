Lantern Man

A malevolent will-o-the-wisp of the Norfolk Broads, more dangerous than most, it actively pursues lone travellers across the marshes. Whistling or swearing at it only makes it angrier.

The Legend

The Lantern Man is the will-o'-the-wisp of the Norfolk Broads and fens, and in local belief a far more dangerous spirit than the merely misleading marsh-lights of other regions. Seen as a bobbing flame moving over the reed-beds and waterways at night, the Lantern Man was said not just to lead travellers astray but actively to pursue and hunt down anyone abroad on the marsh after dark, bent on drowning them in the dykes or smothering them in the mire.

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

Fenland wisdom held strict rules for surviving an encounter: never whistle or carry a light, for these drew him; if he gave chase, throw yourself flat on your face and hold your breath until he passed over, for he could not abide a thing that made no sound and showed no flame.

The folklorist Jennifer Westwood distinguished the Norfolk Lantern Man from the gentler will-o'-the-wisps of other counties precisely on these grounds, calling him full-size, physically aggressive and genuinely dangerous rather than merely mischievous. Named victims cling to the legend still: Joseph Bexfield was said to have been lured to his death by the marsh lights, and the Lantern Man of Alderfen Broad in particular had a reputation for terrorising travellers on that stretch of water. Science offers its own quiet explanation, cold flames produced by the oxidation of hydrogen phosphide and methane escaping from decaying vegetation in the marsh, but it does little to dim the dread the lights once carried.

The Lantern Man embodies the real lethal danger of the night marsh, the bog-gas flames, the hidden water, the ease of losing the path, concentrated into a single malevolent pursuer.

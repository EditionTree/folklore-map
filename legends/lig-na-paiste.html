Lig-na-Paiste

LIG-na-PAHSH-tyuh

The last serpent to escape St Patrick's purge terrorised the Sperrins until a local saint caged it beneath Lough Foyle, where it still churns the tides.

The Legend

While St Patrick is credited with banishing every serpent from Ireland, one is said to have escaped the purge. Lig-na-Paiste, 'the last of the serpents', survived undetected in a deep pool of the river at Banagher, County Derry, on the edge of the Sperrin Mountains. From its lair the great serpent laid waste to the surrounding countryside, terrorising communities from the slopes of the Sperrins to the shores of Lough Foyle.

The Caged Last Serpent

The people eventually turned to Murrough O'Heaney, a local holy man whose church stood in Banagher Glen. St Murrough fasted for ten days and nights, praying for the power to confront the beast. Armed with three rods fashioned from river reeds and a divine knowledge of how to trap the creature, he descended to the serpent's pool. Though Lig-na-Paiste begged for mercy, the saint refused it, declaring that the creature had sinned against God's people, and bound it in a cage forged by divine power before casting it into the depths of Lough Foyle.

The ruins of Banagher Old Church, where St Murrough worshipped, still stand today within Banagher Glen, now a National Nature Reserve of ancient oak woodland managed for the rare species its tree cover supports. Local tradition holds that Paiste remains coiled beneath Lough Foyle to this day, and that the lough's unusual currents and unpredictable tides are caused by the serpent's endless struggle to break free.

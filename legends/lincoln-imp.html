Lincoln Imp

Sent by the Devil to wreak havoc, two imps were blown to Lincoln — one flew straight into the cathedral, taunted the angels of the Angel Choir, and was turned to stone where it still grins down today.

A visual interpretation of the Lincoln Imp in the Angel Choir
The Legend

According to a tale told in Lincoln for generations, the Devil once sent a host of imps out into the world to cause mischief, and the wind carried two of them to the city of Lincoln. At first the pair were so awed by the size and splendour of the newly built cathedral that they hung back, afraid to go in. Eventually one grew bold enough to fly inside, where he set about his work with relish — tripping up the Bishop, knocking down the Dean, and pulling faces at the choir as they sang.

The angels duly did - turning the imp instantly to stone and fixing him forever in the cleft of a column high on the north side of the choir.

The Imp in the Choir

When the imp began smashing the cathedral's stained glass, a band of angels carved into the newly finished Angel Choir told him to stop. His reply was a single defiant taunt: 'Stop me if you can!' The angels duly did — turning the imp instantly to stone and fixing him forever in the cleft of a column high on the north side of the choir, where, the story goes, his fellow imp escaped and was last seen riding away on the back of a witch.

The Angel Choir was built between about 1250 and 1280, and the carving the legend explains — a grinning, horned, cross-legged figure barely a foot tall — has been there ever since. Whatever its original purpose among the choir's many grotesques, the Lincoln Imp went on to become the unofficial emblem of the city itself, reproduced on everything from regimental badges to jewellery and souvenirs, and is now probably the best-known carving in the cathedral.

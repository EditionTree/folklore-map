Sacred Sites Carrowmore, County Sligo

Listoghil

The great central tomb of Carrowmore, aligned on sunrise at Samhain and Imbolc, whose entrance stone bears a footprint-like carving echoing the coronation rituals of ancient Irish kings.

A visual interpretation of Listoghil at Carrowmore
The Legend

Listoghil stands at the heart of the Carrowmore complex in County Sligo, the largest of some thirty-five Neolithic monuments scattered across the Cúil Irra peninsula. Its cairn, thirty-four metres in diameter, was erected around 3500 BC and recently restored to something approaching its original profile — a great mound dominating a ritual landscape that stretches from the peninsula to the cairn-crowned summit of Knocknarea, where Queen Maeve is said to lie buried upright with her spear facing Ulster.

The central tomb of Carrowmore turns toward the seasonal sunrise and echoes old rites of sovereignty.

The Sunrise Tomb

The monument’s entrance kerbstone carries a feature that has drawn particular attention: a depression resembling a human footprint, echoing the tradition of coronation stones in Irish mythology. At Tara, the Lia Fáil was said to scream when the rightful king placed his foot upon it. Medieval texts describe inauguration rituals in which kings symbolically married the sovereignty goddess Maeve on ancient mounds, renewing a sacred bond between ruler, land, and the divine feminine. Whether Listoghil served a similar ceremonial function remains unproven, but its alignment on sunrise at Samhain and Imbolc — two of the most potent dates in the Celtic calendar — places it firmly within a world where the movements of the sun, the turning of the seasons, and the authority of kingship were deeply intertwined.

Though the surrounding district is steeped in legend — stories of druids, fairy hosts, and the great cattle raid of Cooley swirl around the Sligo landscape — Listoghil itself has never been satisfactorily identified with a single named myth. It belongs instead to a deeper, older layer of tradition: a monument whose builders encoded their beliefs in stone and alignment rather than in words that survived into the age of writing.

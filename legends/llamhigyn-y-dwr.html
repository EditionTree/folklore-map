Llamhigyn y Dŵr

The Llamhigyn y Dŵr, or Water Leaper, is a Welsh aquatic creature described as a monstrous winged toad with a long tail. Folklore associates it with attacks on livestock and fishermen, including stories around Llyn Gwynant.

The Legend

The Llamhigyn y Dŵr — the 'Water Leaper' — is a strange aquatic monster of Welsh folklore, particularly feared by the fishermen of the mountain lakes and rivers of Gwynedd, with tales attached to waters such as Llyn Gwynant in the heart of Snowdonia. It is described as a grotesque creature like a monstrous frog or toad, but with a bat-like or webbed wing in place of legs and a long, lashing tail, often furnished with a sting — a thing built for leaping and skimming across the surface of the water.

The Story

Its name comes from its manner of moving: rather than swimming, the Water Leaper springs and bounds across the surface of the lake, using its wings to leap from place to place. It was blamed for a range of misfortunes on the water. It would seize and devour any sheep or livestock that strayed too close to the lake's edge, and worst of all it preyed on fishermen — snatching the bait or the hook, snapping fishing-lines, and in the most frightening tales seizing the line so violently as to drag the unwary angler from the bank into the deep water to drown, or letting out a terrible shriek that froze him with fear.

The Llamhigyn y Dŵr gave shape to the real dangers of the lonely upland lakes — the drownings, the lost livestock, the snapped lines and sudden squalls — in the form of a single uncanny predator lurking beneath the surface. It belongs to the rich Welsh tradition of lake-monsters and water-demons, the afanc among them, that made the still, dark mountain waters of Gwynedd places to be approached with caution and respect.

Llyn Cwm Llwch

HLIN koom HLOOKH

A Welsh tradition says that an enchanted island appeared on Llyn Cwm Llwch each May Day, welcoming visitors into a fairy realm. When a visitor stole a flower and lost his senses, the island vanished and was never seen again.

A visual interpretation of Llyn Cwm Llwch
The Legend

Llyn Cwm Llwch is a small, deep glacial lake cupped in the northern flank of Pen y Fan in the Brecon Beacons, and Welsh tradition made it a gateway to the fairy world. The story tells that on May Day each year a hidden door opened in a rock by the shore, and beyond it lay a passage to a beautiful enchanted island that floated, unseen at other times, on the lake. The fairies who dwelt there would welcome visitors in, feasting and entertaining them, on the one strict condition that nothing — not so much as a single flower or pin — be carried away.

The Otherworld at the Heart of the Tale

The spell was broken, as such spells always are, by a thief: one visitor pocketed a bloom from the fairy island, and the moment he stepped back onto the mortal shore the door slammed shut, the island vanished, and it was never opened again. In a darker strand of the tradition, men once tried to drain the lake to seize the treasure thought to lie beneath, only to be driven off by a terrible voice rising from the water that threatened to drown the whole valley. Llyn Cwm Llwch keeps the common Welsh theme of the lake as a threshold to Annwn, the otherworld, open only briefly and never to be plundered.

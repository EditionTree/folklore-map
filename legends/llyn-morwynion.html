Llyn Morwynion

HLIN mor-WUHN-yon

Llyn Morwynion, 'Lake of the Maidens' above Ffestiniog, is where Blodeuwedd's maidens drowned fleeing Gwydion in the Mabinogion — and where abducted maidens are said to have chosen the water over capture.

A visual interpretation of Llyn Morwynion, Lake of the Maidens
The Legend

Llyn Morwynion — the 'Lake of the Maidens' — is a hill lake above Ffestiniog in Gwynedd, and its name preserves a dark episode from the Fourth Branch of the Mabinogi, the great Welsh cycle of the magician Gwydion and the tragic flower-woman Blodeuwedd. After Blodeuwedd betrayed her husband Lleu Llaw Gyffes to her lover Gronw and Lleu was struck down, Gwydion set out in vengeful pursuit of her and her serving-maidens.

The waters were remembered for the maidens who fled into the lake rather than be taken.

The Lake of the Maidens

As Gwydion hunted them across the hills, Blodeuwedd's terrified maidens fled before him, looking back over their shoulders in fear — and in their blind panic they ran straight into the mountain lake and were all drowned, giving the water its name, the Lake of the Maidens. Blodeuwedd herself Gwydion overtook and, rather than kill her, transformed into an owl, condemned to haunt the night and be shunned by all other birds. The drowning of the maidens is one of the stark, memorable images of the Mabinogi, fixing the myth to a real place on the map of Snowdonia.

A second, related tradition attaches to the lake: a tale of maidens carried off in a raid who, given the choice between captivity and the water, chose to drown together rather than be taken — another story of women meeting their end in its depths. Whether through the Mabinogi's fleeing handmaids or the captured maidens of the later legend, Llyn Morwynion is a lake whose very name is a memorial of drowned women, one of the places where Welsh myth is written into the landscape.

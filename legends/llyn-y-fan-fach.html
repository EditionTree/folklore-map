Llyn y Fan Fach

A Welsh lake famed for the Lady of the Lake, who married a mortal under strict conditions before returning beneath the water. Their sons became the legendary Physicians of Myddfai.

The Legend

Llyn y Fan Fach is a glacial lake cradled beneath the steep slopes of the Black Mountain in Carmarthenshire, and the setting of one of the most beautiful of all Welsh fairy legends — the tale of the Lady of the Lake and the origin of the Physicians of Myddfai. A young farmer, herding his cattle by the water, saw a woman of surpassing beauty sitting on its surface, combing her hair. He fell instantly in love and offered her bread; she refused his first hard-baked loaf and his second under-baked one, but accepted the third, and agreed to become his wife — on one strange condition.

The Story

She warned him that if he should strike her three times without cause, she would leave him and return forever to the lake. For years they lived happily and prospered, and she bore him three sons. But over time the farmer gave her, almost without meaning to, three light, unthinking taps — at a christening, a wedding and a funeral, where she wept or laughed in ways he failed to understand and corrected with a touch. At the third blow the condition was fulfilled: she rose, called her fairy cattle to her, and walked back into the waters of Llyn y Fan Fach, never to live with him again.

But she did not abandon her sons. She returned to them at a place still called Llidiad y Meddygon, the Physicians' Gate, and gave the eldest, Rhiwallon, knowledge of herbs and the healing arts, taking her boys about the meadows to teach them the virtues of every plant. From them descended the famous Physicians of Myddfai, a real medieval dynasty of Welsh doctors whose remedies were written down and survive — so that an enchanted fairy bride of the lake was remembered as the source of a genuine tradition of healing.

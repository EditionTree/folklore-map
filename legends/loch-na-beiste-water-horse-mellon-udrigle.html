Loch na Beiste Water Horse, Mellon Udrigle

Loch na Beiste — 'Loch of the Beast' — near Mellon Udrigle was so feared that a 19th-century minister attempted to drain it after locals reported a creature shaped like an upturned boat-keel.

A visual interpretation of the attempt to drain Loch na Beiste in the 1840s
The Legend

Loch na Beiste — Gaelic for 'the Loch of the Beast' — is a small hill loch near Mellon Udrigle in Wester Ross, in the remote north-west Highlands, and its name records a genuine 19th-century episode of monster-belief and monster-hunting. The local people held that a fearsome creature, a water-horse or each-uisge, dwelt in its dark waters, and described it as a great dark shape like the upturned keel of a boat moving on the surface. So real and so frightening was the belief that the loch became a place of dread, avoided after dark.

The creature, needless to say, was never found, and the loch defeated its would-be exterminators.

The Loch They Tried to Drain

What sets Loch na Beiste apart from the many Highland water-horse lochs is what was done about it. In the 1840s the local landlord or factor, pressed by tenants terrified of the beast, resolved to destroy it. An attempt was made to drain the loch by cutting a channel to let its waters out, and when that failed, quantities of quicklime were tipped into it in the hope of poisoning or driving out whatever lay beneath. The creature, needless to say, was never found, and the loch defeated its would-be exterminators.

The episode is a vivid snapshot of the each-uisge tradition — the malevolent shape-shifting water-horse of Highland and Hebridean lore, which lured victims onto its back and dragged them under to drown and devour them — colliding with the practical, improving spirit of the 19th century. The beast kept its loch, and its name, and the story endures as one of the best-documented cases of a Highland community living in real fear of its local water-monster.

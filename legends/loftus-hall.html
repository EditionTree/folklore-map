Loftus Hall

Loftus Hall is a large country house on the Hook peninsula, County Wexford, Ireland. Built on the site of the original Redmond Hall, it is said to have been haunted by the devil and the ghost of a woman.

The Legend

Loftus Hall is a great, gaunt mansion standing in isolation on the windswept Hook Peninsula in County Wexford, and it is famous as 'the most haunted house in Ireland' on account of a celebrated legend in which the Devil himself came to call. The tale is set in the 1760s, when the house, then an older building called Redmond Hall, was occupied by the Tottenham family, and centres on a young woman of the household, usually named as Anne Tottenham, lonely in the remote great house.

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

One wild stormy night, the story goes, a ship anchored in the bay and a handsome stranger was given shelter at the hall. He was charming and welcome company, and the family sat up late playing cards with him. But during the game Anne, stooping to pick up a card that had fallen to the floor, glanced beneath the table and saw to her horror that the elegant guest had a cloven hoof in place of a foot. At her scream the stranger's true nature broke loose. The Devil rose up in a ball of fire and shot straight up through the ceiling and the roof, leaving a scorched hole that, tradition insists, could never afterwards be repaired.

Anne Tottenham, the legend says, was so traumatised by the encounter that she lost her reason; she was confined to the room where it had happened and pined away there, and her unquiet spirit is said to haunt Loftus Hall to this day, along with the lingering presence of the Devil's visit. Other tellings place her death closer to 1775 rather than the 1760s, a discrepancy that suggests the exact year was never firmly fixed even in the earliest versions of the story. The tale made the house a place of dread and, in modern times, a celebrated destination for ghost-tours.

Longwitton Dragon

A Northumbrian dragon that guarded three healing wells near Longwitton and denied villagers their water. The beast was eventually overcome by a knight who used reflection, timing, and a sharp blade.

A visual interpretation of the Longwitton Dragon guarding the wells
The Legend

The Longwitton Dragon is a Northumbrian dragon-legend tied to the village of Longwitton and its three wells, which were famed for their healing and medicinal waters. According to the tale, a dragon came and took up its guard over the three wells, coiling itself about them — though in some versions it had the power of invisibility — so that the villagers could no longer reach the healing springs on which they relied. For a long time the beast denied them their water, and several would-be slayers failed against it.

A Northumbrian dragon that guarded three healing wells near Longwitton and denied villagers their water.

The Guardian of the Wells

At last a wandering knight came to try his fortune against the dragon. In the first encounters he found the creature almost impossible to defeat, for whenever he seemed to gain the upper hand the dragon would slip back and dip the tip of its tail in one of the wells, whose magical waters instantly healed any wound he had dealt and renewed its strength — so that the fight could never be won by force alone. The knight, realising the secret, changed his tactics.

Cunningly manoeuvring the battle so that he stayed always between the dragon and the wells, he cut the beast off from the source of its healing power; unable to reach the water to renew itself, the dragon at last weakened under his blows and was slain, and the wells were freed for the people of Longwitton once more. The legend is a neat example of the 'dragon undone by wit' type, in which the hero wins not by sheer strength but by understanding and breaking the source of the monster's invulnerability.

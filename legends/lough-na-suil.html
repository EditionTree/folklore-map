Lough na Súil

lokh nah SOOL

Lough na Súil, the Lake of the Eye, is linked to the death of Balor at the Second Battle of Mag Tuired: his fallen eye is said to have burned a hollow into the ground. The Sligo lake periodically drains through its limestone bed and later refills.

The Legend

Lough na Súil — 'the Lake of the Eye' — lies near Geevagh in County Sligo, and its name binds it to one of the most violent moments in Irish mythology: the death of Balor of the Evil Eye. Balor was the dreadful one-eyed king of the Fomorians, whose single great eye, when its heavy lid was levered open, blasted and withered everything its gaze fell upon. At the Second Battle of Mag Tuired it was prophesied that he would be killed by his own grandson, the young god Lugh.

Balor's fallen eye was said to burn a hollow into the land where the lake drains and returns.

The Lake of the Eye

In the legend Lugh faced Balor across the battlefield and, as the monstrous eye was being opened to destroy the host of the Tuatha Dé Danann, struck it through with a sling-stone (in some tellings a spear), driving the eye out through the back of Balor's head so that its killing power fell upon the Fomorians instead. Local tradition fixes the spot at Lough na Súil: the eye, falling burning to the earth, is said to have scorched and burned a hollow into the ground that filled with water to become the lake — the Eye that gives it its name.

The lake has a genuinely strange habit that has kept the legend alive. Lying over fissured limestone, Lough na Súil periodically drains away almost entirely through underground channels, leaving a dry or near-dry basin, before refilling — an event recorded several times over the centuries. To the folk imagination this disappearing and returning water is the restless memory of Balor's burning eye, never quite at peace in the ground it scorched.

Lowri and Siân Owen, the Witches of Llanarmon

Two women near Pwllheli, said to run a cottage "school," cast out unclean spirits by ceremony and were reputed to read the sea's ebb and flow before it turned.

The Legend

In Llanarmon, a small parish near Pwllheli on the Llŷn Peninsula, local memory preserves two women named Lowri and Siân Owen as figures of both fear and use. They were said to run a kind of school from a cottage in the village, holding ceremonies that could cast out unclean spirits and demons from those who came to them for help.

The Story

Their other reputed gift was reading the sea itself. Villagers believed the two women could sense the turn of the tide in advance, anticipating its ebb and flow before it happened, a skill valued in a fishing community where the sea set the rhythm of daily life.

Little else survives about them individually, no firm dates and no account of how their reputation began or ended, but their story fits a pattern found across Wales. The artist Efa Lois has traced around sixty such women through her Gwrachod Cymru (Welsh Witches) project, and found that most functioned less as villains than as healers, using herbal remedies, rituals and chants to treat neighbours for ailments no one else could explain, then taking the blame when something went wrong. Wales prosecuted its accused witches far more rarely than continental Europe did: only five were executed between 1482 and 1782, against tens of thousands killed across Europe in the same period. Lowri and Siân Owen's story today rests mainly on oral tradition gathered by recent Welsh folklore projects rather than any older written record, one small entry in a much larger, and much less bloody, tradition than the word "witch" usually suggests.

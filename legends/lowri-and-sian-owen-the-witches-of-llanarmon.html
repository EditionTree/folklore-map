Lowri and Siân Owen, the Witches of Llanarmon

Two women near Pwllheli, said to run a cottage "school," cast out unclean spirits by ceremony and were reputed to read the sea's ebb and flow before it turned.

The Legend

In Llanarmon, a small parish near Pwllheli on the Llŷn Peninsula, local memory preserves two women named Lowri and Siân Owen as figures of both fear and use. They were said to run a kind of school from a cottage in the village, holding ceremonies that could cast out unclean spirits and demons from those who came to them for help.

The Story

Their other reputed gift was reading the sea itself. Villagers believed the two women could sense the turn of the tide in advance, anticipating its ebb and flow before it happened, a skill valued in a fishing community where the sea set the rhythm of daily life.

Little else survives about them. No firm dates and no account of how their reputation began or ended have come down, and their story today rests mainly on oral tradition gathered by recent Welsh folklore projects rather than any older written record.

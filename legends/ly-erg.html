Ly Erg

A solitary Scottish Highland fairy — neither beautiful nor monstrous — who appears as a soldier with a blood-stained hand on lonely roads. To shake that hand is a death sentence; those who meet him on the road rarely reach their destination.

The Legend

The Ly Erg, from the Gaelic for 'red hand', is a solitary and sinister fairy of the Scottish Highlands, neither beautiful nor monstrous in form, but appearing as a small soldier in old military dress on a lonely moorland path or by a quiet ford. The one mark that betrays its nature is its right hand, which is stained a deep blood-red.

The Otherworld at the Heart of the Tale

When it meets a traveller it raises that red hand in challenge, as if barring the way or demanding a fight. To accept the challenge, to draw and engage, is fatal, for any who cross the Ly Erg are said to sicken and die within a short span. The only safe course is to ignore it utterly and pass on without acknowledging the gesture.

The earliest surviving account of an actual encounter dates to 1669, near Glenmore in what is now the Cairngorms, where three brothers are said to have met the Ly Erg on the road and one accepted its challenge; he sickened and died within the fortnight the tale demands. The story was preserved in eighteenth-century manuscripts gathered by the antiquarian Walter Macfarlane and later reached a wider readership through Sir Walter Scott's Minstrelsy of the Scottish Border, fixing the red-handed soldier as one of the best-documented death-omens of Highland tradition.

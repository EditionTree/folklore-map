Mabon ap Modron

MAH-bon ap MOD-ron

The divine son stolen from his mother Modron when only three nights old. In Arthurian tradition, his rescue becomes one of the great mythic errands before the hunt for Twrch Trwyth.

The Legend

Mabon ap Modron — the name means simply 'Son, son of Mother' — is the divine youth of Welsh myth, a faded god whose very name preserves his nature: Mabon derives from the Romano-British deity Maponos, 'the divine son', and Modron from Matrona, 'the divine mother'. His central story is one of imprisonment and rescue. He was stolen away from his mother Modron when he was only three nights old, and vanished so completely that no one in the world knew whether he was living or dead, or where he might be found.

The Divine Presence

His recovery forms one of the great set-piece quests of the early Arthurian tale Culhwch and Olwen. Among the impossible tasks set the hero Culhwch before he can win Olwen is to find and free Mabon, the only huntsman able to handle the hound needed to hunt the monstrous boar Twrch Trwyth. Arthur's men set out to find him by questioning the oldest animals of the world in turn — the Ousel of Cilgwri, the Stag of Rhedynfre, the Owl of Cwm Cawlwyd, the Eagle of Gwernabwy — each older than the last, until the most ancient of all, the Salmon of Llyn Llyw, carries them on its back to the walls of Gloucester, where Mabon lies imprisoned, lamenting his endless captivity. Arthur's warriors storm the prison and set him free.

Mabon then takes his place in the hunt for Twrch Trwyth, snatching a razor from between the great boar's ears. Behind the adventure-tale stands a much older mythic pattern — the divine child lost and found, the imprisoned god of youth restored to the world — which links Mabon to the wider Celtic cult of Maponos worshipped in northern Britain, and makes him one of the clearest examples of a god surviving, half-forgotten, inside a medieval story.

Maeve Roe, the Banshee of Dunluce Castle

Locked in Dunluce Castle's tower for refusing an arranged marriage, Maeve Roe drowned fleeing by boat with her lover, and her wails are still said to echo from the tower on stormy nights.

The Legend

According to Ulster legend, Maeve Roe was the only daughter of Lord MacQuillan, ruling lord of Dunluce Castle on the County Antrim coast. Her father demanded she marry a suitor of his choosing, but Maeve had already given her heart to Reginald O'Cahan (in some tellings, a MacDonnell), the son of a rival clan. When she refused the arranged match, MacQuillan had her locked in the castle's northeastern tower overlooking the sea.

The Story

On a stormy night, Reginald came to rescue her, and the couple fled down through the sea cave beneath the castle known today as Mermaid's Cave, launching a small boat to cross to the safety of Portrush. The crossing proved fatal: the boat was overwhelmed by the rough waters, and the couple drowned together, Maeve's body never recovered from the sea. Her grieving spirit is said to remain at Dunluce, and visitors and staff have long reported wailing cries and screams from the northeastern tower, particularly during storms, the origin of the site's popular name, the Banshee's Tower.

The legend has been retold in numerous popular collections of Ulster folklore and ghost stories over the past century, including a dedicated modern retelling, Maeve Roe of Dunluce: The Banshee of Dunluce Castle, but its print history goes back further. The Vicomte d'Arlincourt's travel account The Three Kingdoms: England, Scotland, Ireland (1844) already mentions 'Mava, the sweeper of Dunluce, and the Banshee of the Macquillans', the earliest identified written trace of the story. It appears alongside other Ulster tales of the MacQuillan and MacDonnell clans who successively held Dunluce, and while no contemporary medieval record confirms Maeve Roe's historical existence, the story is consistently associated with the specific architectural features of the castle, the North Tower and Mermaid's Cave, giving it an unusually concrete physical anchor for a legend of this kind.

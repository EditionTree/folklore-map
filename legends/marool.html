Marool

The Marool is a Shetland sea-devil associated with mareel, the glow of phosphorescent water. Folklore describes a many-eyed fish-like creature with a fiery crest that revels in storms and shipwrecks.

The Legend

The Marool is a sea-devil of Shetland folklore, one of the malign supernatural creatures of the islands' Norse-rooted tradition, and it is bound up with one of the most beautiful natural phenomena of the northern seas — the mareel, the phosphorescent glow that lights up the water when it is disturbed at night, as the wake of a boat or the break of a wave kindles into cold sea-fire. Where the islanders saw that eerie green-blue luminescence, they might fear the presence of the Marool beneath.

The Sea-Fire in the Storm

The creature was described as a fish-like sea-monster of dreadful aspect, covered in eyes over its whole body and crowned with a flickering crest of flame-like light, so that it shone in the dark water. Far from fearing the storm, the Marool revelled in it: it was said to delight in wild weather and rough seas, rising and dancing in the waves during gales, and rejoicing above all in shipwreck and drowning, singing or shrieking with glee as boats foundered and sailors were lost. To meet the Marool in a storm was a sentence of death.

The Marool belongs to the rich Shetland tradition of sea-terrors — kin to the dreaded Nuckelavee and the Njuggle — which gave shape to the very real dangers of the northern ocean. In the Marool, the haunting glow of the phosphorescent sea, so lovely and so strange, was understood as the shining of a malevolent thing that loved storms and feasted on the drowned.

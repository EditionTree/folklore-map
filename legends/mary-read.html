Mary Read

Mary Read sailed with Anne Bonny and became one of the best-known women of the Golden Age of Piracy. Her birthplace is uncertain; an influential traditional account places her in London. The gaps in the surviving record allowed her story to grow through repeated retelling.

A visual interpretation of Mary Read defending Rackham's sloop in 1720
The Legend

Mary Read was one of the very few women known to have sailed and fought as a pirate during the Golden Age of Piracy, and her story, told in Captain Charles Johnson's 1724 General History of the Pyrates, blends documented fact with legend. Raised from infancy disguised as a boy, she served as a soldier in Flanders before going to sea, and fell in with the pirate company of 'Calico Jack' Rackham in the Caribbean.

It was said that Read and Bonny were the only two still defending the deck while the men hid below.

The Last Fighter on the Deck

Aboard Rackham's sloop she sailed alongside Anne Bonny, the captain's lover, the two women fighting as fiercely as any of the crew. When the pirates were taken in 1720, it was said that Read and Bonny were the only two still defending the deck while the men hid drunk below. Both 'pleaded their bellies' — claimed pregnancy — to escape the gallows; Read died of a fever in a Jamaican jail soon after. Her life, crossing every line of sex and law, made her a lasting legend of the pirate age.

Mary Read

Raised disguised as a boy and hardened as a soldier in Flanders, Mary Read sailed with Anne Bonny and became one of the best-known women of the Golden Age of Piracy.

The Legend

Mary Read was one of the very few women known to have sailed and fought as a pirate during the Golden Age of Piracy, and her story, told in Captain Charles Johnson's 1724 General History of the Pyrates, blends documented fact with legend; the book itself is credited to a pseudonymous 'Johnson' whom some scholars have long suspected was Daniel Defoe, though the attribution remains disputed. Raised from infancy disguised as a boy, she served in the army in Flanders during the Nine Years' War against the French, first as a foot soldier and later in a cavalry regiment, fighting with enough courage to earn respect among men who never knew her sex.

It was said that Read and Bonny were the only two still defending the deck while the men hid below.

The Last Fighter on the Deck

It was in that same army that Read is said to have fallen in love with a fellow soldier and married him once her disguise came out, the couple later running an inn together in the Netherlands before he died and she went back to sea disguised once again. She fell in with the pirate company of 'Calico Jack' Rackham in the Caribbean, sailing aboard his sloop alongside Anne Bonny, the captain's lover, the two women fighting as fiercely as any of the crew.

When the pirates were taken in 1720, it was said that Read and Bonny were the only two still defending the deck while the men hid drunk below. Both 'pleaded their bellies', claimed pregnancy, to escape the gallows; Read died of a fever in a Jamaican jail soon after. Her life, crossing every line of sex and law, made her a lasting legend of the pirate age.

Melangell and the Hare

A hare fled beneath a hermit princess's skirts to escape the hounds of a Powys prince, who was so struck by her courage that he gifted her the whole valley as a sanctuary.

The Legend

Melangell was a seventh-century Irish princess who, according to her medieval Historia, fled to Wales rather than accept an arranged marriage, settling as a hermit in a remote valley of Powys now known as Pennant Melangell. She lived alone there for fifteen years in prayer and solitude until Brochwel Ysgithrog, the ruling prince of Powys, arrived in the valley while hunting a hare. As his hounds closed in, the hare ran to Melangell and hid beneath the folds of her cloak; when Brochwel urged the dogs on, they fled howling, unable to approach her, and his hunting horn stuck fast to his lips so that he could not sound it again.

The Story

Struck by what he had witnessed, Brochwel granted Melangell the entire valley as a permanent place of sanctuary and asylum, declaring that anyone who took refuge there — human or animal — should never be pursued or harmed. Melangell went on to found a small community of women at the site and lived a further thirty-seven years in the valley, during which hares were said to gather fearlessly around her as tame companions; she became venerated as the patron saint of hares and small wild creatures, and local tradition held for centuries afterward that hares in the parish should never be hunted, being known locally as 'Melangell's lambs'.

A twelfth-century church, St Melangell's, was built over her supposed grave at Pennant Melangell and remains a place of pilgrimage to this day, housing what is believed to be the oldest Romanesque shrine still standing in its original location in Britain. The valley's remoteness and the shrine's continuous use as a pilgrimage site over nearly a thousand years give the legend an unusually well-preserved physical and devotional record for a Welsh saint's tale of this age.

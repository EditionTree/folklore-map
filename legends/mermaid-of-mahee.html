Mermaid of Mahee

A sea-maiden with a golden harp haunted the monks of Nendrum, luring one beneath the waves, until the sound of the monastery bells called him home to be shut in the round tower for his trouble.

The Legend

On Mahee Island, in the sheltered reaches of Strangford Lough, the fifth-century monastery of Nendrum stands on ground sacred long before Christianity arrived in County Down. Local tradition holds that a mermaid haunted the waters below the monastic walls, rising at dusk with a golden harp to sing the fishermen and monks into a trance. Those who heard her music followed her voice into the lough and were never quite themselves again, the story says, living out their days as creatures of the sea in caves beneath the water.

The Story

One monk of Nendrum, so the tale goes, dived in after her and lived beneath the waves as her husband, forgetting his old life entirely, until the sound of the monastery's bells carried down through the water and called him back to shore. His brothers, certain he had lost his reason, shut him in the top of Nendrum's round tower, where he spent the rest of his days ringing the very bells that had once saved him.

The legend survives chiefly through the Belfast antiquarian John Vinycomb, MRIA, who set it down as a poem in his pamphlet The Monks of Mahee Island, alongside an account of the site's archaeology and history. Nendrum itself is real and remarkable: founded in the fifth century by St Mochaoi, excavated in the 1920s by H. C. Lawlor, and still ringed by three concentric enclosure walls. The mermaid has since been claimed by Mount Stewart's gardens on the far shore of the lough, where a weathered stone sculpture of her, harp and all, keeps the story visible on the Ards Peninsula.

Metheringham Lass

The Metheringham Lass is the name given to an apparition which has been reported at RAF Metheringham.

The Legend

The Metheringham Lass is a modern ghost legend of Lincolnshire, a variant of the worldwide 'phantom hitchhiker' tale attached to the lonely roads around the former wartime airfield of RAF Metheringham, south-east of Lincoln. Many drivers over the years have reported the same unnerving experience: on a dark and usually rainy night, a distressed young woman appears in the road, flagging them down and begging for help, saying that she and her boyfriend have been in a motorcycle accident and that he is badly hurt and needs aid at once.

The young woman is said to be accompanied by a strong, sweet smell of lavender as she pleads for help.

The Woman in the Road

What marks the encounter out from an ordinary vanishing-hitchhiker story is the detail of scent. The young woman is said to be accompanied by a strong, sweet smell of lavender as she pleads for help — but the moment the driver stops and gets out to assist her, she vanishes utterly, and the lavender is replaced by a sudden, sickening stench of rotting flesh, leaving the witness badly shaken on the empty road.

Researchers into the airfield's history have generally identified the 'lass' with Catherine Bystock, a nineteen-year-old member of the Women's Auxiliary Air Force from Horncastle, who during the Second World War was courting a flight sergeant stationed at Metheringham. She was killed when she was thrown from the sidecar of her sweetheart's motorcycle — a death that matches the ghost's own desperate story of a motorcycle crash, and that has come to be seen as the origin of the haunting on the old airfield roads.

Miosgán Meadhbha

mish-GAWN MAY-va

Miosgán Meadhbha, anglicized Miosgan Meva and also called Maeve's Cairn, is a large cairn on the summit of Knocknarea in County Sligo, Ireland. It is thought to conceal a passage tomb from the Neolithic. It is the largest cairn in Ireland, excepting those at Brú na Bóinne in Meath.

The Legend

Miosgán Meadhbha — 'Maeve's Cairn', also called Maeve's Lump — is the colossal stone cairn crowning the summit of Knocknarea, the great flat-topped hill that dominates the landscape of County Sligo. Unexcavated, it is believed to cover a Neolithic passage tomb some five thousand years old, and it is one of the largest cairns in Ireland; but tradition knows it as the grave of a far later and far more famous figure — Medb (Maeve), the legendary warrior-queen of Connacht.

The immense cairn crowns Knocknarea, where tradition places Queen Medb's tomb.

Maeve's Cairn

Queen Medb is the towering figure of the Ulster Cycle, the proud, fierce and willful queen whose desire to match her husband's wealth by seizing the great brown bull of Cooley launched the Táin Bó Cúailnge, the Cattle Raid of Cooley, and her long war with the hero Cú Chulainn and the men of Ulster. Legend holds that she is buried beneath the great cairn on Knocknarea — and, gloriously, that she was interred standing upright in her armour, fully armed and facing north toward her enemies in Ulster, defiant in death as in life.

A custom attaches to the cairn: that visitors should carry a stone up the hill to add to the great pile (and never take one away), each adding to Maeve's monument. Whether or not the historical reality is a queen of the Iron Age or a goddess of sovereignty older still, the vast cairn riding the skyline of Knocknarea, visible for miles across Sligo, keeps the memory of the warrior-queen Medb fixed upon the land — one of the grandest meetings of monument and myth in Ireland.

Moddey Dhoo

The Moddey Dhoo is the phantom black dog of Peel Castle. Guards became accustomed to its nightly presence, but tradition tells that a soldier who followed it alone through a passage returned terrified and died soon afterwards.

The legend

The Moddey Dhoo — the 'Black Dog', in Manx — is the phantom hound of Peel Castle on the Isle of Man, the most famous ghost of the island. For many years, the tradition runs, a great black spaniel-like dog with shaggy hair would appear in the guard-room of the castle each evening as darkness fell, lying quietly by the fire among the soldiers and slipping away by the passage that led to the captain's quarters at dawn. The men grew so used to it that they took care, while it was present, never to swear or speak ill, and never to walk that dark passage alone.

The story

One night a drunken guard, scorning the others' caution, swore he would go down the forbidden passage alone, with or without the dog, to prove there was nothing to fear. He took up a light and went — and the others heard a dreadful commotion in the dark. He returned white and speechless, and within three days he was dead, never having told what he had seen. The Moddey Dhoo never appeared again. Sir Walter Scott wove the tale into his poem *The Lay of the Last Minstrel*, fixing the black dog of Peel among the best-known spectral hounds of the British Isles.

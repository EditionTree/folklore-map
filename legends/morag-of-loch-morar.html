Morag of Loch Morar

A serpentine creature dwelling in Loch Morar, Britain's deepest freshwater loch. Unlike Nessie, Morag is traditionally considered an omen of death for the local Macdonald clan.

The Legend

Morag is the monster, or water-spirit, of Loch Morar in the western Highlands, the deepest loch in Britain, and the southern sister, as it were, of the more famous beast of Loch Ness. Her Gaelic name, Mhorag, ties her to the loch itself, and reports describe her in much the same terms as other Highland loch-monsters: a large, dark, serpentine creature, sometimes seen with humps breaking the surface or a small head raised on a long neck above the black water of the deep loch.

The Water Legend at the Heart of the Tale

But in the older tradition Morag was far more than a curiosity to be photographed. Among the local families, and the MacDonalds in particular, her appearance was dreaded as a death-omen: to see Morag was to know that a death was coming to the clan, the creature rising from the depths as a herald of mortality in the manner of a banshee rather than as a mere animal. This gave the Loch Morar monster a solemn, fateful quality quite different from the holiday spectacle of Nessie.

Morag entered the wider monster-hunting record through a celebrated incident in 1969, when two local men reported that their motorboat struck, or was struck by, a large creature on the loch one evening; alarmed, one beat at it with an oar while the other fired a rifle, and the thing sank away into the deep. The encounter, widely reported, fixed Morag among the sea- and loch-monsters of cryptozoology, but in her native tradition she remains above all the dark spirit of the deepest loch, whose surfacing foretells a death.

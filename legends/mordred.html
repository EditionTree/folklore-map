Legendary Figures East Lothian, Scotland

Mordred

Arthur's nephew — or in the darker tellings, his son — who seized the throne while Arthur was on the Grail quest, forced Guinevere toward remarriage, and at Camlann fatally wounded the king he had been. The one who ends the Round Table, and cannot be entirely condemned for it.

A visual interpretation of Mordred planning the campaign that ends the Round Table
The Legend

Mordred is the betrayer of the Arthurian world, in the earliest tradition Arthur's nephew and in the darker later tellings his son, begotten unknowingly upon his half-sister Morgause. Left to govern Britain while Arthur warred overseas, Mordred seized the crown, spread word that the king was dead, and moved to take Guinevere — by force or by her reluctant consent, depending on the version — as his own queen.

The one who ends the Round Table, and cannot be entirely condemned for it.

The Man Who Ended the Round Table

Arthur returned to put down the usurpation, and uncle and nephew met at the last battle, Camlann. There the two destroyed each other: Mordred fell on Arthur's spear, but with his dying strength dealt the king the wound that ended his reign and sent him to Avalon. Mordred embodies the worm in the bud of Camelot — the kin-betrayal and hidden sin that, from the moment of his conception, doomed the Round Table from within.

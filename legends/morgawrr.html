Morgawrr

A serpentine sea monster reported in Falmouth Bay since the 1870s. Witnesses describe a humped creature fifteen to twenty feet long, raising a small head on a long neck from the swell.

A visual interpretation of Morgawrr surfacing in Falmouth Bay
The Legend

Morgawr — the name is Cornish for 'sea-giant' — is the sea-monster of Falmouth Bay on the south coast of Cornwall, a serpentine creature reported off the Cornish coast since at least the 1870s and especially during a flurry of sightings in the mid-1970s. Witnesses have described it in consistent terms: a long, dark, humped creature perhaps fifteen to twenty feet in length, raising a small head on a long neck above the swell, sometimes likened to a plesiosaur, gliding through the waters around the Helford estuary, Rosemullion Head and Falmouth Bay.

Witnesses describe a humped creature fifteen to twenty feet long, raising a small head on a long neck from the swell.

The Monster in the Bay

The creature became a celebrated modern sea-serpent during the 1970s, when a series of reported sightings, photographs of disputed authenticity, and accounts from local fishermen and beach-walkers brought it national attention. The episode was bound up with the same Cornish coast and some of the same colourful personalities associated with the Owlman of Mawnan, leading sceptics to suspect hoaxing and folkloric showmanship, while believers pointed to the long history of sightings stretching back into the 19th century.

Whether genuine unknown animal, misidentified basking shark or seal, or a piece of modern myth-making, Morgawr has taken its place as Cornwall's own sea-monster, a salt-water counterpart to the freshwater beasts of the Highland lochs. It continues the very ancient human habit of peopling the deep, mysterious waters off a coast with a great serpentine creature — here given a Cornish name and a thoroughly modern career of sightings and controversy in the waters off Falmouth.

Mother Ludlam

A benevolent witch who lived in a cave near Frensham in Surrey, lending household items to those who needed them from her magic cauldron. When a borrower failed to return it within three days, she refused to lend anything more. The cauldron still sits in Frensham church as proof of the broken bargain.

A visual interpretation of Mother Ludlam's cave legend
The Legend

Mother Ludlam was the benevolent witch of Moor Park near Farnham in Surrey, who dwelt in a cave by the River Wey from which a spring still flows, called Mother Ludlam's Hole. Tradition held that she would lend household goods to any neighbour who came to the cave at midnight and asked aloud, provided the loan was returned within two days; pots, pans and tools would be found waiting at the cave mouth.

Unlike many witch tales, Mother Ludlam's legend remembers generosity before fear.

The Cauldron at the Spring

The spell broke when a borrower kept her great cauldron beyond the appointed time. In her anger Mother Ludlam refused ever to lend again, and the cauldron, passed from hand to hand, is said to have ended up in Frensham church a few miles off, where a large old copper cauldron is indeed kept and shown as hers to this day. In some versions the vessel is instead the Devil's, snatched from him as he leapt across the heath — leaving a chain of hollows known as the Devil's Jumps — a sign of how the kindly witch and the older spirit of the place became entangled.

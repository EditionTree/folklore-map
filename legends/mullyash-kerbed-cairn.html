Mullyash Kerbed Cairn

On Mullyash Mountain’s summit, a Neolithic cairn guards tales of a nobleman’s murdered daughter and hidden gold — and hosted Lughnasadh matchmaking gatherings until modern times.

The legend

The cairn atop Mullyash Mountain in County Monaghan dates to around 4000 BC, a stepped two-tiered structure some sixteen metres across that commands views to Slieve Gullion, Loughcrew, and the Mourne Mountains. Local legend holds it contains the urn burial of a nobleman’s daughter, murdered by her father after she eloped with a young prince he deemed unworthy. The tale promises that hidden gold treasure lies within, visible only to those who eat certain foods — an echo, perhaps, of ancient shamanic practice.

The story

Fionn mac Cumhaill threads through the mountain’s lore too. A standing stone two hundred metres west of the cairn — once 3.6 metres tall, now broken — is said to be the very stone that Fionn hurled from the summit of Slieve Gullion, some sixteen kilometres distant. The feat places Mullyash within the network of Fenian landscape legends stretching across Ulster and beyond.

Until modern times, locals climbed Mullyash on the last Sunday in July — Crom Dubh’s Sunday — keeping alive a remnant of the Celtic harvest festival of Lughnasadh. Celebrants ascended from the southwest, danced and celebrated at the cairn, then walked down westward to visit the standing stone. The mountain was also renowned for matchmaking, making the annual gathering a social institution as much as a spiritual one.

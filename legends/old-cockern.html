Ghosts Dartmoor, Devon

Old Cockern

The spectral huntsman of Dartmoor, keeper of the Wisht Hounds, who rides out on stormy nights to gather the souls of the unbaptised. He is sometimes identified with the Devil, sometimes with an ancient moorland spirit older than Christianity. Farmers left offerings on the moor to keep him from their doors.

A visual interpretation of Old Cockern
The Legend

Old Cockern, or Dewer, is one of the names given on Dartmoor to the spectral huntsman who leads the Wild Hunt across the high moor on storm-black nights. At the head of his pack of fire-eyed Wisht Hounds he rides to run down the souls of the unbaptised and the damned, and to hear his hounds in full cry was reckoned a death-omen for any who listened.

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

The legend is anchored to the Dewerstone, a great crag above the West Dart where Dewer is said to drive his quarry — man or beast — over the edge to destruction. In one well-known tale a moorland farmer riding home in the dark meets the huntsman and boldly asks what sport he has had; the rider tosses him a bundle, which by his own fireside proves to be the body of his own child. The Dartmoor hunt belongs to the great web of Wild Hunt traditions across northern Europe, the older pagan host christianised into a procession of the doomed.

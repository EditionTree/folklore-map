Owain Glyndŵr

OH-wine glin-DOOR

The last native Prince of Wales, who raised a great revolt against English rule and then vanished without trace, and whom tradition makes a sleeping hero who will return at his country's need.

The Legend

Owain Glyndŵr (c.1359-c.1415) was the last Welshman to hold the title Prince of Wales, who in 1400 rose in revolt against the rule of Henry IV and for over a decade led a war that at its height controlled much of the country. In June 1404 he summoned representatives from across Wales to a parliament at Machynlleth, where he was crowned Prince of Wales and discussed an alliance with France; the following February he went further still, signing the Tripartite Indenture with his son-in-law Edmund Mortimer and Henry Percy, Earl of Northumberland, a pact to overthrow Henry IV and carve England and Wales into three, with Owain's own share reaching as far east as the River Severn.

raised a great revolt against English rule and then vanished without trace

The Prince Who Vanished

As the rebellion failed and his strongholds fell, Glyndŵr did not surrender or die in any recorded battle: he simply disappeared around 1415, and no one ever learned where he was buried. That vanishing turned the historical prince into a figure of legend. Like Arthur and other 'sleeping heroes', Owain is said in folk tradition not to be dead but hidden, sleeping in a cave with his men, or simply waiting unseen, ready to return and lead his people when Wales has need of him again. Shakespeare gave him an air of the uncanny in Henry IV, having him 'call spirits from the vasty deep', and across Wales his name became, and remains, the enduring symbol of national identity and the hope of deliverance.

Padfoot

A shaggy spectral dog said to follow travellers with soft padding steps. Like many northern black dogs, Padfoot is both warning and threat, appearing where lanes grow dark and company grows thin.

The Legend

The Padfoot is a spectral black beast of the West Riding of Yorkshire, especially around Leeds, a death-omen creature akin to the Barghest, named for the soft padding of its feet heard following the traveller on a dark road. It appears most often as a large shaggy black dog with broad glowing eyes, but it is a shape-shifter: it may show as a white dog, a woolly fleece rolling along the ground, or a donkey, and it drags a clanking chain behind it.

The Padding Steps Behind You

Old Sally Dransfield, a Leeds carrier who worked the road to Swillington, was a firm believer, and described seeing it roll along the ground before her like a woolpack, then vanish suddenly through a hedge. The Reverend J. C. Atkinson of Danby, who collected such accounts, described it as ever and again padding lightly behind travellers, then appearing before them or at their side, and uttering a roar unlike the voice of any known animal.

To meet the Padfoot, or merely to hear its padding tread and the rattle of its chain behind you, was an omen of death in the household. Tradition warned that a word spoken to it, or a blow struck against it, gave the creature power over you: one story tells of a man whose way it blocked again and again until, in frustration, he kicked it, and was dragged bodily through hedge and ditch all the way to his own front door. Leaving it be, by contrast, let it pass unharmed. The Padfoot stands alongside the Barghest and the Gytrash as one of Yorkshire's classic spectral, death-foretelling beasts of the road.

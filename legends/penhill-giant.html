Penhill Giant

A giant said to have haunted Penhill in Wensleydale, keeping a pack of hounds that terrorised the shepherds of the dale. A local hermit eventually brought about his downfall. His hill-top eyrie commands the whole of Wensleydale, a landscape that still feels watched from above.

The Legend

The Penhill Giant, said in one local telling to be a descendant of Thor himself, haunted the long whaleback hill of Penhill above Wensleydale in the Yorkshire Dales from a fortress on its summit, a brutal ogre who kept a savage hound named Wolfhead and lorded it over the shepherds of the dale, seizing their flocks and crushing any who crossed him.

The Tyrant of Penhill

The story names his end as much as his cruelty. A shepherdess called Gunda begged the giant to call Wolfhead off her flock, all her family owned, and when he refused an old man helped her turn the hound against its own master. Freed by Gunda, Wolfhead pounced on the giant instead of the sheep and drove him over the cliffs of Penhill, and neither giant nor hound was ever seen again.

The tale belongs to the rich seam of Yorkshire giant- and ogre-lore tied to particular hills, in which the looming landmark of Penhill is given a tyrant for a former master, and his fall becomes the dale's deliverance. The local writer Ian Taylor, in his book The Giant of Penhill, argued that the story may even preserve a distorted memory of a real and lawless lord of the dale.

Penhill Giant

A giant said to have haunted Penhill in Wensleydale, keeping a pack of hounds that terrorised the shepherds of the dale. A local hermit eventually brought about his downfall. His hill-top eyrie commands the whole of Wensleydale — a landscape that still feels watched from above.

The legend

The Penhill Giant haunted the long whaleback hill of Penhill above Wensleydale in the Yorkshire Dales, a brutal ogre who kept a pack of savage hounds and lorded it over the shepherds of the dale, seizing their flocks and crushing any who crossed him. The legend tells how his cruelty brought his downfall: when one of his hounds savaged a shepherd's child, or in other versions a maiden he had wronged, the giant met defiance at last.

The story

A local hermit or holy man is said to have foretold the giant's doom, and the monster, raging, perished — struck down, or swallowed by the earth, or destroyed by the curse his own brutality had earned. The story belongs to the rich seam of Yorkshire giant- and ogre-lore tied to particular hills, in which the looming landmark of Penhill is given a tyrant for a former master, and his fall becomes the dale's deliverance.

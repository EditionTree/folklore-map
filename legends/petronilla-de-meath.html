Petronilla de Meath

Petronilla de Meath, maidservant of Alice Kyteler, was burned at Kilkenny on 3 November 1324 — the first person executed for witchcraft in Ireland, after torture forced her confession while her mistress escaped.

The legend

Petronilla de Meath was the maidservant of the wealthy Kilkenny noblewoman Alice Kyteler, and in 1324 she became the first person known to have been burned for heresy and witchcraft in Ireland. When Alice Kyteler was accused before Richard de Ledrede, the zealous Bishop of Ossory, of sorcery, of poisoning her husbands and of consorting with a demon familiar named Robin Artisson, her wealth and powerful connections allowed her to flee the country — but Petronilla could not escape.

The story

Seized in her mistress's place, the servant was flogged and tortured until she confessed to acting as Kyteler's accomplice in witchcraft, and on 3 November 1324 she was burned at the stake in Kilkenny. Her execution — decades ahead of the great European witch-hunts — marked the importation into Ireland of the new continental idea of diabolical witchcraft, and stands as a stark early example of how the poor and powerless paid the price for accusations against the rich. Petronilla de Meath is remembered as the first witch burned in Ireland, condemned for the crimes of the mistress who escaped.

Petticoat Loose

Damned for killing an unbaptised child, the ghost Petticoat Loose was banished to Bay Lough in the Knockmealdowns to twist ropes of sand forever; parents still warn children she will get them.

The Legend

Petticoat Loose is a malevolent Irish ghost known across the south from Waterford into Tipperary, called Moll Shaughnessy in Cork and Sprid na Bearnan in Limerick. In life, the stories say, she was a woman who murdered an unbaptised child, and for that crime her soul was denied rest.

Condemned to an impossible, endless task: to twist ropes of sand, or empty the lough with a thimble, so that she can never finish and return.

The Rope That Can Never Hold

She is best remembered haunting the mountain road above Bay Lough in the Knockmealdowns, where riders travelling by night told of their horses breaking into a foaming, uncontrollable bolt the moment her spirit drew near. One well-told tale has a man riding through the dark to fetch a priest, set upon along that same road until words of holy scripture drive her off; a priest is needed to lay her properly, and he banishes her, in some versions to the Red Sea, in others to Bay Lough itself, to an impossible, endless task: twisting ropes of sand, or baling out the lough with a thimble, so that she can never finish and return.

Bay Lough itself has a matching reputation. Local tradition holds it fills the crater of an extinct volcano, a small dark tarn with no visible outlet cut into the mountainside, and that when the people of nearby Clogheen once tried to drain it, they looked down and saw their own town apparently ablaze, and abandoned the work rather than test whether the vision was true.

Her name became a household warning long before that. From Cork to Waterford, parents still tell wilful children to behave or 'Petticoat Loose will get you', and the lonely waters of Bay Lough keep their reputation as a haunted place where her restless spirit is said to linger yet.

Phantom Battle of Edgehill

After the Battle of Edgehill in 1642, witnesses reported ghostly armies replaying the fighting above the battlefield. The tradition includes apparitions, battle cries, cannon fire and the sound of horses around the anniversary of the clash.

The Legend

The Battle of Edgehill, fought in Warwickshire on 23 October 1642, was the first great clash of the English Civil War, and within weeks it had given rise to one of the most remarkable apparition stories in English history. That Christmas, shepherds and travellers crossing the fields near Kineton reported seeing and hearing the battle fought all over again in the sky and over the ground — phantom armies with drums, colours and the cries of men, the spectral hosts of King and Parliament charging through one another in the cold air.

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

The sightings caused such a stir that, according to the contemporary pamphlet A Great Wonder in Heaven, King Charles I sent a party of officers to investigate; they are said to have not only witnessed the spectral battle themselves but recognised among the ghostly combatants the faces of men they knew to have fallen there. Whatever lay behind the reports, the Phantom Battle of Edgehill became the classic English example of a 'replaying' haunting — a violent event so charged that the land itself was believed to re-enact it.

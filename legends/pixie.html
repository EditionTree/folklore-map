Pixie

Small, mischievous fae of the West Country with pointed ears and a gift for leading travellers astray — being pixie-led means going in circles until you turn your coat inside out to break the spell. They also steal horses to ride through the night, returning them exhausted and sweated by dawn.

The legend

The pixie — or piskie — is the small fairy of the West Country, Cornwall and Devon especially, distinct in name and temper from the fairies of other regions. Often described as little, ragged, red-haired and pointed of ear, pixies are above all mischievous: they dance in rings on the moor by night, ride and tangle the manes of farm horses, and delight in leading travellers astray.

The story

To be 'pixie-led' was a real and feared misfortune — to find oneself walking in circles on familiar ground, unable to reach home, the paths confused by laughing unseen sprites. The classic remedy was to turn one's coat or a pocket inside out, which broke the enchantment. Yet pixies could also be kindly, threshing corn or doing housework for the deserving in the manner of a brownie, provided they were neither watched nor thanked with gifts of clothing. On Dartmoor and the Cornish moors the piskie remained a living part of country belief well into modern times.

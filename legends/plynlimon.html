Plynlimon

PIM-lih-mon

A great giant of Welsh legend whose three daughters — Hafren, Gwy, and Rheidol — raced each other from his summit to the sea. Hafren reached the sea first, winning greatest fame, and her name became the River Severn. Plynlimon watches still from the highest ground in mid-Wales, father of rivers.

A visual interpretation of Plynlimon, father of rivers in Welsh legend
The Legend

Plynlimon (Pumlumon), the high boggy massif of the Cambrian Mountains, was in Welsh tradition a giant from whose slopes three great rivers rise — the Severn, the Wye and the Rheidol. Folklore made the rivers his three daughters: Hafren (Severn), Gwy (Wye) and Rheidol, who agreed to race one another from the summit down to the sea, each choosing her own road.

The Father of Rivers

Rheidol, taking the shortest and steepest course, reached the sea at Aberystwyth first. Hafren, the Severn, chose the longest and most roundabout way, wandering east and south across half of England before turning back to the sea, and so saw the most of the world; the Wye took a path between. The tale is a poetic geography, explaining in the shape of a giant's daughters why three rivers of such different lengths should all spring from one bleak mountain — the watershed of Wales made into a family story.

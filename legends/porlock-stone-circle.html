Porlock Stone Circle

A rare Exmoor stone circle haunted by a phantom horse — locals say the sound of invisible hoofbeats echoes along the road at night and living horses refuse to pass after dark.

The Legend

Porlock Stone Circle sits on open moorland above the village of Porlock in Somerset, one of only two stone circles surviving on Exmoor. Unlike granite-rich Dartmoor, Exmoor’s Devonian slates and sandstones break easily into small slabs, making large standing stones scarce here. The circle’s thirteen remaining green micaceous sandstone rocks — of perhaps forty-three originally — form a ring about twenty-four metres across, with a cairn and stone row extending to the north-east. The monument dates to the Late Neolithic or Early Bronze Age, somewhere between 3300 and 900 BCE.

The Story

The circle was lost to memory until the early 20th century, when local landowner E. T. MacDermot noticed the stones after heather burning exposed them on Porlock Common. He alerted the archaeologist Harold St George Gray, who surveyed the site in 1928. But it is the folklore that gives the circle its atmosphere. According to local tradition recorded by S. Toulson in 1983, the road passing the stones is haunted by a spectral horse. People tell of hooves clattering hollowly along the hard surface of the road when no horse is there. Hill ponies are rarely seen grazing near the stones after dusk, and riders report their mounts refusing to continue along the lane past the circle at night.

The phantom hoofbeats place Porlock within a wider pattern of Exmoor lore in which ancient ceremonial sites carry an uncanny charge — places where animals sense something that human senses miss.

Queen Medb

MAYV

Medb of Connacht was sovereignty itself — no king could rule without her — and the queen who launched Ireland's greatest cattle raid against Ulster was finally brought down by a single shot of hard cheese.

A visual interpretation of Queen Medb on Knocknarea in County Sligo
The Legend

In the Ulster Cycle of early Irish literature, Medb is queen of Connacht, ruling from the great royal site of Cruachan (modern Rathcroghan, County Roscommon). She is no ordinary monarch: many scholars read her as a personification of the sovereignty of Connacht itself, a goddess in human form whose favour — and bed — a man had to win before he could claim the kingship. Across the sagas she takes and discards husbands, among them Ailill mac Mata, and is consistently portrayed as more powerful, more decisive, and more dangerous than any of the men around her.

Medb is said to be buried standing upright, facing her enemies in Ulster, beneath the great cairn known as Meascan Meabha.

The Queen in the Cairn

Medb's most famous exploit is the Tain Bo Cuailnge, the Cattle Raid of Cooley, Ireland's great epic. Determined that her own wealth should at least match her husband Ailill's — down to the last bull — she led the armies of Connacht against Ulster to seize the great brown bull Donn Cuailnge. With Ulster's warriors disabled by a curse, the boy-hero Cu Chulainn held the invading army at bay alone in a months-long series of single combats at fords, one of the central narratives of the entire Ulster Cycle.

Medb's end was as dramatic as her life. Years earlier she had murdered her pregnant sister Clothru; Clothru's son, Furbaide Ferbend, grew up vowing revenge. Learning that Medb bathed each morning in a pool on an island in Lough Ree, Furbaide practised for months with a sling until he could hit a mark at the exact range — then killed the queen of Connacht with a single shot of hard cheese. Medb is said to be buried standing upright, facing her enemies in Ulster, beneath the great cairn known as Meascan Meabha (Maeve's Cairn) on the summit of Knocknarea in County Sligo.

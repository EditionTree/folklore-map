Folklore of England

England's folklore rarely stays still. It moves parish to parish, and each village keeps its own version of a story the next village tells differently. A black dog stalks a lonely lane in Norfolk. A worm coils around a hill in Durham. A witch's name still marks a cottage in Lancashire. Older customs survive alongside the ghosts. Derbyshire still dresses its wells with flowers each summer, and church roofs across the country carry carved green men older than the buildings around them. Much of it is tied to one exact spot: a stone, a bend in a road, a single tree still pointed out today.

Folklore of Ireland

Ireland's oldest stories belong to the Tuatha Dé Danann, the mythical race said to have retreated into the hollow hills and become the sídhe. Their raths and cairns still dot the countryside, and farmers have avoided ploughing through some of them for generations, just in case. Two great cycles carry the human-scale heroes: Cú Chulainn and the warriors of Ulster, then Fionn mac Cumhaill and the Fianna. A banshee's cry still means one thing in the old belief, and holy wells across the country are named for saints said to have blessed them personally.

Rhiannon

The Legend

Rhiannon is one of the most luminous figures of Welsh myth, a woman of the Otherworld bound up with horses, sovereignty and unbreakable endurance. She enters the First Branch of the Mabinogi riding a pale horse past the mound of Arberth, moving at a gentle, even pace — yet no rider of Pwyll's court can overtake her, however hard they gallop, until Pwyll himself thinks to call out and ask her to stop, whereupon she halts at once and reveals she has come of her own will to seek him as her husband, having refused the man to whom she was promised.

She first appears riding calmly beyond mortal pursuit, impossible to catch unless asked to stop.

The Rider Beyond Pursuit

Her marriage to Pwyll is shadowed by tragedy. When their infant son vanishes one night from his cradle, Rhiannon's serving-women, fearing blame, smear her with the blood of pups and accuse her of devouring her own child. Though innocent, Rhiannon accepts a strange and humiliating penance: for years she must sit each day by the horse-block at the gate of the court, tell her tale to every visitor, and offer to carry them on her back into the hall like a beast of burden. The boy, meanwhile, has been rescued elsewhere and is restored to her in time, his recovery clearing her name.

Behind the story lies an older divinity. Rhiannon's link to horses, her association with sovereignty conferred on a king, and the magical birds whose song can wake the dead and lull the living all mark her as a euhemerised goddess, often compared to the Gaulish horse-deity Epona and the Irish sovereignty goddesses. Patient, dignified and unjustly wronged, she endures as one of the great heroines of the Welsh tradition.

Rhitta Gawr

The great giant-king of Wales who made a cloak from the beards of the kings he slew. He demanded Arthur's beard for the final trim. Arthur refused — and killed him on the slopes of Snowdon, which was then called Rhitta's Cairn.

A visual interpretation of Rhitta Gawr demanding Arthur's beard
The Legend

Rhitta Gawr (Rhitta the Giant) is the fearsome giant-king of Welsh tradition who reigned over the mountains of Snowdonia. His cruelty took a peculiar and terrible form: having defeated and slain a host of kings in battle, he made himself a great mantle from the beards he shaved from their faces, a trophy-cloak proclaiming his conquests. When the cloak was finished he found it lacked a border, and sent word to the two greatest kings yet unbeaten — in the most famous version, to Arthur himself — demanding their beards to trim it, or else he would come and take them by force, beard and head together.

The great giant-king of Wales made a cloak from the beards of the kings he slew.

The King with the Beard-Cloak

Arthur, enraged at the insolence, marched against Rhitta, and the two met in single combat on the heights. After a savage struggle Arthur slew the giant, and Rhitta's followers raised over his body a great cairn of stones, each warrior casting one — a mound said to crown the summit of Snowdon, Yr Wyddfa, 'the tomb' or 'the cairn'. So the highest mountain in Wales was remembered as the grave of the beard-cloaked giant brought down by Arthur.

Legendary Figures Rathlin Island, County Antrim

Robert the Bruce

Legend says that Robert the Bruce sheltered in a cave after defeat and watched a spider repeatedly attempt to build its web. When the spider finally succeeded, its persistence inspired Bruce to continue his own struggle. Several caves claim the tale; Rathlin Island preserves a strong local association.

A visual interpretation of the spider inspiring Robert the Bruce
The Legend

The most enduring legend of Robert the Bruce belongs not to the battlefield but to a cave, where the future King of Scots is said to have hidden at the lowest ebb of his fortunes after defeat by the English. Tradition most often sets the cave on Rathlin Island off the Antrim coast, where Bruce had fled in 1306. Sheltering there, despairing, he watched a spider try again and again to swing a thread across a gap in the rock to anchor its web.

When the spider finally succeeded, its persistence inspired Bruce to continue his own struggle.

The Lesson in the Cave

Six times the spider failed, and six times it tried once more, until at last the thread held and the web was begun. Bruce, who had himself been defeated six times, took the spider's persistence as a sign, resolved to fight on, and returned to win back his kingdom — crowned by victory at Bannockburn in 1314. The tale, popularised in the nineteenth century, gave the English-speaking world its proverb of 'if at first you don't succeed, try, try again', and several caves in Scotland and Ireland claim to be the one.

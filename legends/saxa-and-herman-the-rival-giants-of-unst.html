Saxa and Herman, the Rival Giants of Unst

Two feuding giants hurled boulders at each other across a Shetland inlet until a mermaid lured both to their deaths in a swimming race to the North Pole.

The Legend

On Unst, Britain's northernmost inhabited island, local tradition names two giants, Herman and Saxa, as the makers of its wildest headlands. Herman held Hermaness on the island's northwest tip, and Saxa held the opposing headland of Saxa Vord across the narrow inlet of Burrafirth; the two were sworn enemies who spent their days hurling great boulders at one another across the water. The landscape is still read as evidence of the feud — a submerged rock offshore is known as Saxa's Baa, and a flat rock on Herman's side as Herman's Hellyacraig — place names that fossilise the story into the map itself.

The Story

According to the tale both giants fell for the same mermaid, a temptress who used their rivalry to destroy them both: she challenged whichever of them could swim fastest to the North Pole to win her heart. Neither Herman nor Saxa could swim a stroke, but pride sent both wading in after her, and both drowned in the attempt, leaving the headlands named for two giants who never got the girl.

Shetland folklorists count this among the oldest surviving stories from Orkney and Shetland, part of a wider Northern Isles tradition of giants shaping the coastline through their quarrels — a Norse-flavoured strand of folklore distinct from the Gaelic giant lore of the Hebrides and the Scottish mainland further south.

Scota Bess

A storm-witch ruled the Mermaid's Chair on Stronsay's shore, cursing fishermen with endless gales, until islanders beat her to death and still couldn't keep her in the ground.

The Legend

On the Orkney island of Stronsay, tradition holds that a witch called Scota Bess once occupied a rock seat on the shore of Mill Bay, known as the Mermaid's Chair. From there she was said to order up storms, keeping the island's fishing boats trapped in harbour through weeks of unbroken January gales. When the islanders had endured enough, they drove her from her chair and beat her to death in a barn at Huip, one of Stronsay's farming settlements near the loch of Meikle Water.

The Story

The story does not end with her death. Each time the islanders buried Scota Bess, her body was found lying disinterred beside the grave the following morning, as though the ground itself refused her. Unable to keep her under, the men finally carried her body to Meikle Water and threw it in; a mound of earth is said to have grown up in the loch afterwards, later used as a nesting site by swans, read by some as a sign of the witch's uneasy rest, by others as evidence she was still scheming beneath the water.

No historical record of an actual woman behind the name survives, and Scota Bess reads as much like a Norse-inflected storm-hag or minor local deity as a remembered individual, of a piece with Orkney's wider tradition of weather-witches blamed for wrecking boats and ruining harvests. The Mermaid's Chair itself carries a second, gentler strand of folklore alongside hers: local tradition holds that a girl who sits in it will come to understand the mysteries of life and be able to foretell the future, folding the site's fearsome history into a piece of everyday island courting-lore.

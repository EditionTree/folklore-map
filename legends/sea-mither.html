Sea Mither

An Orcadian summer sea-spirit who calms the waters and restrains the monstrous Teran. Her yearly struggle with him explains the turn between gentle seas and winter storms.

A visual interpretation of Sea Mither
The Legend

The Sea Mither — the 'Sea Mother' — is a benevolent spirit of Orcadian folklore, the kindly power of the summer sea who holds the ocean's fury in check through the warm half of the year. Invisible and tireless, she dwells in the sea during summer and spends her strength calming the waters, warming them, nurturing the creatures of the deep, and above all restraining her great enemy, the cruel winter-spirit Teran, whose nature is storm, cold and destruction. To the islanders, dependent on the sea for their living and their lives, she was the gentle force that made the waters safe for fishing and travel.

The Water Legend at the Heart of the Tale

Her story is told as an eternal seasonal war. Twice a year the Sea Mither and Teran meet in a titanic struggle for mastery of the sea, a battle the Orcadians called the Vore Tullye (the spring struggle) and the Gore Vellye (the autumn struggle). In spring the Sea Mither overcomes Teran, binds him to the sea-bed, and ushers in the calm and fruitful summer; but by autumn she is worn out by her long labours of calming storms and tending the sea's life, and Teran, his strength returning, rises up and defeats her in turn, casting her down and seizing the sea for the winter — whereupon the gales and wild seas of the dark months are loosed.

So the cycle turns, year after year, the gentle mother and the raging tyrant each ruling the waters in their season. The myth is a beautiful Orkney explanation of the turning of the year at sea — the calm of summer and the storms of winter understood as the victories and defeats of a loving spirit who spends herself, again and again, to shelter the islands from the rage of the deep.

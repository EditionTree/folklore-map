Shug Monkey

A creature half-shaggy-dog and half-ape said to haunt 'Shug Monkey Lane' near Brinkley in Cambridgeshire, padding the dark road on its hind legs with great saucer eyes and rattling claws.

The legend

The Shug Monkey belongs to the folklore of the Cambridgeshire fens and chalk, a nightmarish hybrid reported along a lane near Brinkley sometimes called Shug Monkey Lane. Witnesses described it as part shaggy black dog and part ape, able to shamble upright on its hind legs, with huge gleaming eyes and claws that scraped the road — a thing that would pad behind a lone walker or rush at children sent out after dark.

The story

Its name ties it to the wider East Anglian family of 'shuck' or 'shug' beasts — from Old English scucca, a demon — of which the Norfolk Black Shuck is the most famous. Where Shuck is a spectral hound, the Shug Monkey is stranger, a creature of indeterminate shape that captures the particular dread of the dark country lane, and it survived chiefly as a bogey to keep children off the road at night.

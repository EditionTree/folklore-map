Sir Galahad

Son of Lancelot, conceived by enchantment, and the only knight pure enough to achieve the Grail. He appeared at Camelot on Pentecost, sat unharmed in the Siege Perilous, and rode out. When he finally held the Grail he asked to die at that moment of perfection, and was taken bodily into heaven.

A visual interpretation of Galahad achieving the Holy Grail
The legend

Sir Galahad is the perfect knight of the Arthurian world, the destined achiever of the Holy Grail. The son of Lancelot, conceived by enchantment upon the Grail-keeper's daughter Elaine, he was raised away from court and brought to Camelot as a youth on the feast of Pentecost. There he sat unharmed in the Siege Perilous, the seat at the Round Table that killed any unworthy man who dared it, and so revealed himself as the one for whom it had been kept.

The only knight pure enough to achieve the Grail.

The knight who reached the Grail

The drawing of a sword from a floating stone confirmed him, and the Grail quest began. Where other knights — even his father Lancelot, flawed by his love for Guinevere — could only glimpse the Grail or fail outright, Galahad's spotless purity let him attain it fully. Having beheld the sacred vessel's deepest mysteries at the far city of Sarras, he asked to be taken from the world, and died in a state of grace, his soul borne up by angels. Introduced in the thirteenth-century Vulgate cycle, Galahad embodies the spiritualising of chivalry into a quest for holiness.

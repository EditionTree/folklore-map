Sir Percival

Raised in the wilderness, ignorant of all chivalry, Percival came to Camelot a laughingstock and became its purest knight. He was the first to reach the Grail castle — and asked the wrong question, or failed to ask any at all, and the Grail withdrew. He spent the rest of his life finding his way back.

A visual interpretation of Percival at the Grail castle
The Legend

Sir Percival — Perceval, and in Welsh tradition Peredur — is the great innocent of the Arthurian world, the knight raised in ignorance who rises to the highest grace. His mother, having lost husband and sons to chivalry, brought him up in the wild forest knowing nothing of knights or arms; when he first saw armoured riders he took them for angels, and came to Arthur's court a laughing-stock in homespun before proving himself.

He was the first to reach the Grail castle - and asked the wrong question, or failed to ask any at all.

The Question Left Unasked

His defining story is the Grail. In the earliest tellings, Chrétien de Troyes' unfinished romance, Percival comes to the castle of the wounded Fisher King and witnesses the mysterious procession of the bleeding lance and the Grail — but, schooled to be courteous and not to ask too many questions, he fails to ask whom the Grail serves, and so leaves the king unhealed and the land laid waste. Only after long wandering and penance does he learn the cost of that silence. In later cycles his role as Grail-winner is shared with the pure Galahad, but Percival remains the seeker whose holiness is born of simplicity.

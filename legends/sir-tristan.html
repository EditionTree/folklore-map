Sir Tristan

Sent to fetch the Irish princess Iseult as bride for his uncle King Mark of Cornwall, Tristan drank a love potion with her by accident on the voyage home. Bound together by a love neither could resist or escape, they spent their lives between bliss and ruin — and he died calling her name.

The legend

Sir Tristan of the Arthurian and Cornish tradition was nephew to King Mark of Cornwall, sent to Ireland to bring back the princess Iseult to be his uncle's bride. On the voyage home the two unknowingly drank a love-potion brewed for Mark and Iseult's wedding night, and were bound by an irresistible passion that no loyalty or marriage could undo.

The story

The long tale that follows is one of secret meetings, narrow escapes and betrayals at Mark's court at Tintagel, until the discovered lovers are parted and Tristan exiled. Wounded by a poisoned weapon, he sends for Iseult, the only one who can heal him, with the signal of a white sail if she comes and a black if she does not; told falsely that the sail is black, he dies of despair as she lands, and she dies of grief upon his body. From their graves two trees or briars grew and entwined. Older than its absorption into the Round Table legends, the Tristan story is one of the great tragic romances of medieval Europe, closely echoing the Irish tale of Diarmuid and Gráinne.

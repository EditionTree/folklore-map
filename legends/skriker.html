Skriker

A death-omen of Lancashire and the Pennines, the Skriker wanders the night woods uttering a horrible shriek, or pads unseen on great splashing feet. To hear it was to know a death was near.

The Legend

The Skriker, its name from the dialect word for a shriek, is a spectral death-omen of Lancashire and the West Riding, a creature that haunts the night woods and lonely roads of the Pennine country. Often it gives its warning not by being seen but by its dreadful cry, a piercing screech in the dark; to hear it was to know that a death would soon come to one's family or close friends.

To hear the Skriker was to know a death was near.

The Shriek in the Woods

When it does take shape it appears as a huge black dog with broad, drooping ears, shaggy hair and great glowing eyes, padding along on enormous feet that make a wet splashing sound, "like old shoes in soft mud," from which it earns its other name, Trash. It can also show itself as a white cow or horse, and walks backwards before the traveller, or vanishes into the ground when challenged. The name and its core details were set down by Victorian folklorists: John Harland and T. T. Wilkinson's Lancashire Folk-lore (1867) and James Bowker's Goblin Tales of Lancashire (1887) both record it, and Joseph Wright's English Dialect Dictionary (1913) confirms the term was still current in local speech; Wilkinson himself recorded meeting witnesses around Burnley, the name's home ground, who described the creature firsthand.

Trash and Skriker are specifically Burnley names for what is, across Lancashire more widely, a single sprawling tradition of phantom hound known by a dozen local titles, among them Barguist, Gytrash, Padfoot, The Grim and Shag, all describing broadly the same threshold-haunting dog even where the details of colour, size and cry shift from parish to parish. The Skriker belongs to this great northern family of barguests and padfoots, the phantom hounds that patrol the boundary between the living and the dead across the hills of northern England.

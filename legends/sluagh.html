Sluagh

SLOO-agh

The Legend

The Sluagh — the 'host' — are the most dreaded of the spirits in Scottish and Irish Gaelic belief: the airborne multitude of the restless, unforgiven dead. Neither the blessed dead nor quite the fairy folk, they were imagined as a dark flock sweeping through the night sky, often from the west, with a sound like a great rushing of wings, gathering up souls and bent on mischief and harm.

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

Hebridean tradition held that the Sluagh flew in from the west, the direction of the dead, and so people were careful to keep the west-facing windows of a house shut, especially when someone lay dying, lest the host swoop in to seize the departing soul. The Sluagh were said to snatch up unwary travellers caught abroad after dark and carry them for miles through the air, forcing them to take part in their malice — even compelling them to fire elf-shot at cattle and people below — before dropping them, dazed and far from home. They are the night-flying, soul-stealing host at the darkest edge of the Gaelic otherworld.

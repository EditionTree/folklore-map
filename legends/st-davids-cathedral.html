St Davids Cathedral

St Davids Cathedral sits low in its valley at the western tip of Wales, deliberately hidden from the sea, tradition says, so its tower wouldn't catch a raider's eye.

The Legend

St Davids Cathedral stands in a hollow beside the River Alun in the smallest city in Britain, at the far western tip of Pembrokeshire, set low in its valley, it is said, so that its tower should not be seen from the sea and draw the eyes of raiders. It marks the site of the monastery founded around the year 520 by Saint David (Dewi Sant), the patron saint of Wales, and it has been one of the great places of Christian pilgrimage in Britain for well over a thousand years.

The cathedral anchors the sacred landscape of St Davids near the western edge of Wales.

The Cathedral in the Valley

A rich body of legend surrounds the saint and his foundation. David was born, tradition holds, on the storm-lashed clifftop nearby, where the ruined chapel and holy well of his mother, Saint Non, still stand; a spring is said to have burst from the ground at his birth. He founded his community in the valley of Glyn Rhosyn and led a life of famous austerity, his monks labouring with their own hands and drinking only water, earning David the name Aquaticus, 'the water-drinker'. His most celebrated miracle came at the synod of Llanddewi Brefi, where, as he preached to a great crowd who could not see or hear him, the ground rose up beneath his feet into a little hill and a white dove settled on his shoulder.

The building standing today dates from long after the saint's own time. Bishop Peter de Leia began the present cathedral in 1181, and it was largely complete not long afterwards, though its central tower collapsed at least twice in the following centuries. Bishop Henry Gower rebuilt much of the cathedral in the 14th century, adding the rood screen and the fine parapets that still mark the building, so that what pilgrims see today is a medieval reconstruction wrapped around the memory of a sixth-century saint.

So holy was the shrine that the medieval Church reckoned two pilgrimages to St Davids the equal of one to Rome, and kings and commoners came to venerate the saint's relics, kept in the cathedral to this day. With its saint born in a clifftop storm, its rising ground and its sacred wells, St Davids gathers the founding legends of Welsh Christianity around the resting place of the nation's patron saint.

Origins & Dating

Approximate Originc. 520-589 (monastic founding); 1181 (present building begun)
Historical PeriodEarly medieval founding (6th century); High Medieval standing building from 1181 onward, with 14th-century remodelling
Earliest Written RecordCadw's own listed-building record states the cathedral stands 'on the site of the monastery founded by Saint David ca 520'; other sources give St David's death as 589, with the monastic community generally dated to the mid-6th century. The standing cathedral building was begun in 1181 under Bishop Peter de Leia (Bishop of St Davids 1176-1198) and largely completed not long after, with later medieval work (14th century, under Bishop Henry Gower) following two collapses of the central tower.

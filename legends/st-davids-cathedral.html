St Davids Cathedral

St Davids Cathedral is a Church in Wales cathedral situated in St Davids, Britain's smallest city, in the county of Pembrokeshire, near the most westerly point of Wales.

A visual interpretation of St Davids Cathedral
The Legend

St Davids Cathedral stands in a hollow beside the River Alun in the smallest city in Britain, at the far western tip of Pembrokeshire — set low in its valley, it is said, so that its tower should not be seen from the sea and draw the eyes of raiders. It marks the site of the monastery founded in the 6th century by Saint David — Dewi Sant — the patron saint of Wales, and it has been one of the great places of Christian pilgrimage in Britain for well over a thousand years.

The cathedral anchors the sacred landscape of St Davids near the western edge of Wales.

The Cathedral in the Valley

A rich body of legend surrounds the saint and his foundation. David was born, tradition holds, on the storm-lashed clifftop nearby, where the ruined chapel and holy well of his mother, Saint Non, still stand; a spring is said to have burst from the ground at his birth. He founded his community in the valley of Glyn Rhosyn and led a life of famous austerity, his monks labouring with their own hands and drinking only water — earning David the name Aquaticus, 'the water-drinker'. His most celebrated miracle came at the synod of Llanddewi Brefi, where, as he preached to a great crowd who could not see or hear him, the ground rose up beneath his feet into a little hill and a white dove settled on his shoulder.

So holy was the shrine that the medieval Church reckoned two pilgrimages to St Davids the equal of one to Rome, and kings and commoners came to venerate the saint's relics, kept in the cathedral to this day. With its saint born in a clifftop storm, its rising ground and its sacred wells, St Davids gathers the founding legends of Welsh Christianity around the resting place of the nation's patron saint.

St Dwynwen

St Dwynwen is the Welsh patron saint of lovers. Her legend is associated with Llanddwyn Island, where the ruins of her church and a holy well became a place of pilgrimage and a focus for traditions about love and fortune.

A visual interpretation of St Dwynwen on Llanddwyn Island
The Legend

St Dwynwen is the Welsh patron saint of lovers — Wales's own answer to St Valentine — and her feast, Dydd Santes Dwynwen on 25 January, is kept across the country with cards and tokens of love. A princess of the fifth century, daughter of the legendary king Brychan, she fell in love with a young man named Maelon; but the match was thwarted, and in her grief Dwynwen prayed to be freed from her love. An angel, the legend tells, gave her a sweet drink that cured her longing and turned Maelon to ice.

St Dwynwen is the Welsh patron saint of lovers.

The Saint of Lovers by the Well

Granted three wishes, Dwynwen asked that Maelon be thawed, that God watch over all true lovers, and that she herself should never marry. The last she kept, withdrawing to become a nun and founding a church on the tidal island of Llanddwyn off the west coast of Anglesey, whose ruins still stand. A holy well there became famous as a place where lovers could divine the faithfulness of their sweethearts: sacred fish or eels in the well, and the movement of the water, were read for omens of love. So the heartbroken princess became the gentle guardian of all who love.

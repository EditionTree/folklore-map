St Magloire of Sark

The patron saint of Sark was a 6th-century Celtic monk who cured a chieftain's illness in exchange for land, founded a monastery there, then left even that behind to become a hermit on Jersey.

The Legend

Cousin to Saint Samson of Dol, and by tradition a student of Illtud at the monastery school of Llantwit Major in Glamorgan, Magloire crossed to Brittany as a young monk in the sixth century, one of the wandering, sea-crossing clergy who carried the early Celtic church back and forth across the western sea. There he eventually succeeded Samson as abbot and bishop of Dol.

founded a monastery on the island

The Monk of Sark

He came to Sark seeking a stricter solitude than Dol could offer. According to the island's tradition, the local chieftain Lascon gave Magloire the land for a monastery out of gratitude after the saint cured him of illness, and Magloire founded a community there said to have numbered sixty-two monks before he withdrew again, this time to Jersey, where he built a hermitage on the site of what is now Saint Helier's parish. The miracles credited to him along the way are typical of the genre: a stag that led him to fresh water, the sick healed by his touch, wild animals tamed at his approach.

The life as it survives is inseparable from legend. The earliest account, the Vita Sancti Maglorii, was not written down until the late ninth or mid-tenth century, three hundred years after Magloire's traditional death around 575, and each retelling seems to have added to the store of miracles. His relics were moved twice after his death, first around 850 to the Abbey of Lehon near Dinan, then around 963 to an abbey founded in his name in Paris, and his cult spread as far as Italy, where he is remembered as much as in his native Brittany. Sark still keeps him as its patron saint, his monastery placed by tradition near the island's modern village, and his feast falls each year on 24 October.

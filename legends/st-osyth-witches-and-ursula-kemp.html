St Osyth Witches and Ursula Kemp

A cunning woman's falling-out with a neighbour over a nursemaid's job triggered a 1582 witch-hunt that swept up fourteen women from one small Essex village.

The Legend

Ursula Kemp worked in the Essex village of St Osyth as a cunning woman, valued by her neighbours for curing illness through charms and folk remedies. Her friendship with a local woman, Grace Thurlow, soured after Ursula, having helped cure Grace's son Davy, hoped to be taken on as nursemaid to Grace's infant daughter Joan and was passed over. When baby Joan later died after falling from her cradle, suspicion fell on the spurned Ursula, and in 1582 she was brought before the Chelmsford Assizes accused of witchcraft.

The Story

Under questioning, Ursula confessed and named four other women as witches: Elizabeth Bennet, Alice Hunt, Alice Newman and Margery Sammon, who in turn implicated a further nine, dragging a total of fourteen women from the single village of St Osyth into the trial. Only Ursula Kemp and Elizabeth Bennett are known to have been sentenced to hang. The case remained more than a historical footnote: in 1921, the skeletons of two women were unearthed in a St Osyth garden by Charles Brooker, one widely believed at the time to be Ursula Kemp's own remains, pinned in the ground with iron rivets in a manner recalling folk practices for preventing revenants from rising, a grim echo, in a real prosecuted case, of the same fear of the restless dead found in more overtly supernatural folklore.

St Swithin's Day

Weather lore holds that rain on St Swithin's Day, 15 July, will continue for forty days. The tradition is linked to St Swithin, a ninth-century Bishop of Winchester, and to stories about the movement of his remains.

The Legend

St Swithin's Day, 15 July, carries one of the best-known pieces of English weather-lore: 'St Swithin's day if thou dost rain, for forty days it will remain; St Swithin's day if thou be fair, for forty days 'twill rain nae mair.' Swithin was a real ninth-century Bishop of Winchester, a saintly and humble man who, the story goes, asked to be buried not within his cathedral but outside, in a simple grave where the rain might fall on him and passers-by walk over him.

Rain on St Swithin's Day

For a century his wish was honoured, until, in 971, the monks resolved to move his remains into a splendid shrine inside the rebuilt cathedral. The day appointed for the translation, tradition says, was met by a tremendous downpour that went on for forty days, taken as a sign of the saint's displeasure at being brought in out of the weather he had chosen. From this grew the belief that the weather on his feast day sets the pattern for the forty days to follow. Meteorologically it does not hold, but as folklore it has endured for a thousand years, tying the British summer's habit of rain to a modest bishop's grave at Winchester.

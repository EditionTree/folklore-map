Stratford Lion

A spectral lion said to haunt the roads around Stratford-upon-Avon — silent, luminous-eyed, always seen alone on the road at night. Unlike the great black dogs of other counties, this phantom takes the form of a big cat, one of England's stranger and more localised legends.

The legend

The Stratford Lion is a spectral beast said to haunt the roads about Stratford-upon-Avon in Warwickshire — a great luminous-eyed lion that pads silently along the night lanes, always met by a solitary traveller and never by a crowd. It makes no sound and offers no violence, but its sudden, glowing presence on an English country road, where no lion has any earthly business, fills the witness with dread.

The story

Like the spectral black dogs that haunt so many parishes, the phantom lion seems to be a guardian or omen of the boundary places — crossroads, bridges and lonely stretches of highway. Such out-of-place apparitions, a lion in the Warwickshire dark, belong to the strand of English road-ghost lore in which the familiar route home becomes, after nightfall, the haunt of something that should not be there at all.

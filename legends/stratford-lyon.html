Stratford Lyon

A huge red lion with a wild mane and stag's antlers, pulled bodily from the New Forest earth by a landowner named Stratford around 1400 — tamed and sworn to defend his family, it is still reported around Boldre and South Baddesley today.

The Legend

Around 1400, tradition says, a New Forest landowner named Stratford was walking his grounds near South Baddesley when he found a huge pair of red antlers sticking from the earth. Pulling on them, he found they were fixed to an enormous red head — a lion's — and hauled the whole creature bodily out of the ground. It ran three times round the forest trying to throw him before Stratford brought it to heel; tamed, it pledged itself to his family's service, swearing to rise and defend them in times of need.

The Story

The Stratford Lyon is described as a huge blood-red lion with a wild, shaggy mane, great tusks, saucer-like yellow eyes, and a pair of stag's antlers crowning its head — an image with no real parallel among England's other phantom big cats, which are almost always black. The legend is anchored to Boldre and South Baddesley in the New Forest, Hampshire, close to the Red Lion pub at Boldre.

The beast is said to still walk the forest, sometimes with the ghostly figure of Stratford himself riding on its back. Reported sightings recur periodically in the local press: a 1960s cluster near the Red Lion, further reports through the 1990s, and as recently as 2021, when a visitor described 'a huge, red, animal... the size of a single-decker bus' with 'a huge, disproportionate mouth and massive stag antlers' outside the pub — details that closely match the centuries-old description, however the story first spread.

Tarroo Ushtey

The Tarroo Ushtey, or water bull, is a Manx creature said to haunt pools, swamps and coastal marshland, most famously the ground between Andreas and the sea before it was drained. Unlike its Welsh counterpart, which was said to bring fertility to cattle, the Manx water bull was blamed for begetting monstrous or deformed offspring among ordinary herds.

The Legend

The tarroo ushtey is recorded in the Manx folklore collections of A. W. Moore, whose Folk-Lore of the Isle of Man was published in 1891, and John Rhys, whose Celtic Folklore covered the same tradition a decade later. It belongs to a wider family of Celtic water bull beliefs found across Gaelic Scotland (the tarbh uisge) and Ireland (the tarbh uisce), sharing the general idea of a bull-like creature emerging from fresh or brackish water.

NEEDS EDITORIAL: a Heading for This Section

Manx accounts describe the tarroo ushtey as a large, wild-eyed animal with a fearsome bellow said to make the ground tremble, encountered around specific named pools and marshes, including ground between the village of Andreas and the sea (drained since these accounts were recorded) and locations near Ramsey and Sulby. One recorded anecdote describes a man breaking his walking stick over the creature's back after it refused to move out of his way on a road. Where mating with domestic cattle occurred, Manx tradition held that the resulting calves were misshapen or monstrous, a marked contrast to the beneficial, milk-increasing calves attributed to water bull unions in some Welsh tellings of the same broader motif.

Tavish Dhu (Black Tom), the Pirate of the Skerries

A 14th-century sea raider who plundered the Antrim coast for Robert the Bruce is said to lie buried with his silver on the Skerries off Portrush, his ghost still guarding it.

The Legend

In the early 14th century, during the wars of Scottish and Irish independence, a privateer named Tavish Dhu (or Tavish Dubh — "Black Tavish", later anglicised to Black Tom) used the Skerries, a scatter of rocky islets just off Portrush on the north Antrim coast, as his base. Sailing loosely under the banner of Robert the Bruce, he raided English shipping and settlements along the Irish Sea; in 1315 he is said to have delivered captured English supply ships to Edward Bruce's forces up the River Bann, and to have ferried Scottish troops across the water in support of the Bruce invasion of Ireland.

The Story

His career ended in 1317, when Edward II dispatched a squadron to hunt him down; after a bloody engagement in which forty of his crew were killed, Tavish was captured and executed, his head reputedly sent to Dublin as a trophy. Local tradition insists his men carried his body back to the Skerries for burial with three bullock-skins of silver, and that his ghost — and those of his crew — still stand guard over the hoard. The easternmost of the islets, Island Dubh, is said to take its name from him, as does the mainland district of Dhuvarren.

The legend has stayed remarkably alive in Portrush: it was recorded in early-20th-century local histories and is retold today by the town's heritage trust, which stages an annual "Pirates Off Portrush" festival built around his story. Whether or not the historical Tavish Dhu ever set foot on the Skerries, the tale binds a documented Bruce-era privateer to a very specific stretch of Antrim coastline, giving Portrush its own outlaw founding myth.

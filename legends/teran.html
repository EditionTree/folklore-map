Teran

The winter sea-spirit of Orkney, enemy of the Sea Mither. When Teran rules, storms rise, waters turn dangerous, and the ocean remembers its teeth.

A visual interpretation of Teran
The Legend

Teran is the malevolent winter-spirit of the Orcadian sea, the embodiment of storm, cold and the ocean's destructive rage, and the eternal enemy of the gentle Sea Mither. Where she is the warm, calming power of the summer waters, Teran is their winter fury — the gales that wreck the boats, the towering seas, the killing cold of the northern ocean in the dark months. His very name is bound up with anger and the roaring of the storm, and to the Orkney islanders he was the dread force that made the winter sea a thing of terror.

The Water Legend at the Heart of the Tale

His story is inseparable from his battle with the Sea Mither. Twice each year the two spirits clash for dominion over the sea. In the spring struggle, the Vore Tullye, the Sea Mither defeats Teran and binds him fast to the bed of the sea, where he lies pinned and raging through the summer — and the storms of summer are said to be Teran writhing against his bonds, struggling to break free. Come autumn, in the Gore Vellye, the wearied Sea Mither can hold him no longer; Teran bursts loose, overthrows her, and seizes the sea for winter, loosing the full violence of the season's gales upon the islands.

Through the winter Teran rules the waters unchallenged, until the returning strength of the Sea Mither casts him down again in spring and the cycle renews. Teran gives a face and a will to the brutal Orkney winter sea — the explanation, in the old island cosmology, for why the ocean that cradles the isles in summer turns each year into a roaring, ship-killing enemy.

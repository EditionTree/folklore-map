The Black Nun of Bonamargy

Julia McQuillan, the recluse who lived among Bonamargy Friary's ruins in her black cloak, foretold a river running red and a burning ship heralding Irish freedom, prophecies locals say came true.

The Legend

Julia McQuillan lived alone among the ruins of Bonamargy Friary near Ballycastle after the Franciscan friars who founded it departed, traditionally dated to 1642 during the Irish Confederate Wars. She belonged to the McQuillan family that had built the friary around 1500 as the seat of their lordship of the Route, and she took to wearing a dark habit and wandering the ruined cloisters alone, which earned her the name 'the Black Nun.'

The Story

Tradition credits her with the gift of prophecy. She is said to have foretold that the Tow River would one day run red, a claim locals connect to an accident two centuries later when a mill worker was dragged through a water wheel and stained the stream with his blood. She is also said to have predicted that two standing stones four miles apart, at Carnduff and Barnish, would eventually be joined, and that Ballycastle would learn of Irish independence when a ship entered the bay with its sails ablaze.

Before her death she asked, as an act of humility, to be buried at the friary's ruined west doorway so that everyone entering would walk over her grave. A holed disc-headed cross still marks the spot today, at the ruins of Bonamargy, which stand on the outskirts of Ballycastle roughly where the McQuillans first raised them five centuries ago. Her story has become a fixture of Antrim's ghost-lore, kept alive by a dedicated local-history site and repeated in regional heritage coverage, with the friary itself still spoken of locally as a place watched over by her spirit.

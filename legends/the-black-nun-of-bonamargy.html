The Black Nun of Bonamargy

Julia McQuillan, the recluse who lived among Bonamargy Friary's ruins in her black cloak, foretold a river running red and a burning ship heralding Irish freedom — prophecies locals say came true.

The Legend

Julia McQuillan, a member of the family that had founded Bonamargy Friary around 1500, chose to live alone in its ruins near Ballycastle after the Franciscan friars departed — traditionally dated to 1642, during the Irish Confederate Wars — earning the name "the Black Nun" for the dark habit she wore. Tradition credits her with the gift of prophecy: she is said to have foretold that the Tow River would one day run red, a claim locals connect to an accident two centuries later when a mill worker was dragged through a water wheel and stained the stream with his blood. She is also said to have predicted that two standing stones four miles apart, at Carnduff and Barnish, would eventually be joined, and that Ballycastle would learn of Irish independence when a ship entered the bay with its sails ablaze.

The Story

Before her death she asked, as an act of humility, to be buried at the friary's ruined west doorway so that everyone entering would walk over her grave — and a holed disc-headed cross still marks the spot today. Her story has become a fixture of Antrim ghost-lore, with the friary described locally as haunted by her spirit, and she has her own dedicated local-history site and appears in regional heritage press coverage.

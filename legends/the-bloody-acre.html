Sacred Sites Childwall, Liverpool, Merseyside

The Bloody Acre

A field beside All Saints' Church in Childwall has been called the Bloody Acre for as long as anyone has written the name down. It is usually traced to fighting in the 1640s between the parish and the new owners of land taken from the church, and cannonballs are said to have come up when houses went in along Score Lane. The ground has a reputation for refusing what is done to it, and schemes for building on it have a habit of falling through.

The Legend

All Saints' is the oldest church in Liverpool and the only medieval one left in the borough, founded in the fourteenth century and Grade I listed. The field sits immediately beside it and has served as overspill for the churchyard, which is enough on its own to give a patch of grass a certain atmosphere.

The Story

The historical kernel is reasonably solid, though not perfectly settled. Land seized from the church under Henry VIII passed to new owners, and local tradition ties the name to a clash between those owners and the parish, either the Civil War fighting of the 1640s, in the same years Prince Rupert's forces stormed Liverpool Castle a few miles away, or an earlier Reformation-era dispute remembered as the Childwall Riot. Cannonballs and long swords recovered from the field over the years have been verified and are now held in Liverpool's city museum, but which of the two conflicts they belong to has never been established. A name meaning the field where blood was shed needs no curse to explain it either way.

The curse is the later reading and it is the sort that grows backwards from the name. A parish hall was proposed for the site within living memory and did not happen, which the story absorbed as further proof. Whether the ground is unlucky or merely awkward to build on beside a Grade I listed church and a graveyard is not a question the tradition takes any interest in.

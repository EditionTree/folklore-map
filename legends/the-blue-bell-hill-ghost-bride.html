The Blue Bell Hill Ghost Bride

On the night of 19 November 1965, a car carrying three young women home from a hen party, including bride to be Suzanne Browne, crashed on the A229 at Blue Bell Hill, killing all three. Since then, drivers on the road have repeatedly reported a young woman in white who appears suddenly in front of vehicles, asks for or accepts a lift, and then vanishes.

The Legend

Suzanne Browne, due to marry the following day, was travelling with Judith Lingham and Patricia Ferguson from Browne's hen night in Rochester when their Ford Cortina collided with an oncoming Jaguar on the A229 at Blue Bell Hill. Ferguson died at the scene; Browne and Lingham died of their injuries in hospital days later. The crash, and the fact that a wedding never took place, has been treated locally as the origin of the vanishing hitchhiker tradition that followed.

The Story

Reports of a ghostly female figure on the road began within a few years and have recurred over subsequent decades. In one frequently retold account from 1971, motorist James Skene stopped to give a young woman a lift into Chatham, only to find she had disappeared from the car before he arrived. In another from 1972, driver Bob Vandepeer reported picking up a hitchhiker who vanished from the back seat during the journey. Some tellings connect the haunting to an earlier, unconnected fatal accident on the same stretch of road, and the exact origin remains debated locally, but the 1965 crash is the best documented event tied to the modern form of the legend.

The Buggane of St Trinian's

A fearsome shape-shifting spirit haunting the ruined church of St Trinian's on the Isle of Man, repeatedly tearing off its roof whenever rebuilt. The church has stood roofless for centuries.

The legend

The Buggane is the great malevolent goblin or ogre-spirit of Manx folklore, a huge, hairy, shape-shifting creature of enormous strength and foul temper, and its most famous tale is bound to the roofless church of St Trinian's, which stands abandoned beneath Greeba Mountain on the Isle of Man. The legend explains the ruin: every time the church was given a roof, the Buggane that lurked beneath the ground rose up in a fury and tore it off again, so that the building could never be completed.

The story

The best-loved version tells of a bold tailor named Timothy, who wagered that he could spend the night alone in the church and sew a pair of breeches there without fear. As he stitched by candlelight, the floor heaved and the Buggane began to rise — first a great shaggy head, then a body, growing more monstrous as it demanded to know what the tailor was doing in its church. Timothy sewed faster and faster, keeping his nerve and his needle going, until the very last stitch was set — then snatched up his work and bolted for the door. The enraged Buggane gave chase, but the tailor reached consecrated ground (in some tellings, leaping into the churchyard or beyond a boundary the spirit could not cross), and the Buggane, baulked, tore off its own head and hurled it after him before sinking back into the earth with a roar that brought the roof down for the last time.

St Trinian's has stood open to the sky ever since, its very roofless state offered as proof of the legend. The Buggane itself remained a byword on the Island for any fearsome supernatural bogey, invoked to frighten children and to explain ruin, storm and ill luck.

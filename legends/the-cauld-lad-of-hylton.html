The Cauld Lad of Hylton

The spirit of a stable boy killed by a lord of Hylton Castle. He haunted the kitchens, tidying if work was left undone — until given a cloak and hood, whereupon he vanished forever.

The Legend

The Cauld Lad of Hylton — the 'Cold Lad' — is the famous spirit of Hylton Castle near Sunderland, in the old county of Durham, and one of the best-known household hauntings of north-east England. In life, the tradition says, he was a young stable-boy named Robert Skelton, killed by the Baron of Hylton in a fit of temper — struck down, or run through with a sword, perhaps for being slow with a horse — and his body hidden, thrown into a pond or buried, so that the lad was murdered and never decently laid to rest.

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

His ghost haunted the castle ever after, and behaved in the manner of a brownie as much as a vengeful spirit. The servants would hear him at night in the kitchen: if they had left the room tidy, the Cauld Lad would mischievously throw everything into disorder, scattering the pewter and the ashes; but if they had left it in a mess, he would set it all to rights before morning, working away unseen. He was sometimes heard, shivering, to lament his own cold and naked state — 'Wae's me, wae's me...' — a wail that gave him his name.

As with the classic brownie, the way to be rid of him was to give him a gift of clothing. The servants, pitying his cold, left out a green cloak and hood for him by the hearth. At midnight the Cauld Lad came, put them on, and was heard to rejoice that he was now properly dressed — declaring that, having received his reward, he must now depart — and with that he vanished from Hylton Castle and was never seen or heard there again. The tale neatly fuses the murdered-servant ghost with the brownie laid by a gift of clothes, two great strands of British household folklore.

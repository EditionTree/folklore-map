The Cooneen Ghost

After Michael Murphy died in 1907, his widow's cottage near Brookeborough erupted into decades of knocking, flying dishes, and levitating beds — one of Ireland's best-documented poltergeist cases.

The Legend

Michael Murphy of Cornarooslan townland, near Brookeborough, died from a head injury on 7 November 1907, leaving his pregnant wife Bridget with what would become eight children on a modest farm. Not long after, the family began reporting disembodied knocking and footsteps in the loft, followed by escalating disturbances: crockery flying across rooms, bedsteads lifting off the floor, and unexplained music. Two exorcisms performed around 1910-1912 by Fr Eugene Coyle, with the bishop's own permission, failed to quiet the house, and local MP Cahir Healy is recorded among those who witnessed the phenomena firsthand.

The Story

Ostracised and desperate, Bridget emigrated to America with her children in 1913 — but the disturbances reportedly followed them aboard ship, with the captain himself reporting strange noises during the crossing. One daughter is said to have spent the rest of her life in an American institution, her trauma blamed on the years of upheaval. The case is unusually well anchored in the historical record: the 1901 and 1911 Irish censuses confirm the family, and Michael's death certificate corroborates the fatal injury.

The case was covered at the time by the Fermanagh Herald and Impartial Reporter, and later collected in Sir Shane Leslie's Ghost Book (1956), the earliest literary compilation of the story. The abandoned cottage lay hidden under overgrowth for decades until forestry clearing exposed it again in 2016, and it remains a site of local pilgrimage and paranormal interest, including a 2010 BBC-featured investigation.

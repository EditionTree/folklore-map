The Ghost of Hellens Manor

Locked in her room for thirty years after an unsanctioned marriage, Hetty Walwyn scratched her despair into the windowpane with a diamond ring — and is said to haunt the chamber still.

The Legend

Hellens Manor at Much Marcle, first recorded in 1180, is home to one of Herefordshire's best-known ghost stories. Mehetabel "Hetty" Walwyn, the youngest daughter of the 17th-century owner, eloped with a man her family considered beneath her station; by the time she was twenty her husband had died, and she returned home to find her family unforgiving. They locked her in an upstairs bedchamber for the following thirty years as punishment for the disgrace, giving her only a bell to summon servants. She is said to have gone mad in confinement, and to have scratched a mournful verse into the windowpane with her diamond ring: "it is a part of virtue to abstain from what we love if it should prove our bane." Tradition holds that she eventually took her own life in the room.

The Story

Hetty's ghost is said to still haunt her old bedchamber; the current custodian of Hellens has reported sensing her presence while sleeping in the room above. A second, unrelated haunting is attached to the house: an elderly Catholic priest sheltering at Hellens during the Civil War was reportedly cornered and killed by Parliamentarian soldiers in a separate room.

The story is recorded across multiple independent paranormal-history and local-history sources, distinct from Herefordshire's better-known Mordiford dragon and Black Vaughan legends already on the map.

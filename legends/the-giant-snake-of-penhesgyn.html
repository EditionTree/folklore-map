The Giant Snake of Penhesgyn

A venomous giant viper terrorised a Penmynydd farm until a villager tricked it into striking its own reflection in a polished brass pan — but a fortune-teller's prophecy still claimed a life.

The Legend

Tradition tells of a huge, poisonous serpent that took up residence in the wilder corners of Penhesgyn farm, in the parish of Penmynydd on Anglesey, terrorising the local community with its size and venom. A fortune teller warned the farm's owners that the snake would kill their only son, so the family sent him away to England for safety.

The Story

A local villager devised a trick to be rid of the creature: he dug a deep pit and set within it a large, shallow brass pan, polished until it shone like a mirror. Mistaking its own reflection for a rival, the viper attacked the pan again and again until it had exhausted itself and spent its venom, at which point the villager killed it with a single blow. The prophecy proved impossible to escape, though: years later the son returned home, visited the burial spot, and contemptuously kicked the serpent's skull — a splinter of which pierced his thin shoe and delivered a final dose of venom that killed him within hours.

The tale is a distinctively local variant of Wales's broader serpent-and-dragon folklore (echoing the Dinas Emrys tradition), and it is recorded independently in both a dedicated Anglesey local-history site and a folklore blog.

The Gwybr of Llanrhaeadr

A winged serpent living in the lake above Wales's tallest waterfall swooped down to carry off villagers, until they tricked it into impaling itself on hidden spikes.

The Legend

Above Pistyll Rhaeadr — at over 240 feet the tallest waterfall in Wales, near Llanrhaeadr-ym-Mochnant in Powys — local tradition places the lair of a winged serpent called the Gwybr, said to have lived in Llyn Luncaws, the lake feeding the falls. The Gwybr was no shy lake-dweller: it flew down from its pool to seize children, women and livestock from the village below, carrying them back up to the lake to be devoured, terrorising the community for years.

The Story

The villagers eventually turned the creature's own aggression against it. According to the story, they built a decoy — a mock dragon set with hidden iron spikes — and needled the real Gwybr into attacking it. The creature threw itself at its rival with such force that it impaled itself on the spikes and died, freeing the village from its reign of terror. The tale belongs to a small but distinct cluster of North and Mid Wales dragon-and-waterfall legends, sitting alongside the much more famous Dinas Emrys story, but anchored to its own waterfall and its own named creature rather than retelling the red-and-white dragon myth.

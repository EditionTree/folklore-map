The Hairy Hands

On a lonely stretch of Dartmoor road, a pair of disembodied hairy hands are said to seize the steering or handlebars of passing travellers and wrench them off the road to their doom.

The Legend

The Hairy Hands haunt a stretch of the B3212 road across Dartmoor, between Postbridge and Two Bridges, where a run of crashes from around 1910 gave rise to one of the moor's most unsettling legends. The most notorious was fatal: in June 1921, E. H. Helby, medical officer at Dartmoor Prison, was thrown from his motorcycle and killed on the road, though his two children survived unharmed in the sidecar. The story broke nationally that October, when the journalist T. Gifford wrote it up for the Daily Mail under the headline 'Unseen Hands — Eerie Mystery of Devon Road,' turning a string of local accidents into a named legend: witnesses described a disembodied pair of large, muscular, hairy hands that would suddenly appear and grip the steering wheel or handlebars, forcing the vehicle off the road.

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

Reports multiplied through the 1920s — a caravan was said to be visited at night by the hands pressing against the window — and the local belief grew that some malevolent presence on that bleak crossing of the moor reached out for the unwary. The road was eventually re-cambered, and the crashes were doubtless owed to a genuinely dangerous bend, but the Hairy Hands have kept their grip on Dartmoor's imagination as a peculiarly modern haunting, a ghost of the motor age clinging to one of the loneliest roads in England.

The Hogboon of Helliehow

A mound-dwelling guardian spirit kept Helliehow farm prosperous for generations in exchange for offerings of milk and ale — until a disrespectful new wife let the farm fall to ruin.

The Legend

The hogboon (from Old Norse haug-búi, "mound-dweller") is Orkney's name for a family guardian spirit believed to live in an earthen mound beside a farm. At Helliehow, a farm on the island of Sanday, tradition held that the resident hogboon protected the household and ensured good fortune so long as the family shared its produce with him — pouring milk or ale over his mound and leaving butter and bannocks as offerings after a birth or marriage.

The Story

The story's turning point comes when a new wife arrives at Helliehow and refuses to keep up the old customs, withholding the hogboon's due. The farm's fortunes collapse soon after and it falls into ruin, told locally as a cautionary demonstration of what happens when the old obligations to the mound-spirit are broken. The tale belongs to a wider Orcadian tradition of named hogboons tied to specific farms (a similar figure is recorded at Maeshowe itself, described in an 1862 antiquarian account as a supernaturally strong "person named Hogboy"), but the Helliehow version is one of the best-preserved individual accounts, having been collected in published local folklore anthologies.

The Loe

Cornwall's largest natural freshwater lake, separated from the sea by a shingle bar. Local legend links it to Arthurian tradition, including claims that Excalibur was cast into its waters.

A visual interpretation of The Loe
The Legend

The Loe, also called Loe Pool, is the largest natural freshwater lake in Cornwall, lying near Helston and separated from the open sea only by a great shingle bank, the Loe Bar, which dams the River Cober behind it. Wild, beautiful and prone to flooding, the pool has gathered legend to itself, and is among the several waters of the West Country that lay claim to one of the most resonant moments in British myth — the return of Excalibur.

The Water Legend at the Heart of the Tale

In the Cornish version of the legend, it was into the waters of Loe Pool, rather than Dozmary or a Welsh lake, that the dying King Arthur commanded Sir Bedivere to cast the great sword Excalibur after the last battle, and from whose surface a hand rose to catch the blade and draw it beneath — making the Loe one of the candidate sites for the Lady of the Lake's water. The nearness of the pool to other Arthurian sites in Cornwall lends the claim its local force.

The Loe carries darker traditions too. The treacherous Loe Bar, which has wrecked ships and drowned many — including, in one notorious disaster, much of the crew of a wrecked warship — was said in folklore to demand a toll of lives, claiming victims at intervals, and the pool was linked to the restless spirit of the wicked Cornish ghost Jan Tregeagle, set to labour at impossible tasks along that coast. Between Arthurian glamour and grimmer legends of drowning and doom, the Loe holds a powerful place in Cornish lore.

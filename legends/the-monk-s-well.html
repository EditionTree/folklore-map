Sacred Sites Wavertree, Liverpool, Merseyside

The Monk's Well

A sandstone well on Mill Lane in Wavertree carries a Latin line cut in 1414. Qui non dat quod habet, daemon infra ridet, which runs roughly as he who gives not what he has, the Devil below laughs at him. Travellers who drank at the spring were expected to leave alms for the monks, and the inscription tells them exactly who is listening when they walk on without paying.

The Legend

The well is Grade II listed and was built in the early fifteenth century. Steps once went down to the spring itself and a cross stood over the structure carrying a second and gentler inscription, Deus dedit, homo bibit, God gives and man drinks. An iron pump was fitted in 1835 to save people the descent.

The Story

By 1893, when Robert Charles Hope gathered it into his Legendary Lore of the Holy Wells of England, the cross had already gone. It was replaced later. The well no longer runs and the basin is planted as a flower bed, so the threat is now addressed to people waiting at a bus stop.

What makes it unusual among British holy wells is that the folklore was never recovered from oral tradition at all. It is cut into the stone, in the fifteenth century, in the voice of the institution that wanted the money. Most wells had a story attached to them afterwards by somebody else. This one arrived with its own.

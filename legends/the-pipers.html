The Pipers

The Pipers are a pair of standing stones near The Hurlers stone circles, located on Bodmin Moor near the village of Minions, Cornwall, UK. They share the name with another pair of standing stones near the Merry Maidens to the south of the village of St Buryan, also in Cornwall.

The Legend

The Pipers are a pair of tall granite standing stones on Bodmin Moor, set a little to the west of the three stone circles called the Hurlers, near the village of Minions in Cornwall. They take their name from the same legend that named the circles beside them: a Cornish tale of men and music punished for breaking the holy day.

The Story

According to the story, a group of local men were playing the old Cornish game of hurling on a Sunday — a grievous profanation of the Sabbath — while two pipers played music for them. For their sin they were all struck and turned to stone where they stood: the hurlers becoming the rings of upright stones now known as the Hurlers, and the two musicians the pair of taller pillars set apart to the west, the Pipers. The antiquarian William Camden recorded the belief as early as 1610, noting that the country people held the stones to be men 'transformed into stones for profaning the Lord's Day.'

The legend is a classic example of how the medieval and early-modern Church reinterpreted prehistoric monuments as moral warnings against pagan revelry and Sabbath-breaking. The Hurlers themselves are an exceptional site — a rare linear alignment of three Bronze Age circles, unique in England — and the Pipers, standing sentinel to the west, anchor the whole group to one of Cornwall's most enduring petrifaction tales.

The Pirates' Ambush at Sark

In 1356 pirates who had seized Sark were tricked and slaughtered by merchants who smuggled swords ashore inside a fake funeral coffin.

The Legend

During the depths of the Hundred Years' War, with plague, French raids and Scottish attacks emptying Sark of its population, the island fell into the hands of pirates and wreckers who lit false beacons on stormy nights to lure passing ships onto its rocks. England's garrison amounted to little more than a handful of archers, leaving the pirates effectively in control. In 1356, so the story goes, a party of merchants from the Cinque Ports towns of Rye and Winchelsea decided to take the island back by trickery rather than force.

The Story

They sailed to Sark and told the pirates that their captain had died at sea, asking permission to bury him according to Christian rite at St Magloire's chapel. The pirates, promised payment and having searched the newcomers for weapons, allowed them to carry the coffin up the island's 300-foot cliff path into the chapel. Once inside, during what looked like a private funeral service, the merchants broke open the coffin — packed not with a body but with swords and longbows — armed themselves, and burst out to slaughter the pirate garrison. Only one elderly woman is said to have survived, escaping with a loaf of bread to hide in the Convanche Chasm until a passing Guernsey fisherman rescued her.

Historians have long noted how closely the tale echoes the Trojan Horse, and it has been current on Sark for at least four centuries — Sir Walter Raleigh recycled essentially the same coffin-and-weapons trick in his History of the World to describe Flemish corsairs retaking Sark from the French in 1553, suggesting the story had already become a kind of stock local legend for how the tiny island changes hands. The island's vulnerability to exactly this kind of seizure persisted for two more centuries, until Elizabeth I granted Sark by royal patent to Hélier de Carteret in 1565 on condition he settle it permanently with forty armed tenants.

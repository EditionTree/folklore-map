Ghosts Queensway Tunnel, Liverpool and Birkenhead, Merseyside

The Queensway Tunnel Hitchhiker

A figure stands out in the carriageway inside the Mersey road tunnel with a hand raised for a lift. Drivers who stop and carry the hitchhiker under the river find the seat beside them empty before they reach daylight at the far end. Swerving to miss the figure has caused wrecks of its own, which is the part of the story that gets repeated most.

The Legend

The tunnel was dug between 1925 and 1934 and seventeen men were killed building it. Two miles of tiled roadway under a tidal river, with no daylight and nowhere to stop, is close to ideal ground for a story of this kind, and the tunnel has attracted several.

The Story

There are two hitchhikers and they are told interchangeably. In one, a man killed in the tunnel in the middle of the last century gets in, talks to the driver and is gone before the exit. In the other, told more often on the Wirral side, a young woman came off the back of a motorbike in the 1960s and died of her injuries, and was afterwards seen standing in the road by a run of motorists and by the tunnel police.

The vanishing hitchhiker is one of the most widely travelled legends in the world and turns up wherever there is a stretch of road people are frightened of. The Queensway version is worth recording for how firmly it has fastened to one particular tunnel, complete with a named decade and an accident specific enough to sound checkable.

Origins & Dating

The Ratman of Southend

A modern urban legend from Southend-on-Sea in Essex: a half-human, half-rat creature said to lurk in the storm drains and sewers beneath the seafront. One of the more persistent pieces of contemporary English folklore, kept alive on local social media and in playground tradition.

The Legend

The Ratman of Southend is a modern urban legend of the Essex seaside town, a half-human, half-rat creature said to lurk in the storm drains, railway arches and sewers beneath Southend-on-Sea. Stories describe a hunched, matted, red-eyed figure that scavenges by night and lunges at those who venture into the tunnels, especially around the old arches near the seafront and the railway.

The Haunting at the Heart of the Tale

Two distinct origin stories circulate for the same underpass. In the more common version, a homeless man sheltering from the cold was set upon by a gang of youths and beaten until he died of cold and blood loss, after which rats were said to gather and devour his face, transforming him into the creature that still haunts the tunnel. A stranger, less-told version blames a philandering past mayor of Southend, whose mistress bore him a child with severe deformities; in this telling the mayor used his influence to have the underpass built with a hidden chamber to conceal the boy away from the town.

Spread by word of mouth and, latterly, the internet, the Ratman shows the old machinery of folklore still at work in modern towns, the dark, forbidden, underground place given a monster to guard it, and a frisson of dread to keep the curious away.

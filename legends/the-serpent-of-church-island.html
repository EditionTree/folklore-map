The Serpent of Church Island

Beneath a great stone on this Lough Gill island lies a crock of gold guarded by a serpent — legend insists a life must be lost before anyone can take it.

The Legend

Church Island, the largest of the wooded islands scattered across Lough Gill outside Sligo town, carries a double legend. Historically it was the site of a monastery founded in the sixth century by St Lomán of Trim, later home to a bardic school run by the O'Cuirnín family and destroyed by fire in 1416 — a blaze the Annals of the Four Masters record as having consumed a legendary harp, an ornamental cup, and the manuscript known as the Leabhar Gearr along with the church itself. It is out of the wreck of that lost wealth that the island's folk legend grows.

The Story

Local tradition holds that a crock of gold — some say the remnants of the monastery's own treasures — lies hidden beneath a large stone on the island, guarded by a serpent coiled beneath it. The guardianship comes with a grim condition: a life must be lost before the gold can ever be recovered. The story is kept alive by a widely repeated modern anecdote in which two botanists, searching the island for rare mosses, were caught in a sudden thunderstorm and found dead the next day — a tragedy locals folded straight into the old curse as proof it still holds.

Church Island's serpent belongs to a distinctively Irish strand of dragon lore: rather than a fire-breathing beast, it is one of the country's many treasure-guarding "ollphéisteanna", a lake-bound worm whose danger lies less in tooth and claw than in the price it exacts from anyone greedy enough to go looking for what it protects.

The White Trout of Cong

A noblewoman whose betrothed was murdered and thrown into a Cong lake vanished after him — and reappeared forever after as a white trout that bleeds when cut and heals when returned to the water.

The Legend

According to the tale as collected by Samuel Lover from local storytellers and later reprinted by W. B. Yeats in his 1888 anthology of Irish peasant folklore, a beautiful young woman near Cong was promised in marriage to a king's son. When her betrothed was murdered and his body thrown into the lake, grief drove her out of her mind, and she disappeared — swallowed, tradition says, by the fairies who followed him into the water. From that day a white trout appeared in the lake and was never caught, since the people came to believe it was no ordinary fish.

The Story

The legend's most vivid episode concerns a soldier who caught the trout regardless and set it in a frying pan to cook. No matter how long he cooked it, the fish would not brown — until he cut into it with a knife, at which point it screamed, leapt from the pan, and became a bleeding woman in white and gold, who rebuked him for the wound before asking to be returned to the water. When he threw the trout back, the lake ran red for a time, and the fish is said to carry the scar of the cut to this day.

The tale is one of the most frequently anthologised pieces of Connacht folklore, distinct from — though sometimes confused with — the region's separate Dobhar-chú legend from County Leitrim.

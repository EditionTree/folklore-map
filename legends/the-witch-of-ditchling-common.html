The Witch of Ditchling Common

A Sussex witch who turned into a hare by night was unmasked when the hare, bitten while fleeing hunters' dogs, showed the same wound as an old woman's leg the next morning.

The Legend

Ditchling Common in East Sussex carries a pair of linked witch legends recorded by local heritage bodies. In the best-known version, a hare seen bounding across the common at night was long suspected by locals of being an old woman from the area in disguise. When men hunting with dogs cornered and bit the hare on the leg one night, they let it escape — and the following day, an old woman in the village was seen limping home nursing an injured ankle, confirming what everyone had already believed. The shapeshifting-witch-as-hare motif recurs across Sussex folklore, but the Ditchling Common telling is distinguished by its specific, verifiable injury as proof, a common device in English witch legend used to convert suspicion into supposed evidence.

The Story

A second, related tradition ties an unnamed witch of Ditchling Common to a different power entirely: the ability to stop carts and wagons dead in the road. According to the story, however hard the horses pulled, a cart could not be made to move past a certain point on the common until the witch's spell was lifted — a variant on the widespread English 'stayed cart' witch legend, in which a witch demonstrates her power over travellers passing through her territory rather than over animals or the weather. Together the two tales place Ditchling Common within the broader documented tradition of South Downs witch-lore that the South Downs National Park Authority has actively catalogued in recent years.

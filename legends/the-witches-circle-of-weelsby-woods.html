The Witches Circle of Weelsby Woods

A ring in Grimsby's Weelsby Woods known as the Witches Circle, where covens are said to have met since the woods opened to the public in 1951. Locals still dispute what really happens there.

The Legend

Weelsby Woods on the edge of Grimsby is home to a distinctive local legend: a clearing known as the Witches Circle, previously called the Fairy Ring until around the 1950s. According to local tradition, practitioners of witchcraft have gathered there on Friday and Saturday evenings since the woods opened to the public in 1951, performing rituals inside the circle. The tradition took on extra colour once Gerald Gardner's Wicca movement became publicly known in the mid-20th century, with the circle becoming associated with organised modern Pagan witchcraft rather than older cunning-folk practice.

The Story

The legend has generated persistent local disputes: rumours of animal sacrifice at the site have circulated for decades, consistently denied by practising Wiccans who point out that sacrifice plays no part in their religion. Some locals instead believe the circle to be an Anglo-Saxon burial mound reused by later visitors, though no archaeological evidence has been found to confirm this, and the site's true origin remains disputed among local historians. Ghost-hunting groups have also investigated the location, reporting unexplained EVP responses, adding a paranormal layer to the folklore.

Whatever its true origin, the Witches Circle has become a well-known piece of Grimsby's local identity, straddling the line between genuine folk memory and a modern urban legend that grew up around a patch of woodland after it was opened to the public.

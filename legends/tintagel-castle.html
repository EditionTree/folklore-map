Tintagel Castle

A cliff-edge stronghold made famous by Geoffrey of Monmouth as the place of Arthur's conception. Its sea-cut ruins keep Arthurian legend pinned to the Cornish coast.

The Legend

Tintagel Castle is the ruined medieval stronghold dramatically sited on and around the great headland at Tintagel on the north Cornish coast, its broken walls straddling the narrow neck of rock between the mainland and the near-island promontory above the sea. Built in the 13th century by Richard, Earl of Cornwall, the castle was raised in large part because of the place's existing fame in legend — Richard wanting a seat at the spot already celebrated as the birthplace of King Arthur.

Its sea-cut ruins keep Arthurian legend pinned to the Cornish coast.

Arthur's Castle Above the Sea

That fame came from Geoffrey of Monmouth, who in his History of the Kings of Britain made Tintagel the scene of Arthur's conception: King Uther Pendragon, consumed with desire for Igerna (Igraine), wife of Gorlois, Duke of Cornwall, was magically disguised by Merlin to take the form of her husband and so gained entry to the supposedly impregnable cliff-fortress of Tintagel, where Arthur was conceived. The castle's cliff-edge drama and its sea-cut isolation made it the perfect setting for this tale of magic, desire and destiny.

The site's romance is deepened by real history: excavations have revealed that the headland was a major centre in the 5th and 6th centuries, importing fine pottery and goods from the Mediterranean, a genuinely important place in exactly the post-Roman era to which Arthur belongs. With Merlin's Cave in the rock below and the Atlantic breaking around it, Tintagel Castle keeps the Arthurian legend pinned to the Cornish coast, its sea-battered ruins the enduring emblem of the once and future king's beginnings.

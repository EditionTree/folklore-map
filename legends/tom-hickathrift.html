Tom Hickathrift

Tom Hickathrift is a folk hero of the Fenlands, famed for extraordinary strength. In the best-known tale, he killed an ogre terrorising the Smeeth near Wisbech using a cart wheel and axle as an improvised shield and club. His story appears in early chapbooks and was collected by Joseph Jacobs.

A visual interpretation of Tom Hickathrift fighting the giant of the Marshland
The Legend

Tom Hickathrift is the giant-killing folk hero of the Fenland of Norfolk and the Isle of Ely, a poor labourer's son of prodigious strength and appetite. Lazy and slow until his power was roused, he could carry loads twenty men could not lift, and the chapbooks delighted in his feats — moving stacks of straw, felling trees by the armful, knocking down all comers at cudgels and football.

Tom upended his cart, took the axle for a club and a wheel for a shield, and beat the giant to death, winning his hoard and his land for the people.

The Giant on the Marsh Road

His great deed was the slaying of the giant of the Marshland, an ogre who exacted toll and terror over Wisbech and the surrounding fens. Carting beer one day, Tom took the road across the giant's ground; when the monster came roaring out, Tom upended his cart, took the axle for a club and a wheel for a shield, and beat the giant to death, winning his hoard and his land for the people. The tale, hugely popular in cheap print, made Tom one of England's best-loved native giant-killers, and a great stone slab in Tilney churchyard was long shown as his grave.

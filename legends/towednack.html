Towednack

A Cornish parish rich in tales of piskies, saints, and old stone-country spirits. Its church and surrounding moorland belong to the strange magical geography of West Penwith.

The Legend

Towednack is a small, scattered parish in the high moorland country of West Penwith near St Ives, in Cornwall, long famous as a last stronghold of old Cornish superstition and custom — and above all for its Cuckoo Feast, one of the best-loved of Cornish folk traditions. The feast celebrates a charming local legend about the bringing of spring.

Towednack belongs to the stone-country geography of saints, piskies, and old spirits.

The Magical Parish

The story tells that on a cold and dismal day in early April, a Towednack farmer had gathered his friends about his hearth for company against the bitter weather, and to liven the fire he threw on a hollow log. As the flames took hold, a cuckoo — the great herald of the Cornish spring — suddenly flew out of the burning log and away, and at that very moment the weather turned mild and spring-like. Delighted, the farmer resolved to gather his friends every year to mark the day, and so the Cuckoo Feast was founded, held ever since on the Sunday nearest the 28th of April, with music (on the Cornish fiddle, the 'crowd', giving it the alternative name of the 'Crowder Feast'), food and good fellowship to welcome the spring.

The tale was first set down in print by Robert Hunt in Popular Romances of the West of England (1865) and again by M. A. Courtney in Cornish Feasts and Folk-Lore (1890), both recording it as an already-established local custom rather than something either writer invented. Towednack was rich in other lore too — the haunt of piskies and the setting of the comic tale of the 'Sham Mayors' of Towednack — and the parish kept its reputation for the old beliefs long after they faded elsewhere. The Cuckoo Feast, still kept today, is a delightful survival of the old Cornish calendar customs, in which the first call of the cuckoo and the breaking of winter are bound together in a story of a bird loosed from a burning log.

Twelve Apostles, West Yorkshire

The stones on Rombalds Moor take their name from the apostles, but older folklore blames the giant Rombald, whose wife split the nearby Cow and Calf rocks while chasing him in a rage.

The Legend

Twelve standing stones, all that survive of an original ring that nineteenth-century accounts put at sixteen to twenty, stand on Rombalds Moor between Ilkley and Burley in Wharfedale, high enough at 381 metres to catch the wind off the valley below. Built from local millstone grit around 1800 BC, in the early Bronze Age, the circle takes its familiar name from a rough count of its remaining stones, read as the twelve apostles.

The low ring of stones keeps watch over the high ground above Wharfedale.

The Circle on the Moor

The circle has never been formally excavated, only surveyed, and its more recent history is oddly uncertain for a monument this well known. By 1946, when the local archaeologist Eric Cowling recorded the site, every stone had fallen; who re-erected them afterwards, and exactly when, has never been established. Nineteenth-century descriptions mention an earthen bank surrounding the stones that would have given the whole monument a diameter of around 22 metres, but almost no trace of it survives today, and the ring itself is known not to stand in its original position, having been moved and repaired more than once over the centuries.

The moor's older name comes from folklore rather than scripture. Local tradition holds that a giant called Rombald once lived here, and that his wife, pursuing him across the moor in a fury, kicked apart a pair of nearby boulders known ever since as the Cow and Calf. The circle keeps stranger company still: in recent decades it has drawn its share of reported UFO sightings, a modern layer added to a moor already thick with story.

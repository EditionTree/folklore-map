Twelve Apostles, West Yorkshire

The stones on Rombalds Moor take their name from the apostles, but older folklore blames the giant Rombald, whose wife split the nearby Cow and Calf rocks while chasing him in a rage.

The Legend

Twelve standing stones, all that survive of an original ring of sixteen to twenty, stand on Rombalds Moor between Ilkley and Burley in Wharfedale, high enough at 381 metres to catch the wind off the valley below. Built from local millstone grit in the Bronze Age, the circle takes its familiar name from a rough count of its remaining stones, read as the twelve apostles, though every stone had fallen by the mid-twentieth century and the circle has since been re-erected more than once.

The low ring of stones keeps watch over the high ground above Wharfedale.

The Circle on the Moor

The moor's older name comes from folklore rather than scripture. Local tradition holds that a giant called Rombald once lived here, and that his wife, pursuing him across the moor in a fury, kicked apart a pair of nearby boulders known ever since as the Cow and Calf. The circle keeps stranger company still: in recent decades it has drawn its share of reported UFO sightings, a modern layer added to a moor already thick with story.

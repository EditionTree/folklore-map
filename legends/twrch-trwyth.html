Twrch Trwyth

TOORKH TROO-ith

Twrch Trwyth is the enchanted boar hunted by Arthur's warband in the Welsh tale Culhwch and Olwen. Carrying a comb, razor and shears between his ears, the creature lands near Porth Clais and leaves a destructive trail across Wales before fleeing toward Cornwall.

A visual interpretation of Twrch Trwyth near Porth Clais in Pembrokeshire
The Legend

Twrch Trwyth is the great enchanted boar at the heart of the early Welsh tale Culhwch and Olwen, one of the oldest Arthurian stories. Once a king transformed for his sins into a monstrous swine, he carries between his ears a comb, a razor and a pair of shears, and the winning of these treasures from his bristling head is among the impossible tasks set the hero Culhwch before he may marry the giant's daughter Olwen.

Carrying a comb, razor and shears between his ears, the creature lands near Porth Clais and leaves a destructive trail across Wales before fleeing toward Cornwall.

The Hunt Across Britain

Arthur himself musters a vast warband and the hound Cafall for the hunt, which sweeps from Ireland across the sea to Wales and on through the south-west of Britain in a running battle that costs many lives and leaves its mark on the very landscape — place after place is named for where the boar turned, or where a champion fell. The treasures are wrested from him one by one before he plunges at last into the sea off Cornwall and is lost. The hunt of Twrch Trwyth preserves a heroic, mythic Arthur far older than the courtly king of later romance.

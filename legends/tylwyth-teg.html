Tylwyth Teg

TUH-looith TEHG

The Fair Family of Welsh folklore — beautiful golden-haired fae who danced on moonlit hills and beneath lake surfaces. They stole human children and left changelings in their place. To see them was magical; to follow them was to lose days, years, or your mind entirely.

A visual interpretation of the Fair Family dancing in the Welsh hills
The Legend

The Tylwyth Teg — the 'Fair Family' — are the fairies of Welsh tradition, the most common of the many euphemisms by which the Welsh avoided naming them outright. Pictured as beautiful and fair-haired, often dressed in white, they danced in rings on the moonlit hillsides and meadows and dwelt in the otherworld of Annwn, beneath the lakes and within the green mounds.

A mortal drawn into their ring to dance might find, on stepping out, that a single night had been a hundred years.

A Night in the Fairy Ring

Like fairies everywhere they were both alluring and perilous. A mortal drawn into their ring to dance might find, on stepping out, that a single night had been a hundred years; they rewarded tidiness and generosity with luck and silver, but punished meanness, and above all they stole — taking unbaptised infants and leaving a withered changeling, or carrying off young men and women into their world. The Bendith y Mamau ('Mothers' Blessing') of Glamorgan and the lake-maidens, the Gwragedd Annwn, are local branches of the same Fair Family, and the careful courtesy the Welsh showed them reflects a belief that the land was shared with a hidden people best kept friendly.

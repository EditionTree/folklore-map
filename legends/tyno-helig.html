Tyno Helig

TUH-no HEL-ig

A Welsh legend tells of the lost land of Tyno Helig and the palace of Prince Helig ap Glannawg beneath Conwy Bay, off the Great Orme. The tale is sometimes described as a Welsh Atlantis tradition.

The Legend

Tyno Helig is the legendary lost land of the north Welsh coast, a Welsh counterpart to the drowned kingdoms of Cantre'r Gwaelod and Cornish Lyonesse. Tradition tells of a low-lying, fertile realm that once stretched out into what is now Conwy Bay, off the Great Orme, ruled from the palace of a prince named Helig ap Glannog — a rich and prosperous country that was overwhelmed and lost beneath the sea.

The Story

The legend gives the drowning a moral cause. It was said that a curse lay upon the house of Helig, called down by a wrong done generations before — an injustice or a murder — and that it was prophesied the sin would be punished upon his descendants. The doom fell at a great feast in the palace: the sea rose and broke in, flooding the hall and the whole low country in a single catastrophic night, drowning the land and its people, with only a few escaping to the higher ground. The waters never receded, and Tyno Helig was lost for good beneath the bay.

As with the other Welsh and Cornish drowned-land legends, tradition held that traces of the lost kingdom could still be seen or sensed — ruins or walls glimpsed beneath the water at low tide, taken as the remains of Helig's palace. Often described as a 'Welsh Atlantis', Tyno Helig belongs to the powerful British folklore of submerged lands punished for sin, the sea standing as the agent of a long-delayed judgement upon a cursed royal house.

Unseelie Court

The dark host of Scottish fairy lore, malevolent fae who needed no reason to harm mortals. They flew through the winter night as the Sluagh, pelting travellers with fairy shot. No offering could appease them. They are the reason you do not go out alone after dark in the Lowlands.

The Legend

In the fairy belief of Lowland Scotland the Unseelie Court was the dark and malevolent host of the fae, the dread counterpart to the kindlier Seelie Court. 'Unseelie' meant unblessed, unholy: these spirits needed no insult or provocation to do harm, but sought out mortals to torment, and were most feared abroad in the long nights of winter.

The Otherworld at the Heart of the Tale

Foremost among them was the Sluagh, the host of the restless and unforgiven dead, said to sweep through the night sky like a flock of dark birds, snatching up the souls of the dying and carrying off any traveller caught alone after dark. Witches were believed to ride with the Unseelie host, and to be marked out by them was to be beaten, led astray, or borne away.

The Sluagh were also blamed for elf-shot, the sudden unexplained sickness of people or cattle, struck down as if by an invisible arrow loosed from the host as it passed overhead. So real was the danger that into the twentieth century some Highland households still kept west-facing windows and doors shut at a deathbed, for the Sluagh were said to fly in from the west to snatch a soul before it could be shriven.

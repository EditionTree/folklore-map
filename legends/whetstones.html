Sacred Sites Welshpool, Powys, Wales

Whetstones

A destroyed stone circle beneath Corndon Hill on the Welsh Marches — one of three sacred rings guarding the slopes where, legend says, a witch was turned to stone for milking a fairy cow dry.

A visual interpretation of the Whetstones
The Legend

The Whetstones once stood at the foot of Corndon Hill’s northern slope, in the parish of Church Stoke, Montgomeryshire, close to the village of White Grit and the hamlet of Priestweston. Together with nearby Mitchell’s Fold and the Hoarstones, they formed a remarkable concentration of Bronze Age stone circles in the Welsh borderlands — three rings within walking distance, suggesting this stretch of high moorland held deep ritual significance.

The destroyed circle was one of the sacred rings guarding Corndon Hill's slopes.

The Lost Ring Under Corndon

The Welsh Royal Commission’s 1911 report recorded a circle of eight or nine stones, but by then most were already gone. One eyewitness recalled four stones arranged as though forming parts of a circle, with an appendage curving ‘like a hook.’ The larger stones had been incorporated into a boundary wall in the nineteenth century, and in the 1860s most of the remaining stones were blown up. When the last stone was uprooted in 1870, charcoal and bones were found in its hole — a final glimpse of the Bronze Age burials the circle had guarded for millennia.

The Whetstones share their hillside with the legend of Mitchell’s Fold, one of Shropshire’s most celebrated folk tales. A magical white cow appeared during a famine, giving enough milk for all provided each person took only one pailful. A witch named Mitchell brought a sieve and milked the cow dry, whereupon it fled to Warwickshire. The villagers turned the witch to stone and set a circle of standing stones around her to prevent her escape.

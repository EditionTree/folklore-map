Wild Edric

Wild Edric was an Anglo-Saxon thane of Shropshire who became the subject of an early English fairy-bride legend. He broke a condition imposed by his otherworldly wife Godda, causing her to vanish. Later tradition says his spirit rides with a ghostly cavalcade over the Stiperstones whenever England faces danger.

A visual interpretation of Wild Edric and his fairy bride Godda
The Legend

Wild Edric — Eadric the Wild — was a real Anglo-Saxon thane of the Welsh Marches who led resistance against the Normans after 1066, and around his name the Shropshire hills wove an early fairy-bride legend. Riding home through the forest, he is said to have come upon a company of beautiful women dancing in a great house and seized one of them, Godda, for his wife. She consented on the condition that he never reproach her with her sisters or her fairy origin; for years they were happy, until in anger he taunted her and she vanished, and grief soon brought about his death.

The Fairy Bride Condition

In another and older strand, Edric does not die but is bound beneath the earth, condemned with his men and his lady to ride forever through the lead mines of the Stiperstones. There they sleep until England is in danger, when Wild Edric's host is said to ride out across the hills — a sighting reported, the tradition claims, before the Crimean War and again before the World Wars, marking him as one of Britain's many sleeping deliverers.

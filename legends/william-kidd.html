Pirates Dundee, Scotland

William Kidd

The Dundee-born privateer Captain Kidd was sent to hunt pirates and returned accused of piracy himself. His execution and rumours of buried treasure transformed a disputed career into enduring legend.

A visual interpretation of Captain Kidd before piracy and privateering became inseparable in legend
The Legend

Captain William Kidd, the most famous of all the pirates of legend, was born around 1645, probably at Dundee in Scotland, and his story is the great cautionary tale of how a respectable man became, willingly or not, the very symbol of piracy. For years Kidd was a successful and well-connected sea-captain and privateer based in New York, married to a wealthy widow and trusted enough to be commissioned in 1696 — backed by powerful English lords — to hunt down pirates and seize their plunder in the Indian Ocean, under a royal privateering commission.

He was sent to hunt pirates and returned accused of piracy himself.

The Privateer Accused of Piracy

The voyage went disastrously wrong. His crew grew mutinous, disease and ill-fortune dogged the ship, and Kidd — under pressure and perhaps to save his own command — crossed the blurred line between privateer and pirate, most fatally when he captured the Quedagh Merchant, an Armenian-owned ship sailing under French passes. When he learned he had been declared a pirate, Kidd sailed back toward New York hoping to clear his name and trusting in his influential backers; instead, with the political winds turned against them, they abandoned him to his fate. He was arrested, sent to London, and tried.

Kidd was hanged at Execution Dock at Wapping in 1701 — the rope broke at the first attempt and he had to be strung up a second time — and his tarred body was hung in a gibbet over the Thames at Tilbury for years as a warning. It was his reputed buried treasure that made him immortal: the persistent belief that Kidd had hidden a fortune somewhere before his capture launched centuries of treasure-hunts and inspired countless tales of pirate gold and treasure maps, from Edgar Allan Poe to Robert Louis Stevenson, fixing Captain Kidd forever at the heart of pirate legend.

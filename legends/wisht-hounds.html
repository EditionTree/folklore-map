Wisht Hounds

The spectral pack that hunts across Dartmoor on wild nights, led by Dewer the huntsman or the Devil himself. Their baying chills the blood and to be caught in their path is death. They are kennelled beneath Wistman's Wood, where the ancient oaks grow twisted and low.

The Legend

The Wisht Hounds, 'wisht' being a Devon word for eerie or uncanny, are the spectral pack of Dartmoor, a hunt of black, fire-eyed, headless or red-mouthed hounds that course across the high moor on wild and stormy nights. They run down the souls of the unbaptised, the unshriven and the damned, and to hear their baying carried on the wind was an omen of death.

The Hunt Across the Moor

They are led by the dread huntsman Dewer, identified with the Devil himself, and their lair is said to be Wistman's Wood, a stunted, lichen-hung oak grove in the West Dart valley that is one of the strangest places on the moor. From there the hunt issues out, and the Dewerstone crag is where the quarry is driven to its doom.

The hunt's most famous afterlife is literary: Arthur Conan Doyle visited Wistman's Wood, and combined the legend with the story of Richard Cabell, a Buckfastleigh squire said to have been torn apart by hellhounds when he died in 1677, to write The Hound of the Baskervilles. Conan Doyle later credited a friend's throwaway remark about 'a legend about a dog on the moor' as the novel's real spark, a few words that carried the Wisht Hounds from Dartmoor folklore into world literature.

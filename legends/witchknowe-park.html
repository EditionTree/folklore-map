Witchknowe Park

Named for the ‘witch hill’ where dozens of accused witches were executed during the 17th-century persecutions that made Inverkeithing one of Scotland’s most zealous witch-hunting towns.

A visual interpretation of Witchknowe Park
The Legend

Witchknowe Park takes its name from the Scots word ‘knowe’ — a hillock — and marks one of the darkest chapters in Inverkeithing’s history. Between 1621 and 1652, at least 51 people were executed for witchcraft in this small Fife town, a staggering toll when the much larger Kirkcaldy saw only 18 executions in the same period. The killings reached their peak during the Great Scottish Witch Hunt of 1649–50, a nationwide frenzy that may have seen more than 300 executions across Scotland in a single year.

The park's name remembers the hill where accused witches were executed in the 17th century.

The Witch Hill

The driving force behind Inverkeithing’s persecutions was the Reverend Walter Bruce, appointed minister of St Peter’s in 1641. Bruce was a zealous witch-finder whose fervour shocked even the Kirk authorities — he was suspended at one point for preaching at a witch’s execution. Under his direction, women confessed to renouncing their baptism, consorting with the Devil, and attending nocturnal sabbaths. Among his accused was Margaret Henderson, Lady Pittadro, one of the few high-status women targeted in the Scottish witch hunts.

The executions were carried out at Witch Knowe, on land now shared between Witchknowe Park and the adjacent Hope Street Cemetery. The persecutions were fuelled by a toxic combination of cholera outbreaks, famine, and Cromwell’s looming invasion — a perfect storm of fear and superstition that Bruce exploited with lethal effect. Today the park’s name is the sole public reminder of those who died there.

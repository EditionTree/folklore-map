Y Ddraig Goch

The Red Dragon of Wales, buried beneath Dinas Emrys, locked in eternal battle with a white dragon. The young Merlin prophesied that the red dragon — the true Britons — would ultimately prevail.

The Legend

Y Ddraig Goch, the Red Dragon, is the national emblem of Wales and the hero of one of the foundational legends of Welsh sovereignty. Its story is told at Dinas Emrys, a rocky, wooded hill-fort in Snowdonia. There the tyrant-king Vortigern, fleeing the Saxons, tried again and again to build a stronghold, only for the walls to collapse mysteriously each night. His advisers told him the foundations must be sprinkled with the blood of a fatherless boy — and the boy they found was the young Merlin (Myrddin Emrys), who exposed their counsel as false.

The young Merlin prophesied that the red dragon — the true Britons — would ultimately prevail.

The Battle Beneath Dinas Emrys

Merlin revealed the true cause: beneath the hill lay a hidden pool, and in the pool two dragons asleep, one red and one white. When they were freed they fell at once to fighting, and the boy prophesied the meaning of their battle — the white dragon was the invading Saxons, the red dragon the native Britons, and though the red would be driven back and hard pressed, in the end it would rouse itself and cast the white dragon out, the Britons reclaiming their land. An older version in the tale of Lludd and Llefelys explains how the dragons came to be buried there: a shrieking dragon whose scream blighted Britain each May Eve was trapped in a pit at the very centre of the island and hidden away.

The legend gave the red dragon its enduring political charge as a symbol of the Britons against the Saxon. It was carried, tradition holds, by Welsh princes, taken up by the Tudors, and at last fixed on the national flag of Wales, where Y Ddraig Goch still rears, red against green and white — a creature out of dark-age prophecy made the badge of a nation.

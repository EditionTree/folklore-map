Yallery Brown

A tiny, wizened creature with yellow-brown skin found pinned beneath a flat stone in the Lincolnshire fens. When freed, he offered to help with a young labourer's work — but warned never to be thanked. The man thanked him. From that day, everything he touched went wrong.

A visual interpretation of Yallery Brown
The Legend

Yallery Brown is one of the most unsettling fairies of Lincolnshire folklore, the subject of a famous cautionary tale. A farm-lad named Tom Tiver, crossing the fields one night, hears a thin crying and lifts a flat stone to find beneath it a tiny, ancient creature no bigger than a baby, with a wrinkled face and a tangle of yellow-brown hair and beard like a cobweb. Freed, the sprite — Yallery Brown — offers in gratitude to do all Tom's work for him, on the one condition that Tom never thank him.

The Otherworld at the Heart of the Tale

The bargain turns sour. Tom's tasks are done by unseen hands, but the other labourers' work is wrecked, and Tom is blamed; harried and miserable, he at last forgets himself and thanks the creature to be rid of it. That breaks the only protection he had: Yallery Brown declares that since Tom has thanked him he will help no more, but will hinder him for the rest of his days, and dances off singing that ill-luck will dog Tom forever. The tale is a sharp piece of folk-wisdom on the peril of meddling with the fairies and the trap hidden inside their gifts.

Yester Castle

A ruined East Lothian castle famed for the Goblin Ha', an underground vaulted chamber said to have been built by magical arts for the wizard-like Hugo de Giffard.

A visual interpretation of Yester Castle and the Goblin Ha'
The Legend

Yester Castle is a ruined medieval stronghold hidden in woodland near Gifford in East Lothian, and its fame rests on a remarkable survival concealed beneath it — an underground vaulted chamber known as the Goblin Ha' (Goblin Hall), which legend says was built not by human hands but by goblins, at the command of a wizard. The castle and its hall were the work, around 1267, of Sir Hugo de Giffard, a powerful nobleman and guardian of the young King Alexander III — and, in tradition, a notorious warlock and necromancer.

Yester's underground hall was said to have been made by magical arts for Hugo de Giffard.

The Vaulted Chamber

The legend holds that Hugo de Giffard made a pact with the Devil and summoned up an army of hobgoblins, whom he set to labour through the night to construct the great underground vault, the Goblin Ha', by their unearthly arts. There, it was said, the wizard-lord practised his magic, worked his spells, and even raised the dead to do his bidding — the eerie stone chamber, reached by a dark stair descending into the earth, serving as his hall of sorcery.

The fame of Hugo de Giffard as the 'Wizard of Yester' endured for centuries and was taken up in literature, including by Sir Walter Scott, who wove the Goblin Hall into his work. The genuinely impressive survival of the medieval vault, dim and damp beneath the ruined castle in its lonely wood, has kept the legend potent, and Yester Castle remains one of the most atmospheric and reputedly haunted sites in East Lothian — a real underground hall that folklore insists was raised by goblins for a necromancer.

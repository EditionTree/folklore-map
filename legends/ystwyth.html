Ystwyth

UHS-twith

In some later retellings of the Three Sisters of Plynlimon, Ystwyth replaces Rheidol as one of the mountain's daughters. She flows west through Ceredigion to Cardigan Bay.

The Legend

Welsh river lore counts Ystwyth among the daughters of Plynlimon, the great boggy mountain of mid-Wales from whose slopes several rivers rise. Folk tradition personifies these rivers as sisters, each setting out from the same high source to find her own way to the sea, their differing courses explained as the choices of maidens with different temperaments. The best-known sisters are Hafren, the Severn, and Gwy, the Wye, with Rheidol as a third; in some later tellings Ystwyth takes the place of one of the mountain's daughters.

The Divine Presence

In the story the sisters debate which route to take to the sea. Hafren chooses the long, grand wandering course through the rich towns of England, Gwy a gentler way, while the swifter sisters strike out directly for the nearby coast. Ystwyth, true to her own nature, runs west through the hills of Ceredigion to reach Cardigan Bay, joining the Rheidol near the sea at the town that takes its name from her, Aberystwyth, 'the mouth of the Ystwyth'.

The legend belongs to a charming and ancient strand of Welsh folklore in which the land's rivers are living, wilful beings, their journeys from the shared mountain source to their separate sea mouths told as a family tale. Ystwyth, the swift westward sister, gives her name to one of Ceredigion's chief rivers and to its principal town, keeping the old personification of the waters alive in the everyday map of Wales.
